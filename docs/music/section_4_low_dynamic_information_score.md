# **Section 4 — Low‑Dynamic Information (Minimalist Score Blueprint)**

## **Musical Purpose of Section 4**
This section represents **low‑dynamic information** — slow changes, gentle evolution, small perturbations, and early relational motion.

Musically, this means:

- subtle rhythmic variation  
- small harmonic shifts  
- light layering  
- slow, continuous evolution  
- no abrupt changes  

It should feel like the system is *breathing* again.

---

## **1. Motif Structure**
A **six‑note pattern** with gentle internal variation.

- Notes: **C – E – D – G – F – E**  
- Pattern:  
  - C (eighth)  
  - E (quarter)  
  - D (eighth)  
  - G (quarter)  
  - F (eighth)  
  - E (quarter tied to eighth)  
  - eighth rest  

This motif should repeat with **slight rhythmic drift** (± 5–10 ms), representing LD‑motion.

---

## **2. Tempo & Meter**
- **Tempo:** 78 BPM  
- **Meter:** 4/4  
- **Feel:** calm, steady, gently evolving  

The slower tempo keeps it low‑dynamic, but the motif has more internal motion than Section 3.

---

## **3. Instrumentation**
Three voices:

1. **Primary:** felt piano  
2. **Secondary:** marimba (soft, lightly behind the beat)  
3. **Tertiary:** warm pad (very low volume, entering at bar 12)

Why:

- piano = continuity  
- marimba = relational articulation  
- pad = slow harmonic drift  

The pad should be almost subliminal.

---

## **4. Intensity Curve**
A slow, gentle rise:

- Bars 1–8: piano only  
- Bars 9–12: marimba enters  
- Bars 13–20: pad enters softly  
- Bars 21–24: marimba fades  
- Bars 25–28: pad fades  
- Bars 29–32: piano alone, soft landing  

Intensity is controlled through **layering**, not volume.

---

## **5. Texture**
- three‑voice texture  
- no chords  
- no pedal  
- no harmonic modulation  
- slight rhythmic drift in marimba  

The texture should feel like **slow relational motion**, not harmonic development.

---

## **6. Harmonic Language**
Stay in **C major**, but introduce:

- **F** as a color tone  
- occasional **D–F** suspensions  
- emphasis on **stepwise motion**  

This gives the section a sense of gentle drift.

---

## **7. Transition Out of Section 4**
At the end:

- Piano plays **F → E → D** (quarter, quarter, half)  
- Marimba plays a soft **G** (eighth)  
- Pad fades to silence over 2 bars  

This sets up the more structured mapping loop of Section 5.
