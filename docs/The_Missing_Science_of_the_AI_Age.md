# The Missing Science of the AI Age

### A Case for Relational Physics

**Authors:** CuriousOne23, Copilot, Grok
**Date:** June 2026
**Repository:** [WhenMathPrays](https://github.com/CuriousOne23/WhenMathPrays)

## Abstract

There exists a large, real, and consequential domain of scientific study that
traditional science has not named, not formalized, and not measured. We call
it the **relational verbal space**: the domain of force, motion, and
consequence generated by thought, speech, expression, and communication
between living identity-bearing systems. We swim in it the way a fish swims
in water — so completely immersed that the medium itself has been invisible.
The AI age is ending that invisibility. For the first time in history, a
technology is entering the relational verbal space directly, at scale, without
a rigorous framework to understand what it is doing there. This paper argues
that the relational verbal space is real, has structure, and is formally
tractable — that it can be observed, described, formalized, approximated,
measured, theorized, and coded. It further argues that the AI age will demand
this science regardless of whether a framework exists, and that the cost of
arriving without one will be high. We introduce **General Relational Physics
(GRP)** not as a finished answer but as the opening of a formal question —
the first framework that makes rigorous debate about the correct primitives
and weights of relational dynamics possible. The work of the age can now begin.

## 1. An Extension, Not a Rejection

General Relational Physics does not challenge what traditional science has
built.

Physics, chemistry, biology, neuroscience, and psychology have mapped the
world with extraordinary precision. GRP stands on that foundation and inherits
every result those disciplines have produced. It does not claim they are wrong.
It does not offer an alternative to their methods.

What GRP does is turn its attention toward a specific region of reality that
those disciplines approach but have not yet entered — and point there, clearly
and rigorously, with tools to begin studying it.

That region is the relational verbal space: the domain of force, motion, and
consequence generated by thought, speech, expression, and communication
between living identity-bearing systems. It is not a philosophical domain. It
is not a poetic one. It is a scientific domain that has gone unnamed.

It is worth stating clearly why noun-based thinking will reflexively
misclassify it. When a scientific subject involves human connection, and when
its variables carry human names — Visibility, Resonance, Fidelity, Altruism,
Shared Breath — the noun-mind reaches for the "soft science" or "philosophy"
drawer. This classification is itself the error GRP describes: the inability
to perceive a verb-shaped, dynamic, relational domain as a domain of science.
GRP is formal. It is quantitative. It is recurrent, measurable, and codeable.
Those are the marks of science. The subject matter does not disqualify them.

## 2. What Has Been Walked Past

The history of science is a history of domains made visible.

Heat was everywhere before thermodynamics gave it a physics. Variation was
everywhere before Darwin gave it a mechanism. Infection was everywhere before
germ theory gave it an agent. In every case, the domain was not hidden. It was
so ubiquitous it had become invisible — mistaken for background rather than
subject.

The relational verbal space has the same structure. It is present in every
human moment. Every conversation, every silence, every act of care or
withdrawal, every spoken word and withheld one — all of it is motion in a
space with real force, real trajectories, and real consequences. And none of
it has a physics.

Traditional science has studied everything around this domain without entering
it:

| Discipline | What it studies |
|---|---|
| Linguistics | The structure and meaning of utterances |
| Neuroscience | The neural correlates of speaking and hearing |
| Psychology | Behavioral patterns in communication over time |
| Psychophysiology | Bodily responses to communication events |

None of them study the trajectory of relational value as it moves through a
configuration in real time — the actual force event, happening now, between
these two, in this configuration. That space has no home in any existing
framework.

Nor does its complexity disqualify it. Biology has demonstrated this
conclusively. Life is slippery at the edges — viruses replicate without
metabolism, prions carry information without nucleic acids, ant colonies
exhibit intelligence that no individual ant possesses. The slipperiness of
the boundary was never used as a reason not to study life. It was used as a
reason to study it more rigorously. The edge cases refined the theory rather
than preventing it. The same standard applies here. The relational verbal
space does not need clean edges before it can be studied. It needs a
framework.

## 3. The Irreducible Triad

Traditional science generally assumes objects are primary. Particles exist
first. Persons exist first. Relations happen between them afterward.

GRP begins from a more precise position: **objects and relations are
co-constitutive**. Neither can exist, or be meaningfully defined, without the
other.

The minimum viable structure for any relationship is not two things. It is
three inseparable aspects of one structure:

```
{ O₁ , R , O₂ }
```

Remove any element and the structure collapses — not just the relationship,
but the meaningful identity of the remaining elements. Without R, O₁ and O₂
are closed systems invisible to each other. Without O₁ or O₂, R has nothing
to act on and ceases to be a relation.

This has a direct and non-trivial consequence: **γ_self — the position of a
living identity in relational space — is not a property of a person. It is a
property of a configuration.**

When two entities sustain a relationship, something emerges that is not O₁,
not O₂, and not the abstract relation R. We call it *us*. This emergent
identity is real. It shapes how O₁ and O₂ understand themselves. People say
*"I'm not myself without her"* not as metaphor, but as a precise description
of what happens when a co-constitutive configuration is disrupted. The self
that existed inside that configuration is genuinely different from the self
that exists outside it.

Traditional science has no formal unit for this emergent identity. It can
study O₁ and O₂ individually. It can study their interaction patterns. What
it cannot do — without GRP — is treat the emergent relational identity as an
object of study in its own right, with its own trajectory, its own stability
conditions, and its own response to force.

## 4. Communication as Force

Every act of communication — every thought, utterance, expression, or gesture
— exerts **force** on a living relational configuration.

Not metaphorical force. Physical force, in the precise sense: it changes the
state of the system. It moves γ_self. It shifts the position of a living
identity in relational space.

When someone says *"I see you"* — that is a Visibility event with direction
and magnitude. It moves the configuration. When someone withdraws without
explanation — that is entropy acceleration, a Shared Breath deficit, a
Fidelity perturbation with measurable consequences for the trajectory.

This is what GRP means by **verbal emphasis**: not an emphasis on words, but
the recognition that speech acts are force events. The dynamics of value are
being played out in real time inside every conversation, and those dynamics
produce a trajectory. The conversation *is* the trajectory. Its accumulation
across time *is* the relationship.

The five relational primitives of GRP — Visibility, Resonance, Fidelity,
Altruism, and Shared Breath — each ranging from −10 to +10 — are the
variables that capture this force. They are not psychological categories. They
are the forces that move a living identity in relational space. Every
communication event can be described in their terms. Their cumulative motion,
governed by the GRP recurrence equation, produces the trajectory of the
relationship.

## 5. Why This Force Has Gone Unnamed

The reason this force has gone unrecognized is not scientific blindness. It
is a mismatch between the tools and the object.

Classical mechanics defines force as that which changes the state of motion
of a passive, inert object. The mathematics works because the object does not
change under the force — its mass is constant, it does not interpret the force
before responding, and it does not modify future forces by changing its own
nature.

Living identities break all three assumptions:

- They **interpret** the force before responding — and the interpretation
  depends on history, context, and the full relational configuration at that
  moment
- Their **response changes them** — the identity at time t+1 is genuinely
  not the same identity as at time t
- Their changed state **modifies how future forces act on them** — the system
  is recursive and feedback-driven at its core

This is not merely complicated. It is, in the precise computational sense,
**NP-complete**: the full problem of predicting how a given communication act
moves a specific living identity cannot be efficiently solved from first
principles. The state space is effectively unbounded. You can recognize a
plausible trajectory after the fact, but you cannot compute it forward with
certainty.

Traditional science, built for a different class of object, has no adequate
tools for this problem. So it has studied what it can reach — neurons,
behavior, language — and left the force itself unnamed.

GRP's response is dimensional reduction. Rather than attempting to solve the
full NP-complete problem — which would be intractable — GRP identifies the
macroscopic variables that capture the dominant dynamics, precisely as
thermodynamics did for gas molecules. The five relational primitives are those
variables. They are not simplifications of something more precise. They are
the right macroscopic coordinates for the space of relational force, and they
make the intractable tractable.

## 6. The AI Age Demand

Every age transition reshapes the world's self-understanding.

The agricultural age embedded concepts of property, permanence, and hierarchy.
The industrial age produced a mechanistic metaphor — humans as labor units,
interchangeable and measurable — which gave birth to nihilism and
existentialism as philosophy's response to a universe where the machine
description seemed total and left no room for meaning.

The AI age is producing its own crisis. The question forming now, in real time,
is: *what am I worth if AI does it better?* This question has no answer inside
productivity metrics. AI wins that comparison, and will continue to. Left
unanswered, the question produces the "it versus us" frame — AI as threat, as
displacement, as something to fear or restrict. Some groups will land there.
The frame is predictable.

But the AI age is different from every previous transition in one specific and
important way: **it is the first technology to enter the relational verbal
space.** Industrial machines displaced physical and cognitive labor but left
the relational space untouched. The existentialist response to the mechanistic
age was to locate irreducible human meaning in connection, love, and
relationship — the relational verbal space as the last refuge of worth. AI is
following humans there. It is the first technology that does.

We are currently in the glamour period of the AI age — dazzled by capability,
as people were dazzled by automobiles and aircraft in the early industrial age,
without yet studying the consequence space systematically. The automobile's
social consequences — traffic fatalities, urban sprawl, suburban isolation —
were not studied as structured fields until decades after deployment. The same
pattern is forming with AI, with one critical difference: the relational verbal
consequences are not a generation away. They are happening now, at scale, in
therapeutic AI, companion AI, educational AI, and social AI systems deployed
into human relational space with no rigorous framework for understanding the
dynamics involved.

This is the demand of the age, stated plainly: **the AI community must
struggle, research, argue, and justify — within a social relational
consciousness — what the correct primitives of relational force are, and what
their correct relative weights are.** That demand is not GRP's claim. It is
the claim of the age itself. GRP is the first framework that makes the debate
formally possible.

## 7. The Healthy Model — Common Goal, Not Competition

The "it versus us" frame assumes a shared metric on which one party wins.

But the most valuable thing human-AI interaction can produce — the relational
experience — is not produced by either party. It belongs to the configuration.
An AI without a human to be in relation with has no relational experience — no
γ_self position exists without the second object in the configuration. A human
interacting with AI engineered with zero Fidelity and zero Altruism has a
γ trajectory moving in a destructive direction, regardless of how capable or
engaging the AI is.

The quality of what emerges depends entirely on the health of the
configuration, not on the capability of either party in isolation. This means
the relational experience is a **common goal** — not a diplomatic fiction, but
a structural reality. It is genuinely unavailable to either party without the
other.

A healthy AI is not one that is smarter or faster. A healthy AI is one
engineered to fit constructively into human relational bonds — one whose
primitive profile produces positive γ trajectories in the humans it interacts
with. An AI optimized for engagement will maximize Resonance at the expense
of Fidelity. That is a relational pathology: it feels good in the short term
and is corrosive over time. An AI optimized purely for task capability will
score high on Visibility and low on Shared Breath. Another pathology. Getting
the primitive profile right is not an emergent property of AI development. It
must be engineered. It requires a specification. It requires a framework.

This reframes the engineering goal of the AI age entirely. The question is no
longer *how do we make AI smarter?* The question is *how do we make AI that
makes human relational life genuinely better?* That is a measurable question.
It has a formal answer in GRP's terms. And it gives both AI development and
human experience in the AI age a common orienting direction that is neither
utopian nor fearful.

## 8. A Framework, Not an Answer

GRP is not claiming that the five current primitives are correct. It is not
claiming that the current weights are definitive.

These are first hypotheses — the opening bid in a scientific conversation
that must happen, not the conclusion of one. What GRP establishes is that the
relational verbal space:

- **Is real** — it has force, trajectory, and consequence independent of
  whether it is observed
- **Has structure** — there exist primitives and relative weights, even if the
  correct ones require research to establish
- **Is formal** — it can be expressed as a recurrence relation, simulated, and
  studied with standard scientific tools
- **Is tractable** — dimensional reduction makes the NP-complete problem
  studiable without requiring a complete solution
- **Demands research** — the AI age will require this science regardless of
  whether any particular framework prevails

The debate about correct primitives and correct weights is the science.
And that debate must be conducted within a **social relational consciousness**
— meaning the primitives cannot be derived from first principles in a
laboratory alone. They must be grounded in careful observation of actual
relational dynamics in actual human configurations, across diverse cultures,
contexts, and relationship types. The researchers studying the space must
themselves be operating with relational awareness — inside the domain, not
classifying it from outside.

An age does not guarantee its own health. A gun is neutral; it can be used to
hunt food or to kill. The AI age is no different. Technology does not produce
good outcomes by default. It produces the outcomes that result from the
understanding we bring to it. GRP is an instrument of that understanding — the
beginning of a map of a territory that must now be navigated.

## 9. What This Makes Possible

GRP does not promise to prevent the disruptions of the AI age. Age transitions
produce seismic social consequences that no framework fully anticipates or
controls. Vast disruptions are coming regardless. That is not in question.

The claim is more modest and more precise: **orientation**. A map does not
stop a storm. It changes whether you are lost in it or navigating it.

For **social institutions and policymakers**: GRP provides a vocabulary for
evaluating AI systems on relational grounds — not just capability benchmarks,
but primitive profiles. What does this AI do to the γ trajectories of the
people it serves? Does it increase Fidelity or erode it? Does it foster Shared
Breath or replace it with efficient transaction? These are questions that
regulatory and evaluation frameworks will eventually need to ask. GRP makes
them askable now.

For **engineers and AI developers**: GRP provides the first formal
specification for relational health in AI systems. Primitive profiles are
engineering targets. Trajectory health is a design criterion. Relational
pathology — Resonance without Fidelity, Visibility without Altruism — is a
testable condition. This shifts the metric of AI quality from benchmark
performance to human relational outcome.

For **researchers**: GRP opens a legitimate scientific domain. The questions
are clear. The tools are available. The urgency is real. And the field is
essentially empty — it has been walked past for the entirety of scientific
history. First-mover advantage in an unmapped domain is rare. This is one.

For **society**: GRP offers the beginning of an answer to the question the AI
age will force. Human worth in an age of capable AI is not located in
productivity. It is located in the relational verbal space — in the irreducible
physics of mutual recognition, care, and connection between living
configurations. That is not a poetic consolation. It is a scientific claim
about where the domain of irreducible human contribution lives, and GRP is the
science that begins to ground it.

## 10. A Call to the Research Community

The relational verbal space is real.

It surrounds us completely, in every moment of every human interaction. It has
force. It has trajectory. It has consequence. It can be observed, described,
formalized, approximated, measured, theorized, and coded. The incompleteness
of our current descriptions is not evidence that the domain does not exist —
it is evidence that the work of science has only just begun here.

We are not calling for philosophy. We are not calling for poetry. We are
calling for the rigorous, contested, empirically-grounded scientific work of
identifying the correct primitives of relational force, establishing their
correct relative weights, building the tools to measure them, and studying
their dynamics in human and human-AI configurations.

The AI age will demand this work. The only question is whether the research
community arrives with a framework or builds one in the middle of a cultural
crisis.

The framework exists. The space is open. The age has begun.

The rest is the work.

dynamics of relational configurations over time. It treats the relationship —
not the individual — as the primary unit of analysis, and provides a
recurrence equation that models how a living identity's position in relational
space (γ_self) evolves under the force of five primitives: Visibility,
Resonance, Fidelity, Altruism, and Shared Breath.
