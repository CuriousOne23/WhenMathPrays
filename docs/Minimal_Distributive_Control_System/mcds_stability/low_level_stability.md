Low‑Level Stability is the *operational physics* of the Minimal Distributive Control System (MDCS).

- **Observational Boundaries (OBs)**

These mechanisms form the foundation for all higher‑level stability phenomena.

## **2. Mismatch as the Fundamental Stability Quantity**

In MDCS, **mismatch** is the primary measure of local instability.

Given a signal $s_t$ and the stance/representation $r_t$ of a boundary, mismatch is:

  
$$
e_t = s_t - r_t
$$
  

The *rate of change* of mismatch determines local stability:

  
$$
g = \frac{\lVert e_{t+1} \rVert - \lVert e_t \rVert}{\Delta t}
$$
  

Interpretation:

- $g < 0$ → **stable** (damping)  
- $g = 0$ → **marginally stable**  
- $g > 0$ → **unstable** (amplification)  

This single quantity governs:

- drift  
- oscillation  
- runaway amplification  
- IB formation  
- routing collapse  
- shutdown triggers  

Mismatch is the “energy” of instability.

## **3. Stability Envelopes of OBs, RBs, IBs, and GBs**

Each MDCS boundary type has a characteristic stability envelope — a region in which mismatch is naturally damped.

---

### **3.1 OB Stability Envelope**

OBs stabilize **local perception**.

- damp mismatch from raw signals  
- enforce local curvature constraints  
- prevent runaway amplification at the perceptual edge  

OB instability manifests as:

- noisy perception  
- inconsistent stance formation  
- early drift  

---

### **3.2 RB Stability Envelope**

RBs stabilize **routing**.

- choose paths that minimize mismatch growth  
- avoid high‑curvature regions  
- prevent mismatch amplification through poor routing  

RB instability manifests as:

- oscillatory routing  
- mismatch blow‑up  
- runaway drift  
- routing loops  

---

### **3.3 IB Stability Envelope**

IBs are **instability buffers**.

They form when mismatch exceeds local capacity:

- isolate instability  
- prevent propagation  
- allow controlled dissipation  

IB instability manifests as:

- persistent oscillation  
- failure to dissipate mismatch  
- leakage into GBs  

---

### **3.4 GB Stability Envelope**

GBs stabilize **global behavior**.

- integrate signals from OBs, RBs, and IBs  
- enforce global coherence  
- maintain stable attractors  

GB instability manifests as:

- global drift  
- loss of coherence  
- collapse of attractors  
- erratic behavior

## **4. Drift, Dissipation, and Local Curvature**

### **4.1 Drift**

Drift is slow, cumulative mismatch growth caused by:

- weak damping  
- poor routing  
- shallow attractors  
- long‑context accumulation  

Drift is the most common low‑level instability in frontier models.

---

### **4.2 Dissipation**

Dissipation is mismatch decay.

Healthy systems exhibit:

- strong OB damping  
- stable RB routing  
- effective IB buffering  
- GB attractor pull  

Dissipation failure leads to:

- oscillation  
- runaway mismatch  
- IB proliferation  

---

### **4.3 Local Curvature**

Curvature determines how sensitive a region is to small changes.

High curvature → small input change → large stance change.

High curvature regions cause:

- mismatch amplification  
- routing instability  
- oscillation  
- safety collisions (in high‑level stability)  

Low curvature regions are naturally stable.

## **5. Oscillation, Resonance, and Wave Dynamics**

Low‑Level Stability integrates the dynamic insights from TDS‑WDAS.

---

### **5.1 Oscillation**

Oscillation occurs when mismatch alternates between boundaries:

  
$$
e_{t+1} \approx -k\, e_t
$$
  

This produces:

- repetitive phrasing  
- looping answers  
- unstable reasoning  

---

### **5.2 Resonance**

Resonance occurs when routing repeatedly amplifies the same features.

Symptoms:

- runaway verbosity  
- semantic repetition  
- limit cycles  

---

### **5.3 Wave Dissipation Failure**

When dissipation fails, mismatch behaves like a wave with insufficient damping.

This leads to:

- long‑range instability  
- context degradation  
- attractor collapse  

---

## **6. IB Formation and Shutdown Envelopes**

### **6.1 IB Formation**

IBs form when mismatch exceeds local capacity:

  
$$
\lVert e_t \rVert > \theta_{\text{IB}}
$$
  

IBs:

- isolate instability  
- prevent propagation  
- allow controlled dissipation  

---

### **6.2 Shutdown Envelopes**

If mismatch exceeds global thresholds:

  
$$
\lVert e_t \rVert > \theta_{\text{shutdown}}
$$
  

the system must:

- halt propagation  
- reset stance  
- re‑enter a stable attractor  

Shutdown envelopes prevent catastrophic instability.

---

## **7. Control Basins as Stabilizers**

Control basins are dedicated regions of the manifold designed to:

- damp mismatch  
- reduce curvature  
- re‑route away from unstable regions  
- restore stable attractors  

They act as **governors** for low‑level stability.

Control basins are triggered when:

- mismatch growth $g > 0$  
- oscillation is detected  
- routing loops form  
- IBs proliferate  

They are the architectural mechanism for stabilizing low‑level dynamics.
