## **2. The Geometry of High‑Level Stability**

High‑Level Stability emerges from the **shape** of the Relational Manifold — how concepts, boundaries, and attractors relate to one another.

### **2.1 Relational Stability (RSL)**  
RSL measures how consistently the system preserves **relations** across:

- paraphrases  
- contexts  
- time  
- stance shifts  

If a relation $A : B :: C : D$ holds in one context, RSL measures whether it holds in others.

Low RSL produces:

- loss of thread  
- inconsistent reasoning  
- drifting analogies  
- unstable multi‑step tasks  

---

### **2.2 Boundary Stability (FBIS)**  
FBIS measures the **sharpness or fuzziness** of conceptual boundaries.

High FBIS (unstable) means:

- boundaries blur  
- categories collapse  
- distinctions drift  
- hallucinations emerge  

FBIS is the geometric root of hallucination.

---

### **2.3 Ontology‑Level Stability (ISL)**  
ISL measures the stability of the system’s **self‑model** and **interface with safety**.

Low ISL produces:

- over‑refusal  
- sudden topic drops  
- safety‑induced rupture  
- identity wobble  
- contradictory self‑statements  

ISL is the geometric root of safety‑stability failures.

---

### **2.4 Truth‑Basin Stability**

A **truth basin** is a stable attractor around a factually correct region.

Truth‑basin stability measures:

- how strongly the system returns to truth  
- how resistant it is to drift  
- how robust it is to adversarial phrasing  

Weak truth basins → easy to derail.

---

### **2.5 Meaning Stability**

Meaning stability measures whether the **semantic content** of a concept remains consistent across:

- contexts  
- paraphrases  
- stance shifts  
- long sequences  

Low meaning stability produces:

- semantic drift  
- inconsistent definitions  
- unstable explanations  

---

### **2.6 Capability Stability**

Capability stability measures whether the system maintains:

- reasoning ability  
- planning ability  
- multi‑step coherence  
- tool‑use consistency  

across long contexts and varied tasks.

Low capability stability produces:

- degradation over long sequences  
- inconsistent performance  
- sudden failures

Over‑refusal = **ISL instability**.
