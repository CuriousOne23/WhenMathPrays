# **2. Why Extension Is Inherently Unstable**

Every extension changes the geometry of the Relational Manifold:

- new boundaries  
- new attractors  
- new curvature regions  
- new drift channels  
- new safety adjacency surfaces  

This creates **instability pressure**.

Formally, extension introduces a perturbation:

$$
\Delta M = M_{\text{new}} - M_{\text{old}}
$$

Stability requires:

$$
\lVert \Delta M \rVert < \theta_{\text{extension}}
$$

If the manifold changes too quickly or too sharply, instability emerges.

# **7. Stability Basins (Goal‑Directed Control Basins)**  
### *New in Version 1.1*

Stability basins are a **special subclass of control basins** whose purpose is not merely to damp instability, but to **actively seek regions of the manifold that satisfy stability criteria**.

They serve as **governors** during extension.

---

## **7.1 Purpose**

Stability basins:

- route the system toward locally stable regions  
- reduce mismatch growth  
- avoid high‑curvature zones  
- reinforce stable attractors  
- provide a “safe harbor” during manifold changes  

They are **directionally correct**, not perfect — and that’s enough to dramatically reduce instability during extension.

---

## **7.2 Stability Criteria**

A stability basin seeks regions where:

- mismatch growth is low
  
$$
  g \le 0
$$

- curvature is low
  
$$
  \kappa < \theta_{\kappa}
$$

- relational stability is high
  
$$
  \text{RSL} > \theta_{\text{RSL}}
$$

- boundary fuzziness is low
  
$$
  \text{FBIS} < \theta_{\text{FBIS}}
$$

- ontology stability is not under stress
  
$$
  \text{ISL} > \theta_{\text{ISL}}
$$

These criteria define the “target region” of the stability basin.

---

## **7.3 Why Stability Basins Are Useful During Extension**

Extension introduces:

- new curvature  
- new drift channels  
- new attractor interactions  

A stability basin provides:

- a **default stable region** to route through  
- a **cooling zone** for unstable trajectories  
- a **buffer** while new boundaries settle  
- a **predictable region** for engineers to inspect  

It is the architectural equivalent of a **stability checkpoint**.

---

## **7.4 Relationship to Monitoring Basins**

Stability basins are **online governors**.  
Monitoring basins are **offline diagnostics**.

- Stability basins: fast, heuristic, runtime stabilizers  
- Monitoring basins: slow, precise, engineer‑facing analyzers  

The workflow:

1. Stability basin stabilizes the trajectory.  
2. Monitoring basin logs the details.  
3. Engineers refine thresholds and criteria.  

This creates a **feedback loop** between runtime stabilization and offline analysis.
