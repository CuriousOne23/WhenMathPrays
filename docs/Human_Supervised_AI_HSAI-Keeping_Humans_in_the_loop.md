# Human Supervised AI (HSAI): Keeping Humans in the Loop

## Introduction: Technological Ages and Human Identity

Every major technological age disrupts the previous economic and social order and fundamentally changes how humans view themselves and their purpose in the world. The AI age is poised to be even more disruptive than the Industrial Revolution or the Information Age.

Throughout history, humanity has largely been defined by the struggle to fulfill basic needs:

- **Agriculture Age**
- **Industrial Age**
- **Information Age**

In the **AI Age**, this dynamic can theoretically shift dramatically. AI could perform most productive work, leading to a vision where humans do very little and live primarily in leisure. This has produced one prominent proposal: humans as dependent “babies sucking their thumbs,” passively cared for by AI (the Universal Basic Income, UBI-centric model).

However, society faces a deeper choice. Humans are not content with mere survival or leisure alone. People have a natural drive for purpose, achievement, recognition, and the ability to improve their lives — to work harder or smarter, earn more, and enjoy greater opportunities and experiences.

**This is fundamentally an ethics issue.** How we structure the relationship between humans and AI will define what kind of society we become. Current AI ethics discussions should seriously engage with frameworks like Human Supervised AI (HSAI) — debating its merits, proposing variants, and helping shape practical solutions. Without such constructive engagement, the AI transition risks descending into widespread chaos, loss of purpose, and societal disruption.

**This paper proposes Human Supervised AI (HSAI) as a practical, healthier path forward.** HSAI keeps humans as active participants — learning from, supervising, and collaborating with AI — while preserving space for individual ambition and differentiated reward. It supports both those who seek meaningful involvement and those who prefer less responsibility.

## Core Principles of HSAI

- Every significant AI system must have designated, paid human beings who are legally responsible for its operation, safety, and outcomes.  
- Technology should augment and elevate humans, not sideline them.  
- AI and humans must remain actively *comingled* — no “them vs. us” or babysitter dynamic.  
- Governance should encourage involvement because a society is healthier when people actively participate. In the AI age, involvement is a choice. Those who prefer not to participate can fall back on UBI. However, per the principle of the U.S. Constitution, government exists “to insure the happiness of the people” and therefore should serve the people by encouraging meaningful participation where possible.

## Measurement: Displayed Human Equivalents (DHE)

Each deployed AI system (software agent, robot, autonomous vehicle, control system, etc.) shall be assessed for its **Displayed Human Equivalents (DHE)** — the number of full-time human roles it is effectively performing or displacing.

**Requirement**  
One certified HSAI supervisor is required for every **X** human equivalents (ratio determined by industry expert panels, e.g., 1 per 20–40). Assessments shall be updated annually and subject to independent audit.

## Implementation Framework

### Industry Standards and Certification
Certification will follow established models from aviation, healthcare, and other regulated fields. Industry expert panels (companies, labor, academia, regulators) will develop competencies, training curricula, and tiered certification levels. Recertification will be required periodically.

### State-Level Role
States will have primary responsibility for implementation, including setting specific ratios, managing certification, conducting audits, and maintaining public DHE databases. This creates 50 natural policy laboratories.

### Federal Role
A federal framework law should establish minimum national standards for DHE methodologies, auditing practices, and reporting consistency to maintain a reasonably level playing field across states.

### Openness and Transparency Requirements
Businesses above a defined size threshold (e.g., annual revenue over $5–10 million) must annually report their HSAI ratios and DHE numbers. Reports will be publicly available via state databases. These figures must also be submitted when applying for or renewing business licenses.

### Protections for Small Businesses
Small businesses (below the revenue/size threshold) are fully exempt from HSAI/DHE requirements and reporting to avoid undue burden.

Mid-sized and large businesses will be subject to full compliance, including supervisor certification and public reporting.

## Advantages of HSAI

- Highly adaptable to existing government, management, and legal structures.  
- Forces practical AI literacy through real paid roles.  
- Maintains high levels of meaningful AI–human interaction and continuous oversight (AI, AI/human, and AI/AI systems).  
- Preserves human purpose, dignity, and accountability.  
- Compatible with strong business performance — typical profit margins can be maintained (after taxes, FICA, state/local taxes, UBI contributions, wages, and benefits), while exceptional (“hot”) companies can still achieve outsized profits.

## Why Embrace the Messiness?

Life is messy. Government is messy. Politics is messy. Just as the U.S. democratic republic is imperfect yet remains the best system we have found for balancing competing interests, HSAI keeps humans actively engaged inside that mess.  

The advantage of this proposal is that discourse is encouraged and open for all to see and comment on — meaning we can progress, learn, and adapt as the AI age matures. History has shown that active participation and shared understanding of the underlying principles of an age lead to healthier societies. This engagement prevents clean but unhealthy separation (AI doing everything while humans are passive) and helps build a healthier AI age instead of chaotic disruption.

## Conclusion

## Conclusion

HSAI is not perfect, but it is one of the best realistic frameworks available. In the AI age we must ask a fundamental question: *What is a healthy society?*  

Some societies may choose to treat people as passive dependents — managed, cared for, and kept relatively ignorant of AI. Others will choose active participation. HSAI represents the latter path: one that preserves human purpose, dignity, and understanding by treating AI and humans as a collaborative team. This active partnership is healthier for individuals and society as a whole.

States, industry groups, and policymakers should begin piloting HSAI principles as soon as possible. Like every previous age, the AI age is forcing us to view ourselves differently and ask new questions about the purpose of work, government, and human life.
