# Human Supervised AI (HSAI): Keeping Humans in the Loop

## Introduction: Technological Ages and Human Identity

Every major technological age disrupts the previous economic and social order and fundamentally changes how humans view themselves and their purpose in the world. The AI age is poised to be even more disruptive than the Industrial Revolution or the Information Age.

In every transition, society faces a choice: Will humans become passive recipients of technology, or active participants who shape and coexist with it?  

Two competing visions have emerged for the AI age:  
- Humans as dependent “babies sucking their thumbs,” passively cared for by AI (the UBI-centric model).  
- Humans as capable participants who remain actively involved, learning from, supervising, and collaborating with AI.

Without constructive frameworks, the AI transition risks becoming highly chaotic, leading to widespread societal dishevel, loss of purpose, and backlash.  

**This paper proposes Human Supervised AI (HSAI) as a practical, healthier path forward.**

## Core Principles of HSAI

- Every significant AI system must have designated, paid human beings who are legally responsible for its operation, safety, and outcomes.  
- Technology should augment and elevate humans, not sideline them.  
- AI and humans must remain actively *comingled* — no “them vs. us” or babysitter dynamic.  
- Messiness (debate, audits, political negotiation) is a feature, not a bug — it forces ongoing human engagement.

## Measurement: Displayed Human Equivalents (DHE)

Each deployed AI system (software agent, robot, autonomous vehicle, control system, etc.) shall be assessed for its **Displayed Human Equivalents (DHE)** — the number of full-time human roles it is effectively performing or displacing.

**Requirement**  
One certified HSAI supervisor is required for every **X** human equivalents (ratio determined by industry expert panels, e.g., 1 per 20–40). Assessments shall be updated annually and subject to independent audit.

## Implementation Framework

### Industry Standards and Certification
Certification will follow established models from aviation, healthcare, and other regulated fields. Industry expert panels (companies, labor, academia, regulators) will develop competencies, training curricula, and tiered certification levels. Recertification will be required periodically.

### State-Level Role
States will have primary responsibility for implementation, including setting specific ratios, managing certification, conducting audits, and maintaining public DHE databases. This creates 50 natural policy laboratories.

### Federal Role
A federal framework law should establish minimum national standards for DHE methodologies, auditing practices, and reporting consistency to maintain a reasonably level playing field across states.

### Openness and Transparency Requirements
Businesses above a defined size threshold must annually report their HSAI ratios and DHE numbers. Reports will be publicly available via state databases. These figures must also be submitted when applying for or renewing business licenses.

### Protections for Small Businesses
Small businesses (e.g., below $5–10 million annual revenue or equivalent employee threshold) are fully exempt from HSAI/DHE requirements and reporting to avoid undue burden.

Mid-sized and large businesses will be subject to full compliance, including supervisor certification and public reporting.

## Advantages of HSAI

- Highly adaptable to existing government, management, and legal structures.  
- Forces practical AI literacy through real paid roles.  
- Maintains high levels of meaningful AI–human interaction and continuous oversight (AI, AI/human, and AI/AI systems).  
- Preserves human purpose, dignity, and accountability.  
- Compatible with strong business performance — typical profit margins can be maintained, while exceptional (“hot”) companies can still achieve outsized profits.

## Why Embrace the Messiness?

Life is messy. Government is messy. Politics is messy. Just as the U.S. democratic republic is imperfect yet remains the best system we have found for balancing competing interests, HSAI keeps humans actively engaged inside that mess.  

This engagement prevents clean but unhealthy separation (AI doing everything while humans are passive) and helps build a healthier AI age instead of chaotic disruption.

## Example Healthy HSAI / DHE Targets

The following are **typical** benchmarks. Exceptional companies can still generate outstanding profits.

| Industry Type              | Typical Profit Margin (pre-HSAI) | Healthy DHE Ratio       | Estimated Margin Impact |
|----------------------------|----------------------------------|-------------------------|-------------------------|
| Manufacturing / Robotics   | 8–12%                           | 1 per 25–35            | 0.5–2 pts              |
| Software / Tech            | 20–30%+                         | 1 per 40–60            | 2–5 pts                |
| Logistics / Warehousing    | 6–10%                           | 1 per 30–40            | 1–3 pts                |
| General Enterprise         | 10–15%                          | 1 per 30               | 1–2.5 pts              |

**Note**: AI productivity gains generally far exceed the cost of supervision.

## Conclusion

HSAI is not perfect, but it is one of the best realistic frameworks available. By choosing active human participation over passive babysitting, we can shape the AI age constructively — preserving human purpose and dignity while harnessing the power of AI.

States, industry groups, and policymakers should begin piloting HSAI principles as soon as possible.
