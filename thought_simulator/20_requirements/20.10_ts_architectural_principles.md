# 20.10 TS Architectural Principles

## Purpose
Defines the architectural principles that constrain all TS requirement modules.

## Normative Requirements
1. HLR-20.010-001: The TS core SHALL be deterministic for identical input, MTP state, and seed.
2. HLR-20.010-002: Meaning construction SHALL remain explicit, structured, and auditable.
3. HLR-20.010-003: Variability SHALL be isolated to the output layer through the randomness boundary.
4. HLR-20.010-004: Program flow SHALL follow fixed phase ordering and SHALL prohibit cross-phase mutation.
5. HLR-20.010-005: Observer influence SHALL remain external and SHALL NOT mutate TS core state directly.

## Program Flow Integration
This module incorporates the architectural ordering intent previously captured in legacy program-flow requirements.

## TS Architectural Principles (Expanded)
1. HLR-20.010-006: TS SHALL be structured for modifiability, debuggability, extendability, and human clarity through explicit, modular, and transparent architecture boundaries.
2. HLR-20.010-007: TS SHALL meet explicit and measurable execution-speed and memory-efficiency constraints for macro execution workloads.
3. HLR-20.010-008: TS SHALL provide deterministic and structured monitoring through MB, including GB, OuB, and IB observability pathways.
4. HLR-20.010-009: Every TS function, primitive, and macro SHALL define and expose deterministic, auditable, and comparable TCU (or equivalent) execution-speed metrics.
5. HLR-20.010-010: TS SHALL remain platform-independent and SHALL NOT rely on architecture assumptions tied to specific hardware, OS, or runtime implementations.
6. HLR-20.010-011: TS architecture SHALL prioritize top-down thought-processing structure, functions, and primitives over implementation-specific mechanics.
7. HLR-20.010-012: TS internal state and transitions SHALL be highly visible, inspectable, and explainable for diagnostics, research, architectural analysis, and stability investigations.
8. HLR-20.010-013: TS SHALL define bounded, measurable, and comparable efficiency metrics for size, speed, and cost, and SHALL introduce new metrics where existing measures are insufficient.
9. HLR-20.010-014: TS SHALL support controlled yet flexible architectural evolution under explicit governance rules that prevent architectural drift.
10. HLR-20.010-015: TS documentation SHALL remain complete, detailed, and continuously updated, explicitly recording architectural decisions, invariants, and constraints.
11. HLR-20.010-016: TS SHALL deterministically handle noisy or ill-formed input.
  - InB SHALL map noisy surface forms into stable conceptual basins without probabilistic inference.
  - OB activation SHALL be concept-based and spelling-independent.
  - TB SHALL reconstruct intended structure under degraded syntax without nondeterministic interpretation.
  - RB SHALL stabilize meaning without hallucinating corrections or inventing content.
  - ΔH% updates SHALL remain bounded and deterministic under noisy input.
  - Meaning construction SHALL remain stable and auditable despite surface-form degradation.
12. HLR-20.010-017: Testability SHALL be a first-class architectural requirement.
  - All basins (InB, OB, TB, RB, MB) SHALL expose deterministic and reproducible interfaces suitable for automated testing.
  - Every function, primitive, and macro SHALL define test hooks, expected outputs, invariants, and failure modes.
  - ΔH%, MTP evolution, and basin activations SHALL be observable, testable, and reproducible.
  - Testability SHALL be preserved during architecture evolution; no subsystem SHALL become opaque or untestable.
  - Determinism SHALL be treated as a mandatory testability condition: identical inputs SHALL produce identical outputs.

## 1.8 Supervisory Separation Principle
13. HLR-20.010-018: The TS core SHALL focus exclusively on deterministic execution of OB, RB, TB, and MTP logic.
14. HLR-20.010-019: The TS core SHALL NOT make global supervisory decisions, except simple local binary checks (including lane completeness checks, merge validity checks, and ΔH% normalization triggers).
15. HLR-20.010-020: GB SHALL perform global supervisory decisions, including inquiry gating, branching control, stability evaluation, drift/oscillation detection, and safety-mode transitions.
16. HLR-20.010-021: All TS service requests that affect global behavior SHALL be routed through GB.
17. HLR-20.010-022: TS->GB->TS supervisory flow SHALL be explicit, deterministic, and bounded.
18. HLR-20.010-023: OuB MAY tag incomplete meaning; TS core SHALL convert this into a supervisory request; GB SHALL approve or deny.
19. HLR-20.010-024: GB MAY be implemented on a separate processor, microcontroller, or safety controller to preserve isolation, determinism, and bounded supervisory overhead.
20. HLR-20.010-025: TS core and GB SHALL communicate through a deterministic supervisory interface with explicit directionality, timing, and rate limits.
21. HLR-20.010-026: GB SHALL NOT mutate TP/MTP meaning-construction state and SHALL influence only supervisory flow-control.
