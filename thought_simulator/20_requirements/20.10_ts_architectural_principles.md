# 20.10 TS Architectural Principles

**Document ID:** 20.10  
**Version:** 0.2  
**Date:** 2026-06-04  
**Status:** Draft - For CP Review

## Purpose
Defines the architectural principles that constrain all TS requirement modules.

## Normative Requirements

**HLR-20.010-001:** The TS core SHALL be deterministic for identical input, MTP state, and seed.

**HLR-20.010-002:** Meaning construction SHALL remain explicit, structured, and auditable.

**HLR-20.010-003:** Variability SHALL be isolated to the output layer through the randomness boundary.

**HLR-20.010-004:** Program flow SHALL follow fixed phase ordering and SHALL prohibit cross-phase mutation.

**HLR-20.010-005:** Observer influence SHALL remain external and SHALL NOT mutate TS core state directly.

## Program Flow Integration
This module incorporates the architectural ordering intent previously captured in legacy program-flow requirements.

## TS Architectural Principles (Expanded)

**HLR-20.010-006:** TS SHALL maintain strict separation between deterministic core computation and non-deterministic output generation.

**HLR-20.010-007:** All meaning-bearing structures (TP, MTP, OB, etc.) SHALL use explicit, typed semantic fields.

**HLR-20.010-008:** ΔH% regulation SHALL act as the primary dynamic governor for trace stability and termination.

**HLR-20.010-009:** Basin locality SHALL be enforced: computations SHALL prefer local grammar and operators unless explicitly authorized by GB.

**HLR-20.010-010:** Commitments SHALL only be formed on traces that meet explicit stability thresholds.

**HLR-20.010-011:** All supervisory actions by GB SHALL be auditable with fixed reason codes.

**HLR-20.010-012:** TS SHALL prohibit unbounded recursion or search within any single execution cycle.

**HLR-20.010-013:** Input SHALL be mapped to the nearest stable conceptual structures without invention.

**HLR-20.010-014:** Contradictions SHALL be preserved as parallel candidate traces rather than forcibly resolved at low levels.

**HLR-20.010-015:** Emotional and value-laden content SHALL be explicitly tagged and routed to appropriate basins.

**HLR-20.010-016:** Human oversight SHALL be requested for critical commitment promotions or safety boundary violations.

**HLR-20.010-017:** All state transitions SHALL remain replayable from initial seed and input.

## 1.8 Supervisory Separation Principle

**HLR-20.010-018:** GB SHALL act solely as supervisor and arbitrator, not as primary meaning constructor.

**HLR-20.010-019:** GB SHALL NOT perform direct primitive chaining or semantic composition.

**HLR-20.010-020:** Basin-internal logic SHALL remain independent of GB except at explicit safe boundaries.

**HLR-20.010-021:** GB decisions SHALL be limited to approve/deny, select-among-candidates, and request-clarification.

**HLR-20.010-022:** All GB actions SHALL route through deterministic supervisory interfaces.

**HLR-20.010-023:** GB complexity SHALL be strictly bounded.

**HLR-20.010-024:** GB SHALL delegate downward to basin-local grammar wherever possible.

**HLR-20.010-025:** GB intervention frequency SHALL be monitored and kept below defined thresholds.

**HLR-20.010-026:** GB SHALL maintain an explicit responsibility matrix (see 20.16).

## 1.x FIFO Ordering and Sequential Interpretation Invariant

**HLR-20.010-061:** All interpretation phases SHALL respect strict FIFO ordering of input tokens and prior TPs.

**HLR-20.010-062:** Sequential interpretation invariant SHALL be preserved across all basins.

**HLR-20.010-063:** No reordering or lookahead SHALL occur without explicit GB authorization at safe boundaries.

**HLR-20.010-064:** Each interpretation step SHALL be deterministic given prior state.

**HLR-20.010-065:** Full trace SHALL remain reproducible in original sequential order.

## 1.9 GB Supervisory Duties and Inquiry Governance

**HLR-20.010-027:** GB SHALL govern IB instantiation and population.

**HLR-20.010-028:** GB SHALL approve or deny new inquiry creation.

**HLR-20.010-029:** GB SHALL monitor and manage IB split/merge operations.

**HLR-20.010-030:** GB SHALL arbitrate between competing candidate traces.

**HLR-20.010-031:** GB SHALL validate candidate commitments before promotion.

**HLR-20.010-032:** GB SHALL detect and respond to ΔH% oscillations or drift.

**HLR-20.010-033:** GB SHALL enforce cross-basin coordination protocols.

**HLR-20.010-034:** GB SHALL request human oversight for high-stakes decisions.

## 1.14 Architectural Risk Mitigation and Refinements (Added 2026-06-03)

### Scope
This section defines architectural risks and mitigation strategies identified through rigorous external critique. It does not specify implementation details; those belong in the 30- and 40-series.

### Non-Goals
- This section does not define new primitives.
- It does not specify basin-specific grammar.
- It does not define GB algorithms.
- It does not prescribe ΔH% formulas or commitment decay rules. These belong in the 30- and 40-series.

### Strengths (Worth Protecting)
1. **Foundational Principles** — Determinism, auditability, explicit meaning construction, and supervisory control.  
2. **Layered Governance Model** — The 10/20/30/40/50 structure and promotion process.  
3. **Core Mechanisms** — Semantic operators + basin locality + ΔH% dynamic regulation + GB supervision.  
4. **Complexity Management** — Hierarchical grammar, commitments, typed operators, and basin locality.

### Major Architectural Risks
1. **GB Overload (Primary Risk)** — GB responsible for too many critical functions. Risk of becoming a monolithic “god module”.  
2. **Messy Real-World Input** — Insufficient explicit mechanisms for contradictory values, vague goals, emotional volatility, etc.  
3. **Conceptual vs Validated Mechanisms** — Many mechanisms remain largely conceptual.  
4. **Risk of Theoretical Elegance** — Elegant mechanisms on paper may not survive real implementation.

### Required Refinements (20-Layer Priority)
1. **GB Boundaries and Limitations** — Define what GB must/must not do; distribute responsibilities downward.  
2. **Explicit Messy Input Handling** — Rules for contradictory commitments, emotional content, etc.  
3. **Success Criteria and Failure Modes** — Define for all major mechanisms.  
4. **Limits on Complexity** — Maximum trace length, parallel candidates, commitment depth, etc.
