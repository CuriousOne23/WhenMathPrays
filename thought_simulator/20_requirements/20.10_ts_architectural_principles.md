# **20.10 TS Architectural Principles (Rewritten, Part 1)**

---

# **0. Purpose & Scope**  
*(Informative — no SHALL statements)*

This document defines the architectural principles that govern the Thought Simulator (TS). It establishes the foundational invariants, structural boundaries, and design commitments that all TS‑20 modules must satisfy. These principles ensure that meaning construction remains deterministic, interpretable, structured, and auditable across all basins and execution cycles.

20.10 does not define functional behavior or pipeline mechanics in detail; those appear in 20.30 and the 20.40–20.131 series. Instead, 20.10 defines the **why** behind the architecture: the constraints, separations, and invariants that make the TS deterministic, stable, and safe.

The Structural Signal Generator (20.47) defines the canonical structural‑signature model, routing‑input constraints, boundary discipline, multi‑core isolation rules, and determinism requirements that govern all TS architectural principles.

**HLR‑20.10‑080** — The TS SHALL treat the Structural Signal Generator (20.47) as a governing primitive for all architectural principles defined in 20.10, and all principles in this document SHALL be interpreted in a manner consistent with 20.47.


**HLR‑20.10‑080** — The TS SHALL treat the Structural Signal Generator (20.47) as a governing document for all architectural principles defined in 20.10, and all principles in this document SHALL be interpreted in a manner consistent with 20.47.


This document is the root of the TS‑20 requirements tree. All other 20‑series specifications derive their obligations from the principles defined here.

# **1. Architectural Foundations**  
*(Informative — no SHALL statements)*

This section describes the conceptual foundations of the TS architecture. These principles are descriptive and explanatory; normative requirements appear in Section 2.

## **1.1 Determinism as a First Principle**  
The TS is designed to produce identical results for identical inputs, MTP state, and seeds. Determinism is not an optimization; it is the core architectural value that enables replay, auditability, and safety.

## 1.1.1 The SSG Routing Equation (Normative)

The TS architecture is governed by a canonical routing equation defined in 20.47.  
This equation specifies the complete set of fields that participate in routing and establishes the architectural contract for deterministic traversal of the relational topology.

**HLR‑20.10‑073** — The TS SHALL treat the SSG routing equation as the authoritative definition of routing behavior across all basins.

The routing equation is:

$$
RB\_route(TP) = f(TP.input\_fields,\ TP.TR,\ TP.tr\_needs\_update,\ TP.\Delta H,\ TP.lineage,\ TP.routing\_metadata,\ policy\_signature)
$$

This equation defines the only inputs that may influence routing decisions.  
All other fields, including seed and Pipeline B state, are architecturally excluded from routing.

## **1.2 Explicit, Structured Meaning**  
Meaning is represented explicitly in structured objects (TP, MTP). No latent vectors, embeddings, or opaque representations participate in meaning construction. All semantics are visible, inspectable, and auditable.

## **1.3 Basin‑Based Cognitive Decomposition**  
The TS decomposes cognition into specialized basins:

- **OB pipeline (SOB → SROB → CnOB → SmOB)** extracts structural, refined, constraint, and pre‑semantic residue.
- **Identity OB** is a Path A primitive invoked after RB for identity‑specific interpretation or transformation.
- **RB** arbitrates structural transitions and determines routing.  
- **TE** executes structural transformations (split, merge, rebind).  
- **TR** updates semantic routing fields.  
- **TB** interprets stance, affect, intent, logic, and social meaning.  
- **TPU** commits semantic updates to the MTP.

Each basin has a single, well‑defined responsibility.

## 1.4 Pipeline Boundary Discipline (Normative)

The Structural Signal Generator (20.47) defines strict separation between Pipeline A and Pipeline B.  
This separation ensures that routing, structural transitions, and semantic updates remain deterministic, auditable, and free from cross‑pipeline interference.

**HLR‑20.10‑074** — The TS SHALL enforce Pipeline A/B boundary discipline as defined in 20.47, ensuring that no Pipeline B module may influence routing, TR updates, or structural‑transition eligibility.

Pipeline A consists of:
- OB (local meaning extraction)
- RB (routing and structural‑transition arbitration)
- TR (semantic routing updates)

Pipeline B consists of:
- IMR, OBG, SRP, and other interpretive or supervisory modules

Pipeline B modules do not participate in routing and cannot modify routing‑relevant fields.  
Seed values, supervisory signals, and truth‑hypothesis structures are architecturally excluded from routing decisions.

## 1.5 Canonical Ordering as a Determinism Mechanism (Normative)

The Structural Signal Generator (20.47) establishes canonical ordering as a global architectural requirement.  
Canonical ordering ensures that all exported structures, routing filters, and nested maps remain stable across executions, enabling replay determinism and auditability.

**HLR‑20.10‑075** — The TS SHALL apply canonical ordering to all nested maps, ID arrays, and exported routing structures to guarantee replay determinism and structural stability.

Canonical ordering is not an optimization; it is a determinism mechanism.  
It ensures that identical inputs produce identical serialized outputs, regardless of execution timing, core assignment, or internal scheduling.

## 1.6 Multi‑Core Routing Isolation (Normative)

The Structural Signal Generator (20.47) defines multi‑core routing isolation as a global architectural constraint.  
Each core operates as an independent routing domain, ensuring that routing decisions remain deterministic and free from cross‑core interference.

**HLR‑20.10‑076** — The TS SHALL enforce multi‑core routing isolation, ensuring that no cross‑core merges, splits, consolidations, or routing‑metadata interactions occur.

Core identity is treated strictly as routing metadata.  
It does not carry semantic meaning and does not participate in truth evaluation, supervisory interpretation, or any Pipeline B process.

## 1.7 TR‑Gating Contract (Normative)

The Structural Signal Generator (20.47) defines a deterministic gating contract for semantic updates. 
The Thought Router (TR) is the only module permitted to update semantic routing state, and such updates occur only under explicit architectural conditions.

**HLR‑20.10‑077** — The TS SHALL enforce the TR‑gating contract, ensuring that TR updates occur only when `tr_needs_update = true`, and that no other module may write or modify TR state.

The TR‑gating mechanism ensures that:
- semantic updates are deterministic  
- routing decisions remain stable across cycles  
- no basin may bypass or override TR authority  
- TR state is never implicitly inferred or mutated  

The gating condition is expressed as:

$$
TR\_update(TP) \text{ occurs iff } TP.tr\_needs\_update = \text{true}
$$

This contract prevents accidental semantic drift and ensures that routing semantics remain auditable and replayable.

## 1.8 Messy‑Input Handling (Normative)

The Structural Signal Generator (20.47) and the Messy‑Input Specification (20.17) define strict architectural constraints on how the TS treats incomplete, noisy, or structurally inconsistent inputs.  
Messy inputs are permitted, but they do not alter routing semantics and cannot override deterministic behavior.

**HLR‑20.10‑078** — The TS SHALL treat messy inputs as read‑only artifacts and SHALL NOT smooth, resolve, infer, or reinterpret missing or noisy fields during routing or semantic update.

Messy‑input handling ensures that:
- routing remains deterministic even under partial information  
- no basin introduces implicit meaning reconstruction  
- TR‑gating behavior is unaffected by missing fields  
- replay determinism is preserved across noisy inputs  

Messy inputs may be logged, inspected, or passed through, but they do not participate in semantic inference or structural‑transition logic.

## 1.9 Replay Determinism (Normative)

Replay determinism is a foundational architectural requirement defined in the Structural Signal Generator (20.47). It ensures that identical inputs produce identical routing decisions, structural transitions, and semantic updates across all executions, independent of timing, scheduling, or core assignment.

**HLR‑20.10‑079** — The TS SHALL guarantee replay determinism by ensuring that all routing decisions, structural‑transition outcomes, and exported state representations are fully reproducible from logged inputs and canonicalized structures.

Replay determinism is enforced through:
- canonical ordering of all nested maps and ID arrays  
- deterministic routing filter construction  
- deterministic TR‑gating behavior  
- deterministic multi‑core firing‑order computation  
- deterministic cycle‑ordering semantics  

The replay condition can be expressed as:

$$
TS\_run(inputs) = TS\_replay(inputs)
$$

for all architecturally valid inputs and for all execution environments that conform to the SSG.

## **1.10 Structural vs Semantic Responsibilities**  
The TS separates structural and semantic operations:

- **Structural operations** (split, merge, rebind, topology changes) are executed exclusively by **TE**.  
- **Semantic operations** (meaning interpretation, ΔH% integration, truth evaluation) occur in **TB**, **TR**, and **TPU**.

This separation ensures clarity, safety, and deterministic replay.

## **1.11 Lane‑Parallel Meaning Construction**  
Pipeline A operates in lane‑parallel form. Each lane carries a partial view of meaning, and all lanes contribute deterministically to the final MTP state.

## **1.12 Replay Stability and Auditability**  
Every state transition is attributable, logged, and reproducible. ΔH% provides a normalized structural‑entropy measure that tracks evidence flow across basins.

## **1.13 Supervisory Separation (GB)**  
The Global Basin (GB) supervises long‑range stability, drift, and inquiry evolution. GB does not participate in meaning construction and does not mutate TP or MTP semantics.

# **3. Structural Architecture**  
*(Informative — no SHALL statements)*

This section describes the structural components of the TS and their architectural roles. These descriptions are explanatory; normative requirements appear in Section 2.

## **3.1 Thought Packet (TP)**  
TP is the atomic semantic object in Pipeline A. It contains propositions, stance, affect, intent, ΔH%, lineage, semantic tags, and routing metadata. TP is not a vector; it is a structured, typed object with deterministic fields.

## **3.2 Master Thought Packet (MTP)**  
MTP is the authoritative semantic state for the current thought. All lane TPs merge into the MTP through deterministic semantic merge executed by TPU. MTP is frozen at `mtp_update` and becomes the input to Pipeline B.

## **3.3 Observation Basin (OB)**  
The term “OB” refers to two related components:

1. **The OB pipeline** (SOB → SROB → CnOB → SmOB), which extracts structural residue, refined residue, constraint metadata, semantic‑adjacent cues, and ΔH%.  
2. **The identity OB primitive**, a Path A basin invoked after RB selects it using the SSG routing vector, responsible for identity‑specific interpretation or transformation.

The OB pipeline does not interpret meaning, perform routing, or execute structural transitions.  
The identity OB primitive does not extract residue or participate in routing.

### **3.3.1 SmOB’s Two Jobs**  
SmOB performs two distinct pre‑semantic jobs:  
1. **Pre‑semantic cue extraction** — extracting semantic‑adjacent cues, modality cues, affect markers, conflict‑adjacent signals, and TR‑input cues.  
2. **Pre‑semantic residue compression** — producing a deterministic pre‑semantic residue hash and TR‑input cue vector consumed by SSG.

SmOB does not perform semantic interpretation, stance assignment, or truth evaluation.

## **3.4 Relational Basin (RB)**  
RB determines which structural transitions are permitted. RB arbitrates:

- which OB outputs are structurally compatible  
- which transitions TE may execute  
- which lanes require TR updates  
- which lanes require TB interpretation  

RB does not execute transitions; it authorizes them.

## **3.5 Transition Executor (TE)**  
TE is the sole structural executor in Pipeline A. TE performs:

- structural split  
- structural merge  
- rebind operations  
- topology changes  
- structural normalization  

TE executes only RB‑approved transitions.

## **3.6 Thought Router (TR)**  
TR updates semantic routing fields when `tr_needs_update = true`. TR does not perform structural transitions and does not interpret meaning.

## **3.7 Truth Basin (TB)**  
TB interprets stance, affect, intent, logic, and social meaning. TB outputs structured fields, not probabilities.

## **3.8 Routing/Thought Updater (RTU)**  
RTU is the sole writer of authoritative TP and MTP state.  
RTU performs:

- Path A error‑correction commits (the only time RTU writes to TP during Pipeline A)  
- final semantic merge commits at mtp_update

## **3.9 ΔH% Structural‑Entropy Flow**  
ΔH% tracks structural‑entropy changes across basins. It is deterministic, monotonic, and MI‑class normalized.

## **3.10 Structural vs Semantic Merge**  
The TS separates merge into two distinct operations:

- **Structural merge** — TE  
- **Semantic merge** — RTU  

This separation ensures clarity, safety, and deterministic replay.

# **4. Pipeline Architecture**  
*(Informative + Normative subsections)*

This section describes the canonical flow of meaning construction and realization.

## **4.1 Pipeline A Overview**  
*(Informative)*  
Pipeline A constructs meaning through lane‑parallel basin operations. It transforms input into structured semantic state.

## **4.2 Canonical Structural Flow**  
*(Informative)*

The structural flow is:

```
SOB → SROB → CnOB → SmOB → SSG → RB → identity OB → TE → RB → TR → TB → RTU → MTP Update
```

The OB pipeline (SOB → SROB → CnOB → SmOB) performs structural, refinement, constraint, and pre‑semantic extraction.  
SSG generates the routing vector.  
RB consumes the routing vector and selects identity OBs.  
Identity OB performs identity‑specific processing before TE executes structural transitions.

## **4.3 Canonical Semantic Flow**  
*(Informative)*

Semantic interpretation proceeds through:

- ΔH% integration  
- TB interpretation  
- truth evaluation  
- semantic merge  
- MTP commit  

## **4.4 Pipeline B Overview**  
*(Informative)*

Pipeline B consumes the frozen `semantic_core` and performs:

- realization planning  
- expression  
- supervisory integration  
- output generation  

Pipeline B is singular per `commit_id` and never lane‑parallel.

## **4.5 A/B Boundary**  
*(Normative — existing HLRs preserved)*

**HLR‑20.10‑025** through **HLR‑20.10‑030** (from Part 1) define the A/B boundary:

- Pipeline A constructs meaning  
- Pipeline B realizes meaning  
- Pipeline B consumes only frozen semantic state  
- Pipeline B does not mutate TP or MTP

# **9. Architectural Rationale**  
*(Informative — no SHALL statements)*

The TS architecture is designed to:

- eliminate nondeterminism  
- eliminate latent‑space entanglement  
- ensure explicit meaning  
- ensure deterministic replay  
- ensure auditability  
- ensure safety through structural/semantic separation  
- ensure clarity through basin specialization  
- ensure stability through GB supervision  

The separation of structural and semantic responsibilities, the RB→TE→RB loop, and the deterministic commit model together form the foundation of TS‑20.
