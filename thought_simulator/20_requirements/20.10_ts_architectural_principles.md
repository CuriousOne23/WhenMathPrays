# 20.10 TS Architectural Principles

## Purpose
Defines the architectural principles that constrain all TS requirement modules.

## Normative Requirements
1. HLR-20.010-001: The TS core SHALL be deterministic for identical input, MTP state, and seed.
2. HLR-20.010-002: Meaning construction SHALL remain explicit, structured, and auditable.
3. HLR-20.010-003: Variability SHALL be isolated to the output layer through the randomness boundary.
4. HLR-20.010-004: Program flow SHALL follow fixed phase ordering and SHALL prohibit cross-phase mutation.
5. HLR-20.010-005: Observer influence SHALL remain external and SHALL NOT mutate TS core state directly.

This module incorporates the architectural ordering intent previously captured in legacy program-flow requirements.

## 1.14 Architectural Risk Mitigation and Refinements (Added 2026-06-03)

### Scope
This section defines architectural risks and mitigation strategies identified through rigorous external critique. It does not specify implementation details; those belong in the 30- and 40-series.

### Non-Goals
- This section does not define new primitives.
- It does not specify basin-specific grammar.
- It does not define GB algorithms.
- It does not prescribe ΔH% formulas or commitment decay rules. These belong in the 30- and 40-series.

### Strengths (Worth Protecting)
1. **Foundational Principles** — Determinism, auditability, explicit meaning construction, and supervisory control.
2. **Layered Governance Model** — The 10/20/30/40/50 structure and promotion process.
3. **Core Mechanisms** — Semantic operators + basin locality + ΔH% dynamic regulation + GB supervision.
4. **Complexity Management** — Hierarchical grammar, commitments, typed operators, and basin locality.

### Major Architectural Risks
1. **GB Overload (Primary Risk)**  
   GB is responsible for too many critical functions (coherence scoring, commitment management, arbitration, learned component validation, cross-basin coordination, ΔH% oversight). Risk of becoming a monolithic “god module”.

2. **Messy Real-World Input**  
   Insufficient explicit mechanisms for contradictory values, vague/unstable goals, emotional volatility, self-deception, and noisy/incomplete information.

3. **Conceptual vs Validated Mechanisms**  
   Many mechanisms remain largely conceptual and require formalization, stress testing, and failure-mode analysis.

4. **Risk of Theoretical Elegance**  
   Elegant mechanisms on paper may not survive contact with real implementation complexity and messy data.

### Required Refinements (20-Layer Priority)
These refinements SHALL remain consistent with the invariants defined in 20.30 and SHALL NOT weaken any constraints defined therein.

1. **GB Boundaries and Limitations**  
   - Define what GB must and must not do.  
   - Keep GB modular and inspectable.  
   - Distribute responsibilities downward when possible.

2. **Explicit Messy Input Handling**  
   - Rules for contradictory commitments, emotional content, ambiguous goals, and noisy input.  
   - Graceful degradation strategies.

3. **Success Criteria and Failure Modes**  
   - Define success criteria, failure modes, and graceful degradation for all major mechanisms (ΔH%, commitments, BLG, GB arbitration, etc.).

4. **Limits on Complexity**  
   - Maximum trace length per basin.  
   - Maximum parallel candidate traces.  
   - Maximum commitment depth.  
   - Primitive composition constraints.
