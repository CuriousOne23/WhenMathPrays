# **20.30 TS Functional Model (Revised)**  

---

# **Purpose**

This document defines the functional model of the Thought Simulator (TS) and specifies the deterministic meaning‑construction pipeline known as **Pipeline A**. It describes how structured meaning is extracted, routed, transformed, merged, evaluated, and committed into the Master Thought Packet (MTP). It also defines the relationship between Pipeline A and the realization‑side **Pipeline B**, which consumes the committed semantic state.

This specification establishes the architectural boundaries among OB, RB, TE, TR, TB, TPU, and MTP, and defines how meaning construction proceeds deterministically across cycles. It also defines the global invariants, stability constraints, and replay guarantees that govern TS behavior.

This document is descriptive; all normative obligations appear in the HLR‑20.030‑xxx series.

---

# **0. Functional Overview**

The TS operates as a deterministic, structured meaning‑construction engine. Pipeline A transforms input into explicit semantic state through a sequence of basin‑level primitives. The pipeline is lane‑parallel, deterministic, and replay‑stable. All semantic commitments occur through TPU, and all structural transitions occur through TE after RB arbitration.

The canonical structural flow is:

> **OB → TP → RB (arbitrate) → TE (execute) → RB (route) → TR → TB → Merge → TPU → MTP Update**

Pipeline B consumes the frozen `semantic_core` after `mtp_update` and performs realization, expression, and supervisory integration.

# **Dual‑Pipeline Functional Partition (Revised)**

```text
Pipeline A (Meaning Construction):
InB → IIInB → CEx → CE → ISc → TPU → OB → RB → TE → RB → TR → TB → Merge → TPU → MTP Update

Pipeline B (Realization):
semantic_core(commit_id) → XP → IB → TB(B) → GBIB → GB → OuB
```

**Clarifications**

- **Structural split/merge** is executed exclusively by **TE** after RB arbitration.  
- **Semantic merge** is executed by **TPU**.  
- **TP** is a structure, not a stage.  
- **TR, TB, DCB** operate post‑TE within Pipeline A.  
- **Pipeline B** is singular per `commit_id` and never consumes lane TPs.

# **1. Architectural Principles**

## **1.1 Determinism**  
The TS core is fully deterministic: identical input and MTP state produce identical output.  
**HLR‑20.030‑331** applies.

## **1.2 Structured Meaning**  
Meaning is represented explicitly in the MTP, not in latent vectors.  
**HLR‑20.030‑332** applies.

## **1.3 Modularity**  
OB, RB, TE, TR, TB, and TS‑core modules are separable and testable.  
**HLR‑20.030‑333** applies.

## **1.4 Interpretability**  
Every reasoning step is visible: OB activations, RB routing, TE execution, TR updates, TB interpretation, ΔH% flow, and MTP updates.  
**HLR‑20.030‑334** applies.

## **1.5 Constructive Mapping**  
Meaning is constructed from OB/RB path semantics, ΔH% contributions, ΔH% missing mass, and TB interpretations.  
**HLR‑20.030‑335** applies.

## **1.6 Optional Variability**  
Variability is introduced only in designated modules and never affects TS core determinism.  
**HLR‑20.030‑336** applies.

## **1.7 Random Seed Boundary**  
The TS core is seed‑independent; the seed affects only expressive variability.  
**HLR‑20.030‑337** and **338** apply.

## **1.8 Deterministic Runtime Profiles & Artifact Governance**  
Profiles, hashing, diagnostics, export, and parser behavior remain deterministic.  
HLR‑20.030‑277 through 290 apply.

## **1.9 COB/CIL/COP Functional Boundaries**  
Conversation integration, object lifecycle, and proposal subsystems operate deterministically and under GB supervision.  
HLR‑20.030‑291 through 303 apply.

## **1.10 RB Relationship Safety Enforcement**  
RB enforces deterministic relation‑safety boundaries and logs violations.  
HLR‑20.030‑304 through 321 apply.

## **1.11 Semantic Specification Reference**  
Core meaning semantics are defined in 20.31; reference algorithms in 20.35.

## **1.12 Deterministic Replay Guarantee**
HLR‑20.030‑339: TS SHALL achieve deterministic replay for identical (input, MTP state, seed) tuples across all basins and meaning‑construction stages.

This is an architectural invariant, not a benchmark claim.

## **1.13 Deterministic Policy Enforcement**

**HLR‑20.030‑325 — Deterministic Policy Enforcement at RB**  
TS SHALL enforce all policy constraints deterministically at **RB** safe boundaries.  
TB SHALL NOT participate in policy enforcement.  
No unlogged policy violation SHALL be possible.  
All blocked relations or constraint violations SHALL be logged with deterministic reason codes.

This is a functional guarantee, not a performance percentage.

# **2. TS Primitives (Definitions — Pipeline A)**

These are the fundamental units of Pipeline A meaning construction.

## **2.1 TP — Thought Packet**  
A structured semantic object containing:

- propositions  
- stance  
- affect  
- intent  
- ΔH%  
- lineage  
- semantic tags  
- tr_needs_update (boolean TR stale/fresh indicator)  

## **2.2 MTP — Master Thought Packet**  
The global semantic state for the current thought.  
All lane TPs merge into the MTP.

## **2.3 OB — Observation Basin**  
A correlator operating closest to the geometric substrate.  
Extracts local meaning fragments and ΔH%.

## **2.4 RB — Relational Basin**  
Defines the topology of meaning extraction:

- which OBs fire  
- in what order  
- with what projections  

## **2.5 TB — Truth Basin**  
Modules that interpret meaning:

- TB‑logic  
- TB‑emotion  
- TB‑intent  
- TB‑stance  
- TB‑social  

## **2.6 ΔH% — Entropy Change**  
A normalized measure of how much evidence a lane TP contributes to a hypothesis.

---

### **HLR‑20.030‑015 — Unified ΔH% Structural‑Entropy Delta**

> **ΔH% SHALL be defined as the normalized structural‑entropy delta between a primitive’s incoming envelope and its outgoing envelope.  
> All TS‑20 primitives SHALL compute ΔH% using this unified architectural definition.  
> Each primitive SHALL define its local entropy model (H_before, H_after, uncertainty sources, and normalization) in its corresponding 10.50.xx specification.  
> ΔH% SHALL remain deterministic, monotonic, MI‑class‑normalized, and SHALL NOT encode probability, truth, or semantic inference.**

---

## **2.7 TCU — Thought Compute Unit**  
A normalized unit of computational cost.  
Calibrated via the TCU kernel (Section 7).

## **2.8 MCS - Meaningful Cognitive Step**
A Meaningful Cognitive Step (MCS) is a logged, attributable, state-changing transition that advances the task toward a declared success condition.
An MCS SHALL:

- be logged deterministically
- be attributable to a specific TP/MTP transition
- alter validated task state
- map to a success path or corrective action
- NOT include no-ops, formatting changes, or repeated artifact emissions
- NOT allow micro-step inflation

# **3. Meaning Construction Pipeline (Pipeline A)**

Sections 2–3 describe **Pipeline A only** (lane-parallel meaning construction). Pipeline B realization after `mtp_update` is summarized above and in [20.206](20.206_pipeline_a_b_synchronization_contract.md) (draft pending Stage 4).

## **3.1 Input**
A prompt or prior MTP enters the system.

## **3.2 Routing**  
RBs determine:

- which OBs fire  
- the order of activation  
- the lane projections  

HLR‑20.030‑340: RB SHALL route the TP to the TR routine if and only if tr_needs_update = true.

## **3.3 Splitting (Projection)**  
The TP is split into lane TPs.

Projection removes some semantics (e.g., S₂ for OB₁), producing **partial views**.

Each lane TP carries:

- visible semantics  
- suppressed semantics  
- ΔH% potential  
- local context  

## **3.4 OB Processing**  
Each OB:

- processes its lane TP  
- extracts local meaning fragments  
- computes ΔH%  
- returns structured evidence  

HLR‑20.030‑341: Any OB that modifies semantic‑relevant TP fields SHALL set tr_needs_update = true.

## **3.4.1 DCB Observation (Geometric Meta-Basin)**

After OB processing and before the TR routine:

- DCB observes the TP trajectory (position, direction, curvature) across steps.
- DCB emits ephemeral directional-change events when bounded geometric invariants are exceeded.
- DCB does not write TP fields and does not interpret meaning.
- RB does not consume DCB events.
- TR consumes DCB events only when `tr_needs_update = true` and RB routes to the TR routine.

HLR‑20.030‑342: DCB SHALL emit deterministic directional‑change events based on bounded geometric invariants.  
HLR‑20.030‑343: DCB SHALL NOT write TP fields and SHALL NOT interpret meaning.  
HLR‑20.030‑344: TR SHALL consume DCB events only when tr_needs_update = true and RB routes to TR.

Normative cycle ordering:

```text
RB → OB → DCB observes trajectory → (if tr_needs_update) TR routine → RB
```

## **3.5 TB Interpretation**
TB modules interpret:

- stance  
- affect  
- intent  
- logic  
- social position  

TB outputs are structured fields, not probabilities.

## **3.6 Merging**  
Lane TPs merge into the MTP.

Merging is deterministic:

- ΔH% contributions combined  
- missing ΔH% accounted for  
- TB interpretations integrated  
- semantic fields updated  

HLR‑20.030‑345: Any module that modifies semantic‑relevant TP fields during merge SHALL set tr_needs_update = true.

## **3.7 Truth and Completion**
TS evaluates:

- **H%** (entropy / uncertainty of the TP)
- **truth_hypotheses** (structured truth-evaluation fields)
- **done** (completion state)

HLR‑20.030‑348: Truth and completion evaluation SHALL be deterministic and SHALL use structured truth_hypotheses and H% semantics.

## **3.7.1 MTP Update (`mtp_update`) — A/B Boundary**

Pipeline A commits `semantic_core` at `mtp_update` and emits **`commit_id`**. After commit, `semantic_core` is frozen for Pipeline B (read-only). Normative freeze and handoff: `[Looks like the result wasn't safe to show. Let's switch things up and try something else!]`, `[Looks like the result wasn't safe to show. Let's switch things up and try something else!]` (20.500 refactor — draft pending Stage 4).

## **3.8 Response Generation**
The response generator expresses the meaning in the MTP.  
Variability is introduced only here, and only via a pseudo-random seed.
The mapping from (MTP, seed) -> output is deterministic.  
HLR‑20.030‑351: The response‑generator seed SHALL NOT influence meaning construction and SHALL affect only expressive variability.

# **4. Splitting and Merging (Formal Overview)**

## **4.1 Splitting**  
Splitting occurs when the TP is projected into multiple lanes.

HLR‑20.030‑349: Splitting SHALL be deterministic, rule‑based, explicit, and loss‑accounted through ΔH% semantics.

### Properties:

- deterministic  
- rule‑based  
- explicit  
- lossless (ΔH% tracks what was removed)  

### Why it works:  
Meaning is structured, so we can explicitly choose:

- which fields are visible  
- which fields are suppressed  
- which semantics each OB receives  

---

## **4.2 Merging**  
Merging recombines lane TPs into the MTP.

HLR‑20.030‑350: Merging SHALL recombine lane TPs into the MTP deterministically using ΔH%, TB interpretations, and prior MTP state.

### Properties:

- deterministic  
- rule‑based  
- auditable  
- field‑wise  

### Merging uses:

- ΔH% contributions  
- ΔH% missing mass  
- TB interpretations  
- prior MTP state  

### Why it works:  
The MTP is a **schema**, not a vector.  
Fields merge cleanly:

- max  
- sum  
- overwrite  
- weighted  
- rule‑based

# **7. TCU Allocation and Computational Budgeting**

## **7.1 TCU Definition**  
A TCU is a normalized unit of computational cost.  
It abstracts away hardware differences.

## **7.2 Calibration Kernel**  
To compute the machine’s TCU baseline:

```text
for i in range(10^7):
    x = (x * 1.0000001) - (x / 1.0000003)
```

Measure runtime → convert to TCU baseline.

## **7.3 Per‑Subsystem TCU Budgets**

| Subsystem         | Min | Typ | Max |
|-------------------|-----|-----|-----|
| RB Routing        | 1   | 2   | 4   |
| Splitting         | 2   | 5   | 10  |
| OB Processing     | 10  | 25  | 40  |
| TB Interpretation | 5   | 15  | 25  |
| Merging           | 4   | 10  | 20  |
| Truth/Done        | 2   | 5   | 10  |
| MTP Update        | 1   | 3   | 5   |

## **7.4 Deterministic Degradation Rules**  
If a subsystem exceeds its budget:

- skip non‑critical TB modules  
- reduce semantic shading  
- postpone lineage updates  
- never break determinism  

## **7.5 Reporting Requirements**  
Each tick reports:

- TCU usage per subsystem  
- total TCU  
- ΔH% cost attribution  
- MTP update cost  

## **7.6 MCS Counting Rules**
HLR‑20.030‑346: TS SHALL count only deterministic, state‑changing transitions as MCS and SHALL prohibit micro‑step inflation.

TS prohibits:

- micro-step inflation  
- repeated artifact inflation  
- no-op transitions  
- formatting-only transitions  
- artificial subdivision of steps  

All counted steps need to be independently reviewable from the trace.

## **7.7 MCS-to-TCU Deterministic Mapping**
HLR‑20.030‑347: Each MCS SHALL consume a deterministic TCU envelope derived from subsystem budgets, invariant under identical execution signatures.

# **8. TS Stability, Boundedness, and Overflow Control**

## Purpose
Defines global deterministic constraints that keep TS stable, bounded, and non-explosive across cycles. Establishes system-wide limits on lane growth, evidence size, ΔH% flow, provenance depth, and TCU consumption. Specifies deterministic overflow behavior and user-visible tagging rules when limits are exceeded.

## Role in the Development Flow

This document serves as **functional guidance** for the 40-series playground prototypes. It describes the desired high-level behavior and flow of the Thought Simulator.

Insights, practical challenges, and validation results from 40-series prototyping SHOULD be used to refine this document and mature the final normative definitions in the 10-series. This document is **not** intended as final executable specification.

## **8.1 Normative Requirements**

### **Global Boundedness**

1. HLR-20.030-201: TS SHALL define a deterministic global maximum for active lanes per cycle. RB, OB, and TB SHALL enforce this bound.  
2. HLR-20.030-202: TS SHALL define a deterministic maximum number of OB activations per cycle to prevent uncontrolled fan-out.  
3. HLR-20.030-203: TS SHALL define a deterministic maximum routing fan-out per TP to prevent exponential lane growth.  
4. HLR-20.030-204: TS SHALL define maximum evidence payload sizes for OB outputs to prevent unbounded memory growth.  
5. HLR-20.030-205: TS SHALL define maximum TB interpretation channel sizes and hypothesis counts.  
6. HLR-20.030-206: TS SHALL define a deterministic maximum provenance chain depth to prevent runaway lineage accumulation.  
7. HLR-20.030-207: TS SHALL define maximum ΔH% contribution per basin per cycle to prevent runaway entropy inflation.  
8. HLR-20.030-208: TS SHALL define global per-cycle and per-basin TCU budgets (min/typ/max) that all basins must enforce.

### **Deterministic Overflow and Degradation**

9. HLR-20.030-209: If any global bound is exceeded, TS SHALL detect the overflow deterministically within the same cycle.  
10. HLR-20.030-210: When overflow occurs, TS SHALL tag the TP with a deterministic overflow/degradation flag accessible to MB, GB, OuB, IB, and user-facing layers.  
11. HLR-20.030-211: Overflow SHALL trigger deterministic, policy-bounded degradation behavior, including lane truncation, evidence truncation, ΔH% normalization, provenance compression, and routing fan-out reduction.  
12. HLR-20.030-212: TS SHALL NOT silently drop lanes, evidence, or interpretation fields without deterministic tagging and logging.  
13. HLR-20.030-213: Overflow SHALL NOT trigger probabilistic recovery, hallucinated reconstruction, or latent-state inference.

### **User-Visible Stability Reporting**

14. HLR-20.030-214: TS SHALL expose a deterministic, human-readable stability signal when overflow or degradation occurs.  
15. HLR-20.030-215: TS SHALL include stability metadata in the MTP snapshot, including overflow type, affected basin, truncated fields, ΔH% normalization applied, and TCU overrun details.  
16. HLR-20.030-216: All overflow events SHALL be logged in a deterministic, replayable format for verification and debugging.  
17. HLR-20.030-217: TS SHALL define a canonical overflow field schema for TP/MTP stability tags including overflow_flag, overflow_type, overflow_source_basin, overflow_cycle, truncated_fields, ΔH%_normalization_applied, and tcu_overrun_amount.  
18. HLR-20.030-218: Basin-level overflow/degradation telemetry in OB, RB, and TB SHALL conform to the canonical overflow field schema.

## **8.2 TS Stability Invariants**

- Boundedness invariant: TS SHALL remain within global deterministic bounds for lanes, evidence, ΔH%, provenance, and TCU.  
- Deterministic degradation invariant: overflow SHALL always produce the same degradation outcome for identical effective state.  
- Auditability invariant: all overflow and degradation events SHALL be attributable to explicit global bounds and deterministic rules.

## **8.3 Canonical Overflow Field Schema**

Overflow and degradation tagging SHALL use the following shared field names across TP, MTP, and basin telemetry:

- overflow_flag: boolean indicator that overflow/degradation occurred in the cycle.  
- overflow_type: deterministic category of overflow event.  
- overflow_source_basin: basin identifier that emitted or detected the overflow condition.  
- overflow_cycle: cycle identifier or tick index for the overflow event.  
- truncated_fields: deterministic list of fields that were truncated/compressed/dropped by policy.  
- ΔH%_normalization_applied: record indicating whether and how ΔH% normalization was applied.  
- tcu_overrun_amount: deterministic overrun amount relative to the configured TCU envelope.

## **8.4 Global Basin (GB) Supervisory Role and Interfaces**

GB is the global supervisory basin responsible for long-range semantic stability, drift/oscillation detection, and cross-cycle coherence.

19. HLR-20.030-219: GB SHALL act as a global supervisory basin for long-range semantic stability, drift/oscillation detection, and cross-cycle coherence.  
20. HLR-20.030-220: GB SHALL read only TP lane-local semantic state and MPs (monitoring packets / TS health telemetry) for supervisory evaluation.  
21. HLR-20.030-221: GB SHALL NOT read OB, RB, TB, IB, InB, or OuB internal state or MTP internal state directly.  
22. HLR-20.030-222: GB SHALL operate within an explicit small TS cycle share (nominally 3-5%) and a bounded TCU envelope, and SHALL degrade/fallback deterministically when exceeded.  
23. HLR-20.030-223: TS service requests that affect global behavior (including deeper inquiry, extended reasoning, and mode changes) SHALL route through GB for deterministic stability/drift evaluation.  
24. HLR-20.030-224: GB MAY issue bounded deterministic supervisory flow-control actions (such as Stop, Slow, Dampen, Escalate, Safe-Mode) and these actions SHALL NOT mutate TP/MTP meaning-construction state.  
25. HLR-20.030-225: GB SHALL expose a deterministic supervisory command interface for higher-level safety/supervisory controllers.  
26. HLR-20.030-226: GB SHALL translate external supervisory commands into bounded TS-level supervisory actions consistent with the global stability envelope.  
27. HLR-20.030-227: TS<->GB and external<->GB supervisory interfaces SHALL be bounded, deterministic, measurable, and constrainable by directionality, mechanism, timing, and rate/priority limits.  
28. HLR-20.030-228: Detailed GB mode definitions, message schemas, priority models, and concrete rate limits SHALL be specified in 20.80 GB Requirements and traced to this subsection.

## **8.5 GB Architectural Realization and TS/GB Partitioning**

## **8.7 Step Inflation Prohibition**
TS SHALL prohibit artificial inflation of cognitive steps.  
Inflation includes:

- subdividing a single semantic action into multiple logged steps  
- emitting redundant or repeated artifacts  
- generating meaningless micro-steps  

Violations SHALL be tagged as deterministic degradation events.

# **9. Randomness Requirements**

## **9.1 Random Seed Definition**
The TS uses a pseudo-random seed to select among valid expression-layer realizations.

## **9.2 Isolation Requirement**
The seed SHALL NOT affect OB/RB/TB processing, ΔH%, truth evaluation, or MTP updates.

## **9.3 Statistical Requirements**
The pseudo-random generator SHALL have:

- a documented minimum cycle length  
- validated distribution properties  
- stable behavior across hardware  

## **9.4 Reproducibility**
Given the same seed, the TS SHALL produce identical outputs.

## **9.5 Safety Requirement**
Randomness-critical code SHALL NOT be modified without re-verification.

- OB activation SHALL be spelling-independent and concept-based for equivalent noisy variants.

- RB SHALL stabilize meaning without hallucinating corrections or inventing content.  
- ΔH% updates SHALL remain bounded and deterministic under noisy, malformed, or ill-formed input.  
- Meaning construction SHALL remain stable and auditable despite surface-form degradation.
