# 20.30 TS Functional Model

## Normative Requirements (Traceability IDs)
1. HLR-20.030-001: TS core execution SHALL be deterministic for identical input, MTP state, and seed.
2. HLR-20.030-002: Meaning representation and construction SHALL remain explicit, structured, and auditable via MTP-centered semantics.
3. HLR-20.030-003: TS architecture SHALL remain modular and inspectable across OB, RB, TB, and TS-core primitives.
4. HLR-20.030-004: Meaning construction SHALL combine path semantics, ΔH% contribution/missing-mass accounting, and TB interpretation deterministically.
5. HLR-20.030-005: Variability SHALL be isolated from TS core cognition and SHALL NOT introduce nondeterminism into OB/RB/TB/MTP logic.
6. HLR-20.030-006: Random-seed handling SHALL remain isolated, documented, reproducible, and deterministic for fixed input/state/seed tuples.
7. HLR-20.030-007: Primitive contracts for TP, MTP, OB, RB, TB, ΔH%, and TCU SHALL remain explicit and stable.
8. HLR-20.030-008: End-to-end pipeline stages (input, routing, splitting, OB processing, TB interpretation, merging, truth/done, response) SHALL be deterministic.
9. HLR-20.030-009: Splitting and merging SHALL be deterministic, auditable, and loss-accounted through ΔH% semantics.
10. HLR-20.030-010: Operational semantics and MTP update mapping SHALL remain explicit and reproducible.
11. HLR-20.030-011: TCU budgeting, degradation policy, and per-tick cost reporting SHALL be explicit, bounded, and deterministic.
12. HLR-20.030-012: Randomness subsystem requirements SHALL enforce isolation, reproducibility, and safety-gated change control.
13. HLR-20.030-013: Noisy or ill-formed input handling SHALL remain deterministic and SHALL preserve stable/auditable meaning construction.
14. HLR-20.030-014: TS architectural behavior SHALL remain interpretable and diagnostically visible across all lifecycle stages.

# **1. Architectural Principles**

## **1.1 Determinism**  
The TS core is fully deterministic.  
Same input + same MTP state → same output.

## **1.2 Structured Meaning**  
Meaning is represented explicitly in the Master Thought Packet (MTP).  
Meaning is **not** stored in high‑dimensional vectors.

## **1.3 Modularity**  
OBs, RBs, TB modules, and the TS core are separable, inspectable, and testable.

## **1.4 Interpretability**  
Every step of the reasoning process is visible:

- OB activations  
- RB routing  
- lane projections  
- ΔH% contributions  
- TB interpretations  
- MTP updates  
- truth evaluation  
- done evaluation  

## **1.5 Constructive Mapping**  
Meaning is constructed from:

- OB/RB path  
- ΔH% contributions  
- ΔH% missing mass  
- TB interpretations  

## **1.6 Optional Variability**  
Variability is introduced only intentionally:

- in the input  
- in TB interpretation  
- in the response generator  

Never inside the TS core.

## **1.7 Random Seed Boundary**
The TS core is fully deterministic.
Variability enters the system only through a random seed used by the response generator.
The seed SHALL be pseudo-random, documented, and isolated from the TS core.
Given the same input, same MTP state, and same seed, the TS SHALL produce the same output.

This boundary ensures:

- deterministic meaning construction
- controlled expressive variability
- reproducibility
- auditability
- stability under load

# **2. TS Primitives (Definitions)**

These are the fundamental units of TS computation.

## **2.1 TP — Thought Packet**  
A structured semantic object containing:

- propositions  
- stance  
- affect  
- intent  
- ΔH%  
- lineage  
- semantic tags  

## **2.2 MTP — Master Thought Packet**  
The global semantic state for the current thought.  
All lane TPs merge into the MTP.

## **2.3 OB — Observation Basin**  
A correlator operating closest to the geometric substrate.  
Extracts local meaning fragments and ΔH%.

## **2.4 RB — Relational Basin**  
Defines the topology of meaning extraction:

- which OBs fire  
- in what order  
- with what projections  

## **2.5 TB — Truth Basin**  
Modules that interpret meaning:

- TB‑logic  
- TB‑emotion  
- TB‑intent  
- TB‑stance  
- TB‑social  

## **2.6 ΔH% — Entropy Change **  
A normalized measure of how much evidence a lane TP contributes to a hypothesis.

## **2.7 TCU — Thought Compute Unit**  
A normalized unit of computational cost.  
Calibrated via the TCU kernel (Section 7).

# **3. Meaning Construction Pipeline**

## **3.1 Input**  
A prompt or prior MTP enters the system.

## **3.2 Routing**  
RBs determine:

- which OBs fire  
- the order of activation  
- the lane projections  

## **3.3 Splitting (Projection)**  
The TP is split into lane TPs.

Projection removes some semantics (e.g., S₂ for OB₁), producing **partial views**.

Each lane TP carries:

- visible semantics  
- suppressed semantics  
- ΔH% potential  
- local context  

## **3.4 OB Processing**  
Each OB:

- processes its lane TP  
- extracts local meaning fragments  
- computes ΔH%  
- returns structured evidence  

## **3.5 TB Interpretation**  
TB modules interpret:

- stance  
- affect  
- intent  
- logic  
- social position  

TB outputs are structured fields, not probabilities.

## **3.6 Merging**  
Lane TPs merge into the MTP.

Merging is deterministic:

- ΔH% contributions combined  
- missing ΔH% accounted for  
- TB interpretations integrated  
- semantic fields updated  

## **3.7 Truth and Completion**
TS evaluates:

- **H%** (entropy / uncertainty of the TP)
- **truth_hypotheses** (structured truth-evaluation fields)
- **done** (completion state) 

## **3.8 Response Generation**  
The response generator expresses the meaning in the MTP.  
Variability is introduced only here, and only via a pseudo-random seed.
The mapping from (MTP, seed) -> output is deterministic.
The seed SHALL NOT influence meaning construction.

# **4. Splitting and Merging (Formal Overview)**

## **4.1 Splitting**  
Splitting occurs when the TP is projected into multiple lanes.

### Properties:

- deterministic  
- rule‑based  
- explicit  
- lossless (ΔH% tracks what was removed)  

### Why it works:  
Meaning is structured, so we can explicitly choose:

- which fields are visible  
- which fields are suppressed  
- which semantics each OB receives  

---

## **4.2 Merging**  
Merging recombines lane TPs into the MTP.

### Properties:

- deterministic  
- rule‑based  
- auditable  
- field‑wise  

### Merging uses:

- ΔH% contributions  
- ΔH% missing mass  
- TB interpretations  
- prior MTP state  

### Why it works:  
The MTP is a **schema**, not a vector.  
Fields merge cleanly:

- max  
- sum  
- overwrite  
- weighted  
- rule‑based

The TS maps:

```
(path, ΔH%, TB-signals) → MTP update
```

This includes:

- meaning construction  
- truth hypothesis updates  
- stance/affect/intent updates  
- semantic tag propagation  
- lineage updates  

All deterministic.

# **7. TCU Allocation and Computational Budgeting**

## **7.1 TCU Definition**  
A TCU is a normalized unit of computational cost.  
It abstracts away hardware differences.

## **7.2 Calibration Kernel**  
To compute the machine’s TCU baseline:

```
for i in range(10^7):
    x = (x * 1.0000001) - (x / 1.0000003)
```

Measure runtime → convert to TCU baseline.

## **7.3 Per‑Subsystem TCU Budgets**

| Subsystem | Min | Typ | Max |
|-----------|-----|-----|-----|
| RB Routing | 1 | 2 | 4 |
| Splitting | 2 | 5 | 10 |
| OB Processing | 10 | 25 | 40 |
| TB Interpretation | 5 | 15 | 25 |
| Merging | 4 | 10 | 20 |
| Truth/Done | 2 | 5 | 10 |
| MTP Update | 1 | 3 | 5 |

## **7.4 Deterministic Degradation Rules**  
If a subsystem exceeds its budget:

- skip non‑critical TB modules  
- reduce semantic shading  
- postpone lineage updates  
- never break determinism  

## **7.5 Reporting Requirements**  
Each tick reports:

- TCU usage per subsystem  
- total TCU  
- ΔH% cost attribution  
- MTP update cost

# **8. TS Stability, Boundedness, and Overflow Control**

## Purpose
Defines global deterministic constraints that keep TS stable, bounded, and non-explosive across cycles. Establishes system-wide limits on lane growth, evidence size, ΔH% flow, provenance depth, and TCU consumption. Specifies deterministic overflow behavior and user-visible tagging rules when limits are exceeded.

## **8.1 Normative Requirements**

### **Global Boundedness**

1. HLR-20.030-201: TS SHALL define a deterministic global maximum for active lanes per cycle. RB, OB, and TB SHALL enforce this bound.
2. HLR-20.030-202: TS SHALL define a deterministic maximum number of OB activations per cycle to prevent uncontrolled fan-out.
3. HLR-20.030-203: TS SHALL define a deterministic maximum routing fan-out per TP to prevent exponential lane growth.
4. HLR-20.030-204: TS SHALL define maximum evidence payload sizes for OB outputs to prevent unbounded memory growth.
5. HLR-20.030-205: TS SHALL define maximum TB interpretation channel sizes and hypothesis counts.
6. HLR-20.030-206: TS SHALL define a deterministic maximum provenance chain depth to prevent runaway lineage accumulation.
7. HLR-20.030-207: TS SHALL define maximum ΔH% contribution per basin per cycle to prevent runaway entropy inflation.
8. HLR-20.030-208: TS SHALL define global per-cycle and per-basin TCU budgets (min/typ/max) that all basins must enforce.

### **Deterministic Overflow and Degradation**

9. HLR-20.030-209: If any global bound is exceeded, TS SHALL detect the overflow deterministically within the same cycle.
10. HLR-20.030-210: When overflow occurs, TS SHALL tag the TP with a deterministic overflow/degradation flag accessible to MB, GB, OuB, IB, and user-facing layers.
11. HLR-20.030-211: Overflow SHALL trigger deterministic, policy-bounded degradation behavior, including lane truncation, evidence truncation, ΔH% normalization, provenance compression, and routing fan-out reduction.
12. HLR-20.030-212: TS SHALL NOT silently drop lanes, evidence, or interpretation fields without deterministic tagging and logging.
13. HLR-20.030-213: Overflow SHALL NOT trigger probabilistic recovery, hallucinated reconstruction, or latent-state inference.

### **User-Visible Stability Reporting**

14. HLR-20.030-214: TS SHALL expose a deterministic, human-readable stability signal when overflow or degradation occurs.
15. HLR-20.030-215: TS SHALL include stability metadata in the MTP snapshot, including overflow type, affected basin, truncated fields, ΔH% normalization applied, and TCU overrun details.
16. HLR-20.030-216: All overflow events SHALL be logged in a deterministic, replayable format for verification and debugging.
17. HLR-20.030-217: TS SHALL define a canonical overflow field schema for TP/MTP stability tags including overflow_flag, overflow_type, overflow_source_basin, overflow_cycle, truncated_fields, ΔH%_normalization_applied, and tcu_overrun_amount.
18. HLR-20.030-218: Basin-level overflow/degradation telemetry in OB, RB, and TB SHALL conform to the canonical overflow field schema.

## **8.2 TS Stability Invariants**

- Boundedness invariant: TS SHALL remain within global deterministic bounds for lanes, evidence, ΔH%, provenance, and TCU.
- Deterministic degradation invariant: overflow SHALL always produce the same degradation outcome for identical effective state.
- Auditability invariant: all overflow and degradation events SHALL be attributable to explicit global bounds and deterministic rules.

## **8.3 Canonical Overflow Field Schema**

Overflow and degradation tagging SHALL use the following shared field names across TP, MTP, and basin telemetry:

- overflow_flag: boolean indicator that overflow/degradation occurred in the cycle.
- overflow_type: deterministic category of overflow event.
- overflow_source_basin: basin identifier that emitted or detected the overflow condition.
- overflow_cycle: cycle identifier or tick index for the overflow event.
- truncated_fields: deterministic list of fields that were truncated/compressed/dropped by policy.
- ΔH%_normalization_applied: record indicating whether and how ΔH% normalization was applied.
- tcu_overrun_amount: deterministic overrun amount relative to the configured TCU envelope.

## **8.4 Global Basin (GB) Supervisory Role and Interfaces**

GB is the global supervisory basin responsible for long-range semantic stability, drift/oscillation detection, and cross-cycle coherence.

19. HLR-20.030-219: GB SHALL act as a global supervisory basin for long-range semantic stability, drift/oscillation detection, and cross-cycle coherence.
20. HLR-20.030-220: GB SHALL read only TP lane-local semantic state and MPs (monitoring packets / TS health telemetry) for supervisory evaluation.
21. HLR-20.030-221: GB SHALL NOT read OB, RB, TB, IB, InB, or OuB internal state or MTP internal state directly.
22. HLR-20.030-222: GB SHALL operate within an explicit small TS cycle share (nominally 3-5%) and a bounded TCU envelope, and SHALL degrade/fallback deterministically when exceeded.
23. HLR-20.030-223: TS service requests that affect global behavior (including deeper inquiry, extended reasoning, and mode changes) SHALL route through GB for deterministic stability/drift evaluation.
24. HLR-20.030-224: GB MAY issue bounded deterministic supervisory flow-control actions (such as Stop, Slow, Dampen, Escalate, Safe-Mode) and these actions SHALL NOT mutate TP/MTP meaning-construction state.
25. HLR-20.030-225: GB SHALL expose a deterministic supervisory command interface for higher-level safety/supervisory controllers.
26. HLR-20.030-226: GB SHALL translate external supervisory commands into bounded TS-level supervisory actions consistent with the global stability envelope.
27. HLR-20.030-227: TS<->GB and external<->GB supervisory interfaces SHALL be bounded, deterministic, measurable, and constrainable by directionality, mechanism, timing, and rate/priority limits.
28. HLR-20.030-228: Detailed GB mode definitions, message schemas, priority models, and concrete rate limits SHALL be specified in 20.80 GB Requirements and traced to this subsection.

# **9. Randomness Requirements**

## **9.1 Random Seed Definition**
The TS uses a pseudo-random seed to select among valid expression-layer realizations.

## **9.2 Isolation Requirement**
The seed SHALL NOT affect OB/RB/TB processing, ΔH%, truth evaluation, or MTP updates.

## **9.3 Statistical Requirements**
The pseudo-random generator SHALL have:

- a documented minimum cycle length
- validated distribution properties
- stable behavior across hardware

## **9.4 Reproducibility**
Given the same seed, the TS SHALL produce identical outputs.

## **9.5 Safety Requirement**
Randomness-critical code SHALL NOT be modified without re-verification.

# **10. Deterministic Handling of Noisy or Ill-Formed Input**

- TS SHALL accept malformed, misspelled, or grammatically broken input and continue deterministic processing.
- InB SHALL deterministically map noisy surface forms into stable conceptual basins without probabilistic inference.
- OB activation SHALL be spelling-independent and concept-based for equivalent noisy variants.
- TB SHALL reconstruct intended structure deterministically even when syntax is degraded.
- RB SHALL stabilize meaning without hallucinating corrections or inventing content.
- ΔH% updates SHALL remain bounded and deterministic under noisy, malformed, or ill-formed input.
- Meaning construction SHALL remain stable and auditable despite surface-form degradation.
