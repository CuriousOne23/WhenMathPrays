# **20.40 OB Requirements (Revised, Classification‑Based)**  
**Document ID:** 20.40  
**Version:** 1.0 (Revised OB Classification Architecture)  
**Date:** 2026‑06‑18  
**Status:** Structural Rewrite — OB is now a *four‑layer classification system*  

---

# **Purpose (Informative)**  
The Object Basin (**OB**) is no longer a primitive.  
It is now a **four‑layer deterministic classification system**:

- **20.40.010 — SOB** (Structural OB)  
- **20.40.020 — SROB** (Structural Refinement OB)  
- **20.40.030 — CnOB** (Constraint OB)  
- **20.40.040 — SmOB** (Semantic OB)

OB’s role is to:

- classify TP structure  
- refine structure  
- extract constraints  
- extract pre‑semantic cues  
- emit residue fragments  
- produce TR‑input for the Thought Router  

OB **does not** perform semantic interpretation, stance assignment, truth evaluation, or routing arbitration.

This document defines:

- **parent‑level OB invariants**  
- **cross‑layer rules**  
- **XOR and geometric invariants**  
- **global OB responsibilities**  
- **legacy HLR preservation and mapping**  

All SHALL statements in this document correspond to **parent‑level HLRs** numbered:

- **HLR‑20.040‑001 through HLR‑20.040‑051** (legacy, preserved)  
- **HLR‑20.040‑052 and above** (new parent‑level invariants)

---

## OB Terminology Unification (Informative)

Historically, “OB” referred to a single primitive module.  
As of this revision, **OB is a classification system**, not a primitive.  
The OB classification consists of four ordered layers:

- **20.40.010 — SOB** (Structural OB)  
- **20.40.020 — SROB** (Structural Refinement OB)  
- **20.40.030 — CnOB** (Constraint OB)  
- **20.40.040 — SmOB** (Semantic OB)

### Generic Use of “OB” in Other TS Documents  
Any TS document that refers to **“OB”** without specifying a layer SHALL be interpreted as referring to the **OB classification system as a whole**, and the intended meaning SHALL be resolved as follows:

1. **If the document concerns structural pattern detection**,  
   “OB” refers to **SOB**.

2. **If the document concerns structural refinement or normalization**,  
   “OB” refers to **SROB**.

3. **If the document concerns constraint extraction or constraint metadata**,  
   “OB” refers to **CnOB**.

4. **If the document concerns pre‑semantic cue extraction or semantic‑adjacent residue**,  
   “OB” refers to **SmOB**.

5. **If the document concerns OB‑wide invariants, routing invariants, ΔH% behavior, determinism, or multi‑core execution**,  
   “OB” refers to the **entire OB classification system**.

### Backward Compatibility  
Because this interpretation rule is defined here in 20.40:

- **No other TS document requires modification**.  
- All existing references to “OB” remain valid.  
- The meaning of “OB” is now **contextually resolved** to the appropriate layer.

This rule is normative for all TS documents that reference OB.

# **Parent‑Level OB Architecture (Informative)**

OB classification produces:

- a structured TP  
- residue fragments  
- metadata fragments  
- TR‑input cues  
- ΔH% contributions  

Residue fragments across layers must satisfy:

- **addressability**  
- **XOR composability**  
- **geometric smoothness**  
- **monotonicity**  
- **curvature invariance**

The routing block computes:

$$
q = h_1 \oplus h_2 \oplus \dots \oplus h_n
$$

where each $h_i$ is a residue‑derived address fragment.

OB behavior SHALL be platform‑independent.

### **HLR‑20.040‑040 (Mapped to: 010‑019, 020‑019)**  
OB output SHALL NOT depend on TP allocation identity.

### HLR‑20.040‑064 — TP Message Contract for OB Classification
OB SHALL consume a deterministic lane‑local TP message whose required fields are resolved to the appropriate OB layer as follows: SOB consumes structural projection fields and syntactic markers; SROB consumes refined structural residue and canonicalization context; CnOB consumes constraint‑ready fields, missing‑slot and underspecification metadata; SmOB consumes constraint residue plus semantic‑adjacent cues and pre‑semantic anchors. Any TS document referring to “OB input” or “TP message to OB” SHALL be interpreted as referring to the appropriate layer‑specific TP view defined above, without requiring modification of those documents.

# **K. New Parent‑Level HLRs (Beyond 051)**

### **HLR‑20.040‑052 — XOR Address Invariant**  
OB SHALL ensure that all residue‑derived address fragments $h_i$ are XOR‑composable:

q = h_1 \oplus h_2 \oplus \dots \oplus h_n
$$

### **HLR‑20.040‑053 — Residue Addressability**

### **HLR‑20.040‑056 — Curvature Invariance**  
OB SHALL ensure residue curvature invariants are preserved across layers.

## L. Parent‑Level Address‑Space and XOR Invariants

### HLR‑20.040‑057 — Address Space Width  
OB SHALL use an $n$‑bit address space of size $2^n$, fixed for the entire TS architecture.

### HLR‑20.040‑058 — Collision Negligibility  
OB SHALL ensure that distinct residue fragments produce distinct address fragments with collision probability negligible relative to TS routing tolerances.

### HLR‑20.040‑059 — Uniform Distribution  
Residue‑derived address fragments SHALL behave as uniformly distributed values over the $2^n$ address space.

### HLR‑20.040‑060 — XOR Stability  
The OB address space SHALL support XOR composition such that:

$$
q = h_1 \oplus h_2 \oplus \dots \oplus h_k
$$

is deterministic, stable, and preserves uniformity across all OB layers.

### HLR‑20.040‑061 — Non‑Repeatability Under Refinement  
Residue refinement across OB layers SHALL NOT produce repeated address fragments unless the underlying residue content is identical.

### HLR‑20.040‑062 — Monotonicity Under Refinement  
Residue refinement across OB layers SHALL produce address fragments whose information content is monotonic with respect to the refinement order.

### HLR‑20.040‑063 — Geometric Smoothness of Address Trajectories  
The sequence of address fragments produced across OB layers SHALL satisfy geometric smoothness constraints defined in 20.40.
