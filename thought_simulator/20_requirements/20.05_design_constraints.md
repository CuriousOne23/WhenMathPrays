# 20.05 Design Constraints
### **Architectural Design Constraints **  
**Tier:** 20 — Exploratory / Architectural Requirements  
**Status:** Normative Policy (Non‑Testable)  
**Purpose:** Defines cross‑cutting architectural constraints that guide the creation, partitioning, and evolution of TS primitives and routing structures.  
**Scope:** Applies to all primitives (OB, RB, GB, TP), routing logic, and cognitive‑level processing.

---

# **1. Role of This Document**

This document establishes **architectural design constraints** that govern how primitives, routing structures, and TP‑level communication must be shaped.  
These constraints are **not HLRs** and **do not carry HLR IDs**.  
They serve as **policy boundaries** that inform and constrain the creation of canonical requirements in the 10‑series.

This document is **binding at the design level**, but **not directly testable**.

---

# **2. Primitive Simplicity**

### **2.1 Primitives must remain simple and narrowly scoped.**  
Each primitive must be understandable, inspectable, and limited in responsibility.

### **2.2 Complexity must not accumulate inside a single primitive.**  
If a primitive begins to require multi‑stage logic or broad branching, it must be decomposed.

### **2.3 Primitive definitions should remain stable within a major version.**  
Changes to the primitive set imply architectural re‑partitioning and may require a major version increment.

---

# **3. Primitive Function Separation**

### **3.1 Each primitive must have a single, well‑defined function.**  
- OBs detect cues and write deltas into TP.  
- RBs read TP fields and route to GBs.  
- GBs apply basin state and write interpretive bias into TP.  
- TP holds state and performs no logic.

### **3.2 No functional overlap is permitted.**  
Two primitives may not share responsibility for the same semantic domain.

### **3.3 Primitives must not directly depend on each other.**  
All cognitive communication must occur through the TP.

### **3.4 Primitive and Basin Definitions (Complete List)**

#### **Object Basin (OB)**
Local, stateless pattern-detector.  
Reads a fixed subset of TP fields and writes local deltas.  
Evaluates whether the *aggregate* of TPs satisfies its expected pattern.  
Records per-TP contributions.  
Emits global coherence cues.  
Never performs splitting, merging, or inference.

#### **Routing Basin (RB)**
Determines which OBs, GBs, or other primitives should activate next.  
Reads TP fields and applies routing rules.  
Does not modify TP content beyond routing metadata.  
Does not perform inference or branching.

#### **Governing Basin (GB)**
Maintains global interpretive context across ticks.  
Applies long-range constraints, frames, and contextual biases.  
May suppress or encourage branching.  
May trigger merges when context resolves ambiguity.  
Communicates with the TS for global control.

#### **Inquiry Basin (IB)**
Diagnostic primitive that activates when structure is missing or ambiguity is detected.  
Interprets OB cues.  
Decides whether branching is required.  
Performs TP splits.  
May propose new OBs or RBs.  
IBs are few and allowed to be computationally heavier.

#### **Truth Basin (TB)**
Evaluates truth, consistency, and verification.  
Resolves ambiguity by selecting the most plausible or validated branch.  
Performs merges when truth is determined.  
Does not detect patterns; operates on TP lineage and truth-fields.

#### **Input Basin (InB)**
Stabilizes and interprets raw external input.  
Writes a small, bounded set of TP fields.  
Does not perform detection, routing, or inference.  
Acts as the input-side attractor.

#### **Output Basin (OuB)**
Stabilizes and formats outgoing signal.  
Maps TP fields into external expression.  
Does not perform detection, inference, or branching.  
Acts as the output-side attractor.

#### **Monitoring Basin (MB)**
Read-only observer.  
Samples TP/GB/OB state without influencing cognition.  
Used for debugging, drift detection, what-if simulations, and shadow reasoning.  
May trigger controlled TP splits for simulation purposes.  
Must be bounded, optional, and non-intrusive.

#### **Thought Simulator (TS)**
The orchestrator.  
Enforces cost bounds, tick structure, and primitive sequencing.  
Records all split/merge events.  
Provides deterministic replay.  
May trigger splits or merges for simulation or debugging.

#### **ThoughtPoint (TP)**
The atomic unit of thought.  
Holds cognitive state, contributions, lineage, and deltas.  
Records split/merge ancestry.  
Carries all fields used by OB/RB/GB/IB/TB.  
Does not perform computation; it is the substrate.

---

# **4. TP Communication Rules**

### **4.1 TP is the sole channel for cognitive information flow.**  
Operators may not communicate cognitive content through any other mechanism.

### **4.2 TP must remain fixed‑shape within a major version.**  
No dynamic field creation, no unbounded growth, no implicit history.

### **4.3 TP updates must be small, additive, and independent.**  
Large or tightly coupled updates are discouraged.

### **4.4 Non‑cognitive orchestration may bypass TP.**  
Scheduler, logging, profiling, and simulation control may communicate out‑of‑band, provided they do not encode cognitive content.

---

# **5. Routing Sparsity**

### **5.1 Routing must remain sparse.**  
Only a small number of operators should fire per tick.

### **5.2 RB fan‑in per OB must remain bounded.**  
- Typical: handful to tens  
- Acceptable (with justification): low hundreds  
- Undesirable: thousands  
If an OB requires excessive RB connections, it should be decomposed.

### **5.3 RBs may only read a small, fixed subset of TP fields.**  
RBs must not scan the entire TP or perform global inference.

### **5.4 Routing must remain deterministic.**  
No stochastic selection or probabilistic routing.

---

# **6. GB and Basin Constraints**

### **6.1 GBs may not perform detection.**  
GBs must not read raw input or perform cue recognition.

### **6.2 GB updates should be constant‑time in practice.**  
GB logic should remain simple and bounded.

### **6.3 Basin state must remain small and bounded.**  
Basins must not accumulate unbounded history or dynamic structure.

---

# **7. Complexity Boundaries**

### **7.1 No global scans.**  
No component may iterate over all OBs, all RBs, all GBs, or the entire TP.

### **7.2 No dynamic module creation.**  
OB/RB/GB counts are fixed within a major version.

### **7.3 No attention‑like or autoregressive operations.**  
TS must maintain bounded‑cost updates per tick.

### **7.4 Complexity must emerge from composition, not primitive inflation.**  
Intelligence arises from many simple parts, not from complex primitives.

---

# **8. Governance**

### **8.1 This document informs 10‑series HLR creation.**  
HLRs must not contradict these constraints.

### **8.2 Changes to this document require architectural review.**  
Because these constraints shape the entire system, modifications must be deliberate and justified.

### **8.3 Discovery of a new primitive class is an architectural event.**  
Adding a new primitive type requires re‑partitioning and may trigger a major version increment.

# **9. TP Coherence, Splitting, and Merging**

This section defines the architectural rules governing how ThoughtPoints (TPs) participate in coherence evaluation, how OBs record partial contributions, and how TP splitting and merging are orchestrated by higher-level primitives. These rules ensure deterministic replay, bounded cost, and clean separation of responsibilities across primitives.

## **9.1 OB Multi-TP Coherence Model**

### **9.1.1 OBs evaluate patterns, not individual TPs**
An OB's role is to determine whether the *aggregate* of all TPs entering it during a tick satisfies its expected pattern. OBs do not reason about ambiguity, do not compare TPs, and do not perform branching or merging logic.

### **9.1.2 Per-TP contribution recording**
For each TP entering an OB, the OB must record into the TP:

- the percentage of the expected pattern that this TP satisfied
- the specific fields matched
- the specific fields missing
- any local confidence deltas

Each TP independently records its own contribution.  
OBs do not store per-TP state.

### **9.1.3 OB does not set per-TP incoherence flags**
A TP that contributes only partial coherence is not considered incorrect.  
OBs must not mark individual TPs as incoherent or insufficient.

### **9.1.4 OB emits a global coherence cue**
After processing all TPs for the tick, the OB evaluates whether the *sum* of contributions meets its expected pattern.

- If the aggregate contribution meets or exceeds expectations, **no incoherence cue is emitted**.
- If the aggregate contribution is insufficient, the OB emits a **global incoherence cue** for that OB event.

This cue is global, not per-TP.

### **9.1.5 OB remains dumb, local, and stateless**
OBs must not:

- compare TPs
- aggregate beyond a simple sum
- evaluate ambiguity
- decide branching or merging
- perform inference
- coordinate hypotheses
- inspect TP structure beyond their fixed field set

OBs only detect patterns and write local deltas.

## **9.2 TP Splitting and Merging - Architectural Rules**

### **9.2.1 Splitting and merging are inference operations**
TP splitting and merging are not detection operations and must not occur inside OBs.  
They are inference-level events handled by:

- IB (Inquiry Basin)
- TB (Truth Basin)
- GB (Governing Basin)
- TS (the simulator itself)
- MB (Monitoring Basin, in controlled what-if modes)

OBs may only emit cues that higher-level primitives interpret.

### **9.2.2 When splitting is allowed**
A TP may be split only when triggered by:

- **IB**, due to unresolved ambiguity or missing structure
- **TB**, due to truth uncertainty
- **GB**, due to context-dependent divergence
- **TS**, for simulation, debugging, or deterministic replay
- **MB**, for controlled shadow reasoning

No other primitive may initiate a split.

### **9.2.3 Mechanics of a TP split**
A TP split must be:

- constant-time
- fixed-shape
- fully recorded
- non-destructive

Each split produces:

- a new TP ID
- a parent TP ID
- a split reason
- a split trigger
- a branch label (e.g., A, B, C)
- a tagged state increment

Only the minimal divergent fields may differ between branches.

### **9.2.4 When merging is allowed**
A merge is allowed only when:

- **TB** resolves truth
- **GB** resolves context
- **IB** completes inquiry
- **TS** explicitly requests a merge

No other primitive may initiate a merge.

### **9.2.5 Mechanics of a TP merge**
A TP merge must be:

- deterministic
- fully recorded
- bounded
- non-lossy unless explicitly allowed

A merge produces:

- a new TP ID
- a list of parent TP IDs
- a merge reason
- a merge trigger
- a merge strategy (e.g., winner-take-all, weighted, field-wise)
- a tagged state increment

Merges must not overwrite existing TPs.

## **9.3 Cost Model and Constraints**

### **9.3.1 OBs must not perform splitting or merging**
OBs may process multiple TPs per tick but must not directly split or merge TPs.  
OBs may only emit branching cues.

### **9.3.2 IBs are few and allowed to be heavier**
IBs are diagnostic primitives.  
Their population must remain small.  
If many IBs fire, OB design is inadequate.  
IBs may perform more expensive split/merge orchestration without harming perceived performance.

### **9.3.3 OB-phase branching is prohibited**
Allowing OBs to split or merge TPs would cause combinatorial explosion.  
All branching decisions must be escalated to IB/TB/GB/TS.

### **9.3.4 Bounded branching and merging**
The simulator must enforce:

- max splits per tick
- max merges per tick
- max active branches
- max divergent fields per split
- max parents per merge
- max branching depth

These bounds ensure predictable cost and deterministic replay.

## **9.4 Summary of Responsibilities**

### **OB**

- Detects patterns
- Writes per-TP contributions
- Emits global coherence cues
- Does not reason, split, or merge

### **IB**

- Interprets OB cues
- Decides branching
- Performs splits
- Manages hypotheses

### **TB**

- Evaluates truth
- Resolves ambiguity
- Performs merges

### **GB**

- Applies global context
- Suppresses or encourages branching
- Resolves contextual ambiguity

### **TS**

- Enforces cost bounds
- Records all split/merge events
- Orchestrates the cognitive cycle

### **TP**

- Records its own contributions
- Records split/merge lineage
- Holds all cognitive state
