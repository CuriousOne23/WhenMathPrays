# 20.70 MB Requirements

## Purpose
Defines deterministic Monitoring Basin (MB) behavior for non-intrusive diagnostics, drift observation, stability reporting, and bounded what-if supervision in the TS pipeline.
MB provides structured observability and advisory monitoring outputs without mutating TS core meaning-construction state.

## Normative Requirements
1. HLR-20.070-001: MB SHALL remain non-intrusive to core cognition by default.
2. HLR-20.070-002: MB SHALL operate over bounded, explicit observability inputs only and SHALL NOT rely on hidden or latent state.
3. HLR-20.070-003: MB SHALL NOT directly mutate TP, MTP, OB, RB, or TB core meaning-construction state.
4. HLR-20.070-004: MB diagnostics and drift-observation outputs SHALL be deterministic for identical effective state and inputs.
5. HLR-20.070-005: MB SHALL provide deterministic diagnostics over basin activations, routing behavior, interpretation outputs, and stability signals.
6. HLR-20.070-006: MB SHALL support deterministic drift observation over semantically relevant state deltas across cycles.
7. HLR-20.070-007: Any MB-triggered what-if split or probe action SHALL be explicitly flagged, policy-gated, and logged.
8. HLR-20.070-008: MB what-if or supervisory controls SHALL be bounded, optional, and non-authoritative relative to core basin contracts.
9. HLR-20.070-009: MB SHALL preserve lineage/provenance references for all diagnostics, recommendations, and what-if artifacts.
10. HLR-20.070-010: MB SHALL deterministically handle noisy or ill-formed observational signals without probabilistic inference or hallucinated reconstruction.
11. HLR-20.070-011: MB outputs SHALL be deterministic and reproducible for identical observability inputs, MTP context, and seed-bounded system state.
12. HLR-20.070-012: MB SHALL expose deterministic test interfaces with explicit inputs, expected outputs, invariants, and failure modes.
13. HLR-20.070-013: MB SHALL expose test hooks sufficient for unit, integration, system, and property-based verification of diagnostics behavior.
14. HLR-20.070-014: MB lifecycle transitions (entry, running, completion/exit) SHALL be explicitly logged and observable.
15. HLR-20.070-015: MB SHALL expose observability outputs consumable by GB, IB, OuB, and user-facing diagnostics without mutating MB core semantics.
16. HLR-20.070-016: MB telemetry SHALL support deterministic replay, audit, and comparative diagnostics across runs.
17. HLR-20.070-017: MB SHALL define measurable TCU budgets (min/typ/max) and per-tick TCU reporting fields.
18. HLR-20.070-018: MB overload/degradation behavior SHALL be deterministic, policy-bounded, and auditable.
19. HLR-20.070-019: MB behavior SHALL be platform-independent and SHALL NOT depend on hardware-, OS-, or runtime-specific nondeterministic effects.
20. HLR-20.070-020: MB contracts SHALL prioritize top-down thought-processing observability semantics over implementation-specific mechanics.
21. HLR-20.070-021: MB SHALL accept a deterministic input object containing basin telemetry, lane identifiers, lineage/provenance references, MTP snapshot fields, and stability metadata required for diagnostics.
22. HLR-20.070-022: MB SHALL emit a deterministic output object containing diagnostics summaries, drift indicators, advisory recommendations, what-if flags, execution diagnostics, and observability/telemetry fields required by GB, IB, OuB, and user-facing layers.
23. HLR-20.070-023: MB SHALL function as a deterministic monitoring basin that provides advisory diagnostics and SHALL NOT bypass or replace RB routing, OB evidence extraction, or TB interpretation authority.
24. HLR-20.070-024: MB overflow/degradation telemetry SHALL conform to the canonical overflow field schema defined in 20.30 section 8.3, including overflow_flag, overflow_type, overflow_source_basin, overflow_cycle, truncated_fields, ΔH%_normalization_applied, and tcu_overrun_amount.
25. HLR-20.070-025: MB SHALL expose deterministic, human-readable stability and degradation reporting derived from canonical overflow fields for user-facing and verification workflows.
26. HLR-20.070-026: MB intervention recommendations under overflow conditions SHALL be deterministic, explicitly non-binding, and SHALL NOT silently alter core basin behavior without explicit policy-authorized application.
27. HLR-20.070-027: MB SHALL support deterministic telemetry visibility modes (low, medium, high, full) that control MB sampling density and diagnostics detail without mutating emitted basin telemetry.
28. HLR-20.070-028: When selected MB visibility mode or runtime telemetry load materially increases MB TCU cost, MB SHALL emit deterministic, human-readable user notification including active visibility mode and cost impact summary.
29. HLR-20.070-029: If MB exceeds its configured TCU envelope, MB SHALL apply deterministic fallback behavior by reducing telemetry sampling/detail according to explicit policy while preserving non-intrusive operation.
30. HLR-20.070-030: MB SHALL enforce a deterministic, bounded drift-history depth with explicit retention/eviction policy to prevent unbounded memory growth.
31. HLR-20.070-031: MB visibility controls SHALL constrain MB telemetry consumption only; TP and MTP visibility for deterministic diagnostics and audit SHALL remain explicit and safe under core TS contracts.
32. HLR-20.070-032: MB diagnostic flush behavior SHALL use deterministic flush epochs and deterministic output ordering.
33. HLR-20.070-033: MB diagnostics inclusion/exclusion behavior SHALL remain deterministic under fixed policy and signature state.
34. HLR-20.070-034: MB diagnostic artifact retention and purge behavior SHALL follow deterministic retention windows and deterministic purge cadence.
35. HLR-20.070-035: MB SHALL apply deterministic empty-artifact behavior for zero-event monitoring windows under explicit policy.
36. HLR-20.070-036: MB diagnostic/export profiles SHALL be versioned and SHALL be included in execution-signature-bound deterministic profile state.

## MB Invariants
- Determinism invariant: identical effective input state yields identical MB diagnostics, drift indicators, and advisory outputs.
- Non-intrusion invariant: MB SHALL not directly mutate TS core meaning-construction state.
- Auditability invariant: every MB diagnostic or recommendation SHALL be attributable to explicit deterministic inputs, policies, and cycle context.
