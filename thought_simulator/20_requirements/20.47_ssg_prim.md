# 20.47 Structural Signature Generator (SSG-prm)

**Document ID:** 20.47  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 2026-06-20  
**Status:** Draft v0.1  
**Predecessor:** 20.40.040 SmOB-prm  
**Successor:** 20.50 RB-prm  

---

## 1. Purpose

The Structural Signature Generator (SSG-prm) occupies the pipeline position
immediately after SmOB-prm and before RB-prm in Pipeline A. Its role is to
compress the full four-layer OB residue chain into a single deterministic
structural signature that RB-prm consumes for relational routing — without
RB-prm needing to re-read individual OB layer outputs.

The signature encodes *which structural evidence is present*, not *what it
means*. SSG-prm performs no interpretation, inference, or truth evaluation of
any kind.

---

## 2. Scope

SSG-prm is a Pipeline A primitive. It reads the TP after SmOB-prm has
populated pre-semantic residue fields and writes exactly four fields —
`tp.ssg_signature`, `tp.ssg_layer_bitmap`, `tp.ssg_reason_code`, and
`tp.ssg_status` — before handing control to RB-prm.

**In scope:**
- Residue address extraction from all four OB layers (SOB, SROB, CnOB, SmOB)
- Deterministic signature composition via bitwise XOR and deterministic hash
- Layer bitmap annotation recording which OB layers contributed non-zero residue
- Status annotation and audit append to `exec_trace`

**Out of scope:**
- Semantic interpretation of any residue field
- Routing decisions (owned by RB-prm, 20.50)
- Truth evaluation (owned by TB, 20.60)
- Candidate generation (owned by CEx / ISc, 20.44)
- TP correction or merge (owned by TPU-prm, 20.105)
- ΔH% computation or accumulation (owned by OB layers and RBU-prm)

---

## 3. Pipeline Position

Single-core ordering:

```
SmOB-prm → SSG-prm → RB-prm
```

Multi-core ordering (after CTP-prm merge):

```
{ SOB-prm ∥ SROB-prm ∥ CnOB-prm ∥ SmOB-prm } → CTP-prm → SSG-prm → RB-prm
```

SSG-prm is the terminal stage of the OB residue chain. In both configurations
it receives the TP after all four OB layers have contributed their residue and
before RB-prm performs relational routing. In multi-core configurations,
CTP-prm merges parallel OB outputs into a single TP snapshot first; SSG-prm
runs on that merged snapshot.

---

## 4. Functional Requirements

### 4.1 Input

**[HLR-20.47-001]** SSG-prm SHALL accept the SmOB-prm residue envelope, as
present in the TP after CTP-prm merge (or directly after SmOB-prm in
single-core), as its sole structural input. No global state, CIL, CE, or
external data source is accessed.

### 4.2 Signature Computation

**[HLR-20.47-002]** SSG-prm SHALL compute a deterministic structural signature vector $\sigma$ by extracting and normalizing a fixed set of structural invariants from the SmOB structural graph (and, where explicitly specified, residue fields from upstream OB layers), without using XOR, hashing, or any stochastic operation. The invariants SHALL include, at minimum, measures of arc pattern statistics, binding depth, residue entropy, curvature magnitude, and structural motif frequencies. SSG-prm SHALL map these invariants into a fixed-length, ordered real-valued vector $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}^d$, where $d$ is a versioned, implementation-stable dimensionality. The mapping from SmOB (and selected residues) to $\sigma$ SHALL be deterministic, seed-free, and replay-stable under identical OB outputs, and SHALL preserve structural similarity as geometric proximity (e.g., cosine similarity) in the resulting vector space.

### 4.3 Output Fields

**[HLR-20.47-003]** SSG-prm SHALL write the following three content fields into
the TP before yielding to RB-prm:

| Field | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `tp.ssg_signature` | fixed-width hash | The computed $\sigma$ value |
| `tp.ssg_layer_bitmap` | 4-bit mask | Records which OB layers contributed non-zero residue |
| `tp.ssg_reason_code` | enum | Classifies the structural character of $\sigma$ (see §4.5) |

**[HLR-20.47-004]** SSG-prm SHALL write all three content fields into the TP
before RB-prm is invoked. RB-prm reads `tp.ssg_signature` as its primary
structural routing key.

### 4.4 Layer Bitmap

The layer bitmap encodes residue presence across the four OB layers. Bit
positions are assigned in pipeline order where $L_0 = \text{SOB}$,
$L_1 = \text{SROB}$, $L_2 = \text{CnOB}$, $L_3 = \text{SmOB}$:

$$
\text{bitmap} = \sum_{i=0}^{3} b_i \cdot 2^i,
\qquad
b_i = \begin{cases} 1 & r_{L_i} \neq \mathbf{0} \\ 0 & r_{L_i} = \mathbf{0} \end{cases}
$$

A bitmap of `0b1111` indicates all four layers contributed non-zero residue.
A bitmap of `0b0000` indicates all layers produced zero residue and
$\sigma = H(\mathbf{0})$.

**[HLR-20.47-007]** SSG-prm SHALL produce a valid, non-null `tp.ssg_signature`
for every TP, including TPs where one or more OB layers produced zero residue.
Zero residue contributes the zero vector $\mathbf{0}$ to the XOR composition.

### 4.5 Reason Code

The `tp.ssg_reason_code` classifies the structural character of $\sigma$:

| Code | Condition | Bitmap |
|---|---|---|
| `FULL` | All four OB layers contributed non-zero residue | `0b1111` |
| `PARTIAL` | One or more OB layers contributed zero or substituted residue | `0b0001`–`0b1110` |
| `EMPTY` | All OB layers produced zero residue; $\sigma = H(\mathbf{0})$ | `0b0000` |

### 4.6 Status Field

**[HLR-20.47-015]** SSG-prm SHALL expose
`tp.ssg_status` ∈ `{OK, MISSING_INPUT, COLLISION_DETECTED, PARTIAL}` on every
invocation, including failure cases.

### 4.7 Audit

**[HLR-20.47-016]** SSG-prm SHALL append an `ssg_ref` record to `exec_trace`
on every successful invocation. The `ssg_ref` record contains: `commit_id`,
$\sigma$, `bitmap`, `reason_code`, and TCU cost.

---

## 5. Invariants

**[HLR-20.47-005]** SSG-prm SHALL NOT perform semantic interpretation, truth
evaluation, or candidate generation. Residue fields are consumed as opaque
fixed-width vectors; their semantic content is not examined.

**[HLR-20.47-006]** SSG-prm SHALL NOT modify any TP field other than
`tp.ssg_signature`, `tp.ssg_layer_bitmap`, `tp.ssg_reason_code`, and
`tp.ssg_status`. All upstream OB residue fields, semantic fields, truth fields,
and routing fields remain untouched.

**[HLR-20.47-008]** SSG-prm SHALL be invoked exactly once per TP per OB-chain
cycle.

**[HLR-20.47-009]** SSG-prm SHALL NOT be re-invoked for the same TP instance
without a new CTP-prm merge producing fresh SmOB-prm output.

**[HLR-20.47-010]** SSG-prm SHALL produce identical `tp.ssg_signature` output
for identical residue inputs across all invocations, on all cores, and across
replay cycles. The hash function $H$ is deterministic and receives no seed,
clock, or environmental input.

**[HLR-20.47-011]** SSG-prm SHALL complete within one TCU of the preceding
SmOB-prm (single-core) or CTP-prm (multi-core) and before RB-prm reads
`tp.ssg_signature`.

---

## 6. Failure Modes

**[HLR-20.47-012]** If SmOB-prm output is absent or the CTP-prm TP snapshot is
structurally invalid (missing required envelope fields), SSG-prm SHALL set
`tp.ssg_status = MISSING_INPUT`, SHALL NOT write `tp.ssg_signature`, and SHALL
escalate to MB-gov for audit. The TP is not forwarded to RB-prm under this
condition.

**[HLR-20.47-013]** If any OB-layer residue address is null (structurally
absent, not merely zero-valued), SSG-prm SHALL substitute the zero vector
$\mathbf{0}$ for that layer's contribution, clear the corresponding bit in
`tp.ssg_layer_bitmap`, and set `tp.ssg_reason_code = PARTIAL`. Processing
continues normally.

**[HLR-20.47-014]** If `tp.ssg_signature` collides with a previously committed
signature within the same processing cycle, SSG-prm SHALL set
`tp.ssg_status = COLLISION_DETECTED` and SHALL escalate the collision event to
MB-gov for audit. Processing continues; RB-prm receives the TP with the
collision status flag set.

## 7. Non-Normative Notes

> **Note 1 — Why XOR composition?**  
> XOR over fixed-width vectors is commutative and associative, making the
> composition order-independent. When OB layers execute in parallel
> (multi-core), the order in which residue addresses are combined must not
> affect $\sigma$. XOR satisfies this constraint without requiring a sort step.

> **Note 2 — Why hash after XOR?**  
> The raw XOR value $\bigoplus_{l} r_l$ can collide across structurally
> distinct inputs when two layer residues partially cancel. Passing the result
> through $H$ distributes the address space more uniformly and makes collision
> detection by HLR-20.47-014 meaningful.

> **Note 3 — Relationship to ΔH%**  
> The layer bitmap in `tp.ssg_layer_bitmap` is available to downstream
> components as evidence of residue completeness. A bitmap of `0b0111` (SmOB
> contributed zero residue) is consistent with a ΔH% increase in the next
> cycle. However, SSG-prm does not compute or modify ΔH%. That accounting
> belongs to the OB layers and RBU-prm.

> **Note 4 — SSG-prm is not a semantic filter**  
> RB-prm uses `tp.ssg_signature` as a structural routing key, not a semantic
> descriptor. Two TPs with identical structural patterns but different semantic
> content will produce the same $\sigma$. Routing correctness depends on RB-prm
> combining the signature with downstream semantic fields — `tp.ssg_signature`
> alone does not determine routing outcome.

> **Note 5 — EMPTY is a valid outcome**  
> A reason code of `EMPTY` ($\sigma = H(\mathbf{0})$, bitmap `0b0000`) is
> architecturally valid. It signals that no structural evidence was extractable
> from the current TP. RB-prm is responsible for handling this case via its own
> routing rules; SSG-prm does not escalate on `EMPTY`.

> **Note 6 — Collision rarity**  
> Intra-cycle collisions (HLR-20.47-014) are expected to be rare given the
> address space of $H$. The escalation to MB-gov is an audit trigger, not a
> processing halt. The architecture treats collision as a diagnostic signal
> requiring investigation, not a fatal error requiring cycle termination.
