# 20.47 Structural Signature Generator (SSG-prm)

**Document ID:** 20.47  
**Version:** 0.2  
**Date:** 2026-06-20  
**Status:** Draft v0.2  
**Predecessor:** 20.40.040 SmOB-prm  
**Successor:** 20.50 RB-prm  
**Replaces:** v0.1 (XOR/hash model — withdrawn)

---

## 1. Purpose

The Structural Signature Generator (SSG-prm) occupies the pipeline position after SmOB-prm and before RB-prm in Pipeline A. Its role is to map the discrete SmOB structural graph into a fixed-length real-valued vector in $\mathbb{R}^d$ by extracting and normalizing a canonical set of structural invariants — arc patterns, binding depth, residue entropy, curvature, and
motif frequencies.

This vector, `tp.ssg_signature`, acts as a coordinate chart on the structural manifold: structurally similar graphs land geometrically close in $\mathbb{R}^d$, enabling RB-prm to perform proximity-aware relational routing without re-reading individual OB layer outputs.

SSG-prm encodes *which structural evidence is present and how it is arranged*, not *what it means*. SSG-prm performs no interpretation, inference, or truth evaluation of any kind.

---

## 2. Scope

SSG-prm is a Pipeline A primitive. It reads the TP after SmOB-prm has populated the structural graph fields and writes exactly four fields — `tp.ssg_signature`, `tp.ssg_layer_bitmap`, `tp.ssg_reason_code`, and `tp.ssg_status` — before handing control to RB-prm.

**In scope:**
- Structural-invariant extraction from the SmOB structural graph
  (arc patterns, binding depth, residue entropy, curvature, motif frequencies)
- Feature vector assembly and L2 normalization into $\mathbb{R}^d$
- Layer bitmap annotation recording which OB layers contributed
  non-zero structural invariants
- Status annotation and audit append to `exec_trace`

**Out of scope:**
- Semantic interpretation of any structural or residue field
- Routing decisions (owned by RB-prm, 20.50)
- Truth evaluation (owned by TB, 20.60)
- Candidate generation (owned by CEx / ISc, 20.44)
- TP correction or merge (owned by TPU-prm, 20.105)
- ΔH% computation or accumulation (owned by OB layers and RBU-prm)
- Any hash function, bitwise operation, or address-space composition

SSG‑prm writes exclusively to $TP.process.ssg\_{signature}$, $TP.process.ssg\_{{layer}\_{bitmap}}$, $TP.process.ssg\_{{reason}\_{code}}$, and $TP.process.ssg\_{status}. SSG‑prm SHALL NOT modify TP.semantic, TP.metadata, TP.context, or TP.intake.

---

## 3. Pipeline Position

Single-core ordering:

```
SmOB-prm → SSG-prm → RB-prm
```

Multi-core ordering (after CTP-prm merge):

```
{ SOB-prm ∥ SROB-prm ∥ CnOB-prm ∥ SmOB-prm } → SSG-prm → RB-prm
```

SSG-prm is the terminal stage of the OB residue chain. In both configurations it receives the TP after all four OB layers have contributed their structural graph content and before RB-prm performs relational routing. In multi-core configurations, OB layers execute in parallel but produce a single TP; SSG-prm runs directly after SmOB-prm.

---

## 4. Functional Requirements

### 4.1 Input

**[HLR-20.47-001]** SSG-prm SHALL accept the SmOB-prm structural graph, as present in the TP after SmOB-prm, as its sole structural input.. No global state, CIL, CE, or external data source is accessed.

The SmOB structural graph $G = (V, E, \lambda)$ is a labelled directed graph where $V$ is the set of residue nodes, $E$ is the set of directed arcs, and $\lambda : V \cup E \to \mathcal{L}$ assigns structural labels.

### 4.2 Signature Computation

**[HLR-20.47-002]** SSG-prm SHALL compute `tp.ssg_signature` as a deterministic, seed-free, fixed-length real-valued vector $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}^d$ by applying the structural-invariant mapping $\varphi$ to the SmOB structural graph $G$ and L2-normalizing the result:

$$
\sigma = \frac{\varphi(G)}{\lVert \varphi(G) \rVert_2}
$$

where $\varphi : G \to \mathbb{R}^d$ extracts the $d$ named structural invariants defined in §4.2.1, and the denominator is the Euclidean norm of the raw feature vector. When $\lVert \varphi(G) \rVert_2 = 0$ (no structural invariants present), $\sigma = \mathbf{0} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and `tp.ssg_reason_code` is set to `EMPTY` (see §4.5).

The mapping $\varphi$ receives no random seed, clock value, process ID, or other environmental input. The same $G$ produces the same $\varphi(G)$ on every invocation.

#### 4.2.1 Structural Invariant Families

The $d$-dimensional feature vector $\varphi(G)$ is assembled from five named invariant families, concatenated in the order below. The dimensionality contributed by each family is fixed at design time and recorded in the SSG-prm configuration manifest.

| Index | Family | Description |
|---|---|---|
| $f_1$ | Arc patterns | Normalized frequency distribution over arc-type labels in $E$ |
| $f_2$ | Binding depth | Maximum and mean depth of directed binding chains in $G$ |
| $f_3$ | Residue entropy | Shannon entropy over the residue-address distribution of $V$ |
| $f_4$ | Curvature | Graph-theoretic curvature (cycle density and clustering coefficient of $G$) |
| $f_5$ | Motif frequencies | Normalized frequency counts of canonical structural subgraph motifs |

Formally:

$$
\varphi(G) = \bigl[ f_1(G) \ \|\  f_2(G) \ \|\  f_3(G) \ \|\  f_4(G) \ \|\  f_5(G) \bigr]
\in \mathbb{R}^d
$$

where $\|$ denotes vector concatenation and each $f_k(G)$ is computed deterministically from $G$ alone.

### 4.3 Output Fields

**[HLR-20.47-003]** SSG-prm SHALL write the following three content fields into
the TP before yielding to RB-prm:

| Field | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `tp.ssg_signature` | `float[d]` — fixed-length vector in $\mathbb{R}^d$ | The L2-normalized structural-invariant vector $\sigma$ |
| `tp.ssg_layer_bitmap` | 4-bit mask | Records which OB layers contributed non-zero structural invariants (see §4.4) |
| `tp.ssg_reason_code` | enum | Classifies the structural-invariant completeness of $\sigma$ (see §4.5) |

**[HLR-20.47-004]** SSG‑prm SHALL write all SSG fields into TP.process before RB‑prm is invoked. RB-prm reads `tp.ssg_signature` as its primary structural routing key.

### 4.4 Layer Bitmap

The layer bitmap encodes which OB layers contributed at least one non-zero structural invariant to the feature vector $\varphi(G)$. Bit positions are assigned in pipeline order: $L_0 = \text{SOB}$, $L_1 = \text{SROB}$, $L_2 = \text{CnOB}$, $L_3 = \text{SmOB}$:

$$
\text{bitmap} = \sum_{i=0}^{3} b_i \cdot 2^i,
\qquad
b_i = \begin{cases} 1 & \varphi_i(G_{L_i}) \neq \mathbf{0} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
$$

where $\varphi_i(G_{L_i})$ denotes the structural-invariant sub-vector extracted from the residue graph contributed by layer $L_i$.

A bitmap of `0b1111` indicates all four layers contributed structural invariants. A bitmap of `0b0000` indicates no layer contributed non-zero structural invariants, consistent with `ssg_reason_code = EMPTY`.

**[HLR-20.47-007]** SSG-prm SHALL produce a valid, non-null `tp.ssg_signature` for every TP, including TPs where one or more OB layers produced no structural invariants. When no structural invariants are extractable, `tp.ssg_signature` SHALL be the zero vector $\mathbf{0} \in \mathbb{R}^d$.

### 4.5 Reason Code

The `tp.ssg_reason_code` classifies the structural-invariant completeness of
the signature:

| Code | Condition | Bitmap |
|---|---|---|
| `FULL` | All four OB layers contributed non-zero structural invariants | `0b1111` |
| `PARTIAL` | One or more OB layers contributed zero structural invariants; absent layers substituted with zero contribution | `0b0001`–`0b1110` |
| `EMPTY` | No OB layer contributed any structural invariant; $\sigma = \mathbf{0}$ | `0b0000` |

### 4.6 Status Field

**[HLR-20.47-015]** SSG-prm SHALL expose
`tp.ssg_status` ∈ `{OK, MISSING_INPUT, DEGENERATE, PARTIAL}` on every invocation, including failure cases.

### 4.7 Geometric Proximity

Two structural signatures $\sigma_1, \sigma_2 \in \mathbb{R}^d$ are compared by cosine similarity:

$$
\text{sim}(\sigma_1, \sigma_2)
= \frac{\sigma_1 \cdot \sigma_2}{\lVert \sigma_1 \rVert_2 \cdot \lVert \sigma_2 \rVert_2}
\in [-1,\, 1]
$$

RB-prm uses this similarity measure for proximity-aware relational routing. SSG-prm does not compute similarity; it produces the vectors that make similarity computation possible. The cosine measure is defined over non-zero vectors; the `EMPTY` case ($\sigma = \mathbf{0}$) is handled by RB-prm via its own routing rules.

### 4.8 Audit

**[HLR-20.47-016]** SSG-prm SHALL append an `ssg_ref` record to `exec_trace` on every successful invocation. The `ssg_ref` record contains: `commit_id`, $\sigma$ (or `ZERO` if `EMPTY`), `bitmap`, `reason_code`, and TCU cost.

## 5. Invariants

**[HLR-20.47-005]** SSG-prm SHALL NOT perform semantic interpretation, truth evaluation, or candidate generation. The structural graph $G$ is consumed as a topological object; the semantic content of its node and arc labels is not examined.

**[HLR-20.47-006]** SSG‑prm SHALL NOT modify TP.semantic, TP.process, TP.metadata, TP.context, or TP.intake. SSG‑prm SHALL write only to the fields:
tp.ssg_signature, tp.ssg_layer_bitmap, tp.ssg_reason_code, and tp.ssg_status.

**[HLR-20.47-008]** SSG-prm SHALL be invoked exactly once per TP per OB-chain
cycle.

**[HLR-20.47-009]** SSG-prm SHALL NOT be re-invoked for the same TP instance without new SmOB-prm output.

**[HLR-20.47-010]** SSG-prm SHALL produce identical `tp.ssg_signature` output for identical structural-graph inputs across all invocations, on all cores, and across replay cycles. The mapping $\varphi$ is deterministic and receives no seed, clock, process ID, or environmental input.

**[HLR-20.47-011]** SSG-prm SHALL complete within one TCU of the preceding SmOB-prm and before RB-prm reads `tp.ssg_signature`.

---

## 6. Failure Modes

**[HLR-20.47-012]** If SmOB-prm output is absent or the TP snapshot is structurally invalid (missing required structural graph fields), SSG-prm SHALL set `tp.ssg_status = MISSING_INPUT`, SHALL NOT write `tp.ssg_signature`, and SHALL escalate to MB-gov for audit. The TP is not forwarded to RB-prm under this condition.

**[HLR-20.47-013]** If any OB-layer residue graph is absent (structurally missing, not merely empty), SSG-prm SHALL treat that layer's contribution to $\varphi(G)$ as zero, clear the corresponding bit in `tp.ssg_layer_bitmap`, and set `tp.ssg_reason_code = PARTIAL`. Processing continues normally with the remaining layers' contributions.

**[HLR-20.47-014]** If $\lVert \varphi(G) \rVert_2 = 0$ despite the structural graph $G$ being non-empty (i.e., $V \neq \emptyset$), SSG-prm SHALL set `tp.ssg_status = DEGENERATE` and SHALL escalate to MB-gov for audit. Processing continues; RB-prm receives the TP with the degenerate status flag set and `tp.ssg_signature` = $\mathbf{0}$.

## 7. Non-Normative Notes

> **Note 1 — Why structural invariants?**  
> Structural invariants are graph properties preserved under the isomorphisms
> relevant to the Thought Simulator architecture — reordering of equivalent
> residue nodes, relabelling of equivalent arc types. By extracting invariants
> rather than encoding raw graph topology, SSG-prm ensures that structurally
> equivalent graphs produce the same (or geometrically proximate) signatures
> regardless of superficial representational differences introduced by parallel
> OB-layer execution.

> **Note 2 — Why a real-valued vector in ℝ^d?**  
> A real-valued vector supports graded, continuous comparison between structural
> signatures. Discrete identifiers (integers, labels) impose a binary
> equal/not-equal relation; geometric vectors support degrees of structural
> similarity. RB-prm uses cosine similarity to route TPs to relational contexts
> that are geometrically close to previously seen structural patterns, enabling
> smooth generalization across structurally adjacent inputs.

> **Note 3 — SSG-prm as a coordinate chart**  
> In the language of differential geometry, SSG-prm implements a coordinate
> chart $\varphi : \mathcal{M} \to \mathbb{R}^d$ from the structural manifold
> $\mathcal{M}$ (the space of all structurally valid SmOB graphs) to a
> Euclidean patch. The chart is designed so that the metric structure of
> $\mathbb{R}^d$ — specifically cosine distance — approximates the intrinsic
> structural distance on $\mathcal{M}$. RB-prm navigates this chart; SSG-prm
> defines it.

> **Note 4 — Why cosine similarity?**  
> After L2 normalization, all non-zero signatures lie on the unit hypersphere
> $S^{d-1} \subset \mathbb{R}^d$. Cosine similarity on $S^{d-1}$ is equivalent
> to the inner product and measures the angle between two structural signatures.
> It is scale-invariant — two graphs that are structurally identical except for
> overall residue density produce the same normalized signature — and it avoids
> the curse of dimensionality that affects Euclidean distance in high-$d$
> spaces.

> **Note 5 — SSG-prm is not a semantic filter**  
> RB-prm uses `tp.ssg_signature` as a structural routing key, not a semantic
> descriptor. Two TPs with structurally identical graphs but different semantic
> content will produce the same $\sigma$. Routing correctness depends on RB-prm
> combining the structural signature with downstream semantic fields;
> `tp.ssg_signature` alone does not determine routing outcome.

> **Note 6 — EMPTY is a valid outcome**  
> A reason code of `EMPTY` ($\sigma = \mathbf{0}$, bitmap `0b0000`) is
> architecturally valid. It signals that no structural invariants were
> extractable from the current TP — for example, when all OB layers returned
> zero-residue graphs. RB-prm handles this via its own routing rules. SSG-prm
> does not escalate on `EMPTY`; it escalates only on `DEGENERATE` (non-empty
> graph producing a zero-norm feature vector, indicating a computational
> anomaly, not an expected architectural state).

> **Note 7 — Relationship to ΔH%**  
> The layer bitmap in `tp.ssg_layer_bitmap` is observable by downstream
> components as evidence of structural-invariant completeness. A bitmap of
> `0b0111` (SmOB contributed no structural invariants) is consistent with a
> ΔH% increase expected in the next OB cycle. SSG-prm does not compute or
> modify ΔH%; that accounting belongs to the OB layers and RBU-prm.
