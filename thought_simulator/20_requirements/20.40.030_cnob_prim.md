# 20.40.030 — CnOB Primitive (Constraint Object)

## 0. Purpose & Scope (Informative)

The Constraint Object (CnOB) is the structural layer responsible for extracting **constraint‑level residue** from the refined structure produced by SROB (20.40.020).  
CnOB encodes:

- constraint cues  
- missing‑slot signals  
- underspecification markers  
- conflict indicators  
- constraint‑level residue hashing  
- routing‑relevant constraint metadata  
- ΔH%‑derived constraint signals  

CnOB is **not** a semantic interpreter.  
It does **not** normalize structure, infer meaning, or reconstruct missing fields.  
It is a deterministic, monotonic, structural constraint layer that feeds directly into SmOB (20.40.040) and ultimately the Structural Signal Generator (20.47).

---

## 1. CnOB Definition (Normative)

CnOB is a deterministic structural object containing:

- `constraint_families` — the monotonic constraint sets (C1–C7)  
- `missing_slot_signals` — explicit markers for structural underspecification  
- `conflict_indicators` — structural conflict flags  
- `constraint_residue_hash` — deterministic hash of constraint‑level residue  
- `lineage_constraints` — constraints derived from structural ancestry  
- `delta_h_constraints` — constraints derived from ΔH%  
- `routing_constraints` — constraints relevant to routing eligibility  
- `policy_constraints` — constraints derived from policy signatures  

### **HLR‑20.40.030‑001**  
The TS SHALL construct CnOB as a pure structural‑constraint object containing only monotonic, provably entailed constraints derived from SROB, with no semantic or supervisory content.

---

## 2. Deterministic Constraint Extraction (Normative)

CnOB must be constructed deterministically from SROB, ensuring:

- identical SROB inputs → identical CnOB outputs  
- stable constraint sets  
- replay‑safe behavior  

### **HLR‑20.40.030‑002**  
The TS SHALL ensure that CnOB extraction is deterministic for identical SROB inputs, producing identical constraint sets across all executions.

---

## 3. Canonical Ordering of Constraints (Normative)

Constraint families (C1–C7) and all nested structures must be:

- canonically ordered  
- stable across replay  
- comparable across cycles  
- free of nondeterministic ordering artifacts  

### **HLR‑20.40.030‑003**  
The TS SHALL apply canonical ordering to all CnOB constraint families and nested constraint structures to ensure deterministic replay and structural comparability.

---

## 4. Structural Purity (Normative)

CnOB must not include:

- semantic inference  
- smoothing  
- reconstruction of missing fields  
- probabilistic or heuristic constraints  
- Pipeline B artifacts  

### **HLR‑20.40.030‑004**  
The TS SHALL ensure that CnOB contains only provable structural constraints and SHALL NOT infer, smooth, reconstruct, or approximate missing or noisy fields.

---

## 5. Constraint Families (Normative)

CnOB supports the following structural constraint families:

- **C1:** Structural existence constraints  
- **C2:** Structural adjacency constraints  
- **C3:** Structural ordering constraints  
- **C4:** Structural boundary constraints  
- **C5:** Structural lineage constraints  
- **C6:** Structural change (ΔH%) constraints  
- **C7:** Routing‑eligibility constraints  

These constraints are **monotonic**: new constraints may be added, but none may be removed.

### **HLR‑20.40.030‑005**  
The TS SHALL maintain monotonicity of all CnOB constraint families, ensuring that constraints may be added but never removed during structural evolution.

---

## 6. Lineage‑Derived Constraints (Normative)

CnOB must encode constraints derived from structural ancestry, including:

- parent–child structural relationships  
- inherited structural boundaries  
- inherited ordering constraints  

### **HLR‑20.40.030‑006**  
The TS SHALL derive lineage‑based constraints deterministically from SROB lineage and SHALL ensure that these constraints are stable across replay.

---

## 7. ΔH%‑Derived Constraints (Normative)

CnOB must encode constraints derived from structural change metrics, including:

- ΔH% thresholds  
- structural‑transition eligibility  
- stability constraints  

### **HLR‑20.40.030‑007**  
The TS SHALL derive ΔH%‑based constraints deterministically from SROB and SHALL ensure that ΔH% constraints are consistent with 20.47 and 20.12 invariants.

---

## 8. Constraint‑Residue Signals (Normative)

CnOB must explicitly extract:

- missing‑slot signals  
- underspecification markers  
- conflict indicators  
- constraint‑level residue hashing  
- missing‑mass attribution signals  

These are required for routing correctness, conflict detection, and ΔH% attribution.

### **HLR‑20.40.030‑008**  
The TS SHALL extract missing‑slot signals, underspecification markers, conflict indicators, and constraint‑level residue hashing as part of CnOB construction, ensuring deterministic residue for routing and ΔH% attribution.

---

## 9. CnOB as Input to SmOB (Normative)

CnOB feeds directly into SmOB (20.40.040).  
SmOB must incorporate CnOB constraints without modification.

### **HLR‑20.40.030‑009**  
The TS SHALL ensure that SmOB (20.40.040) receives CnOB as a direct input and SHALL NOT modify, reinterpret, or discard any CnOB constraints.

---

## 10. CnOB Is Not (Informative)

CnOB is **not**:

- a semantic interpreter  
- a structural normalizer  
- a truth evaluator  
- a generative mechanism  
- a smoothing or reconstruction layer  
- a semantic repair mechanism  

---

## 11. Example (Informative)

Given a refined structure from SROB:

- a missing argument slot produces a **missing‑slot signal**  
- an inconsistent ordering produces a **conflict indicator**  
- an underspecified subtree produces an **underspecification marker**  
- the combined constraint residue produces a **constraint‑level residue hash**  

CnOB outputs these as deterministic structural constraints for routing and ΔH% attribution.
