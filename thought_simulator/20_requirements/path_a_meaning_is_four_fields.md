The Path A four-field token model consists of token_surface, token_base, token_expression, and token_intent. These four fields are necessary and sufficient for meaning understanding and interpretation in Path A.

TS requires a four-field token model because Path A must operate as a deterministic, lane-local, replay-equivalent pipeline that progressively stabilizes distinct surfaces of meaning before any authoritative commit to semantic_core. The architecture scaffold demands explicit field ownership, YAML-declared dependencies, envelope geometry preservation, anomaly-driven routing, and strict A/B separation. A single undifferentiated meaning representation would violate these invariants by allowing authority drift, non-replayable inference, and unsafe interleaving of structural, lexical, expressive, and pragmatic operations. The four fields partition responsibility so that each primitive family owns only what it can deterministically resolve: intake stages own initialization and early commitment, OB primitives own structural and pre-semantic refinement, IdOB owns identity-conditioned finalization of pragmatic force, and commit stages own integration. Meaning cannot be reconstructed without these fields because downstream stages such as RB, TR, and SSR receive only bounded projections; missing any surface leaves routing, truth-relation shading, or semantic structure construction incomplete or nondeterministic. The model is minimal because each field maps directly onto an architectural primitive group defined in the glossary and flow documents. It is sufficient because the composition of the four fields supplies every input required for safe-boundary enforcement, TPU commit, and SSR(t) construction without introducing extraneous surfaces that would breach the single-writer invariant or replay equivalence.

Explicit invariants govern the model. Field ownership is partitioned by primitive: InB, IIInB, and IE own initialization and early commitment of surface, base, and expression; SOB through SmOB own structural refinement and intent precursors; IdOB owns finalization of token_intent; TR owns shading; SSR owns integration. Commit points are fixed: IE commits the first three fields; IdOB commits token_intent; TPU and OuBA commit the integrated result to semantic_core and SSR(t). Safe-boundary enforcement requires that supervisory annotations and routing decisions occur only at defined points after field stabilization, never mid-basin unsafeguarded mutation. Replay equivalence requires that every field value be deterministically derivable from prior pipeline state under the same input and seed. YAML dependency declarations must list read and write permissions for each field in every primitive definition so that both Python prototypes and C++ engines enforce the same contract.

**token_surface** preserves expressive geometry, adjacency cues, punctuation force, elongation, and emotional shading. Its definition is the canonical surface representation that carries positional, structural, and adjacency markers from raw intake. Its purpose is to supply the stable geometric substrate required by SOB for clause-boundary detection, SROB for normalization, SSG for signature generation, and RB for routing cues. Constraints include immutability after IE commit, adherence to InB schema validation, and prohibition of semantic inference. It interacts with token_expression by providing the geometric canvas that expressive metadata modulates and with token_base by anchoring lexical items within positional and adjacency context. It participates in meaning construction by enabling deterministic extraction of structural residue that feeds routing metadata and TR input formation. Without token_surface, SOB and SSG would lack the geometry required for replay-stable routing decisions.

**token_base** preserves lexical identity, dictionary meaning, semantic roles, and truth-relation mapping. Its definition is the lexical and role-based core that anchors each token to dictionary entries and basic semantic roles. Its purpose is to enable deterministic grounding and role extraction that later stages refine into propositions and referents. Constraints require bounding by KnDt-compatible entries, prohibition of Path B inference or multi-hop reasoning, and immutability of core identity after IE commit. It interacts with token_intent through refinement in IdOB where pragmatic valence is applied to role-bearing structures and with token_surface through harmonization in SmOB. It participates in meaning construction by forming the stable referent and proposition base that TPU commits into semantic_core. Without token_base, grounding primitives and IdOB would lack the lexical anchors required for identity-conditioned refinement.

**token_expression** preserves affective and expressive metadata such as elongation, intensity, repetition, punctuation patterns, and other expressive features. Its definition is the expressive overlay that captures non-lexical cues for affect, style, and emotional shading. Its purpose is to enrich routing metadata and realization substrate without altering lexical identity or pragmatic force. Constraints limit origination to IIInB proposals within bounded USP rules, with deterministic commitment at IE and subsequent harmonization only. It interacts with token_surface by modulating geometric cues and with token_intent by supplying valence precursors that IdOB synthesizes. It participates in meaning construction by providing the affective layer that TR uses for shading and SSR uses for final semantic integration. Without token_expression, affect-sensitive routing and realization would lose deterministic cues.

**token_intent** preserves communicative force, including supportive, clarifying, deceptive, controlling, obscurifying, challenging, hurting, or other pragmatic valence. Its definition is the pragmatic intent layer that encodes communicative purpose. Its purpose is to complete meaning construction by supplying the force that guides truth-relation shading and identity-conditioned decisions. Constraints keep the field empty until IdOB finalization under routed identity basins; earlier stages may produce only low-confidence precursors. It interacts with all prior fields through synthesis in IdOB and supplies direct input to TR for shading. It participates in meaning construction by completing the pragmatic envelope that OuBA freezes into SSR(t). Without token_intent, IdOB and TR would lack the authority and data required for identity-conditioned and truth-relation operations.

**Lifecycle Across Path A Primitives**

InB initializes all four fields to default values as the deterministic, non-inferential front door that performs schema validation and surface canonicalization without semantic repair or intent inference. IIInB proposes expression metadata only, using bounded USP rules to record shorthand expansions and repair proposals while leaving intent empty and committing nothing. IE serves as the commit point that deterministically applies all IIInB proposals and freezes token_surface, token_base, and token_expression into the structured intake object while leaving token_intent empty; this establishes the first safe boundary for downstream refinement.

SOB begins low-confidence intent classification by extracting initial structural cues, clause boundaries, and basic markers that provide the first precursors to token_intent. SROB refines those precursors based on adjacency and roles through structural normalization and canonical ordering. SmOB harmonizes the emerging intent with full structural context by compressing upstream residue and semantic-adjacent cues into a deterministic pre-semantic representation. At this stage token_intent remains a precursor only.

IdOB performs identity-conditioned meaning refinement after RB routing selects the appropriate identity basin. It is the first primitive allowed to finalize token_intent because only identity-basin authority can deterministically assign pragmatic valence consistent with the routed pathway. IdOB applies basin-local rules to the stabilized token_surface, token_base, and token_expression, synthesizing them with identity constraints to produce the final token_intent value. This finalization occurs under the identity-conditioned envelope so that subsequent TPU updates respect both the pragmatic force and the identity pathway.

TR applies truth-relation shading by consuming the now-final token_intent together with structural and routing metadata, producing shaded truth-relation fields that influence routing decisions and ΔH% evaluation. SSR constructs the final semantic structure by integrating all four fields—surface geometry, base roles, expressive metadata, and intent valence—into the immutable SSR(t) snapshot that OuBA commits for Path B consumption. The lifecycle therefore enforces a strict progression from initialization through structural refinement, identity-conditioned finalization, truth-relation shading, and semantic integration.

Consider the extended evolution of the token corresponding to the input fragment “quickly-ish, but really?” under a clarification-seeking identity pathway. InB sets all fields to defaults with raw surface form preserved. IIInB proposes expression metadata for the shorthand “-ish” and records the proposal without committing intent. IE commits token_surface to the canonicalized form with adjacency and punctuation markers, token_base to the adverbial-contrastive lexical roles, and token_expression to intensity and questioning hints, leaving token_intent empty. SOB extracts clause-boundary and low-confidence questioning markers as intent precursors. SROB refines adjacency between the adverbial and contrastive elements. SmOB harmonizes the contrastive structure with expressive intensity cues into a compressed pre-semantic residue. After RB routes to a clarification-seeking identity basin, IdOB finalizes token_intent as questioning-challenging pragmatic valence constrained by the identity pathway. TR shades the truth-relation with uncertainty markers derived from the intent. SSR integrates surface geometry, base roles, expressive intensity, and questioning intent into the final semantic structure committed by OuBA.

**Scout → Commit → General → Officers Architectural Metaphor**

The four-field model maps onto the scout → commit → general → officers metaphor that organizes TS pipeline discipline. Intake primitives (InB, IIInB, IE) function as scouts that gather and initially commit the first three fields without exercising interpretive authority. OB-set primitives (SOB through SmOB) function as refinement generals that harmonize structural and pre-semantic cues while preparing intent precursors. IdOB functions as the officer that exercises identity-conditioned authority to finalize token_intent, the last surface requiring specialized basin-local judgment. TR and SSR function as integrative officers that shade and compose the completed fields for commit. TPU and OuBA perform the final commit. This metaphor enforces that authority is exercised only at the appropriate stage and only over the fields each stage owns, preserving safe boundaries and preventing premature or overreaching decisions.

**Necessity and Sufficiency Argument**

These four fields are necessary because each architectural responsibility in Path A requires a distinct, auditable surface. Structural geometry cannot be derived from lexical identity alone; expressive metadata cannot be recovered from structure or base; pragmatic force cannot be inferred from surface, base, or expression without identity-basin authority. Removing token_surface starves SOB, SSG, and RB of geometry, breaking routing determinism and replay equivalence. Removing token_base starves grounding and role extraction, leaving IdOB without lexical anchors. Removing token_expression starves affect-sensitive shading and realization. Removing token_intent leaves IdOB and TR without the pragmatic layer required for identity-conditioned and truth-relation operations. The fields are sufficient because their composition satisfies every invariant: deterministic initialization at InB/IE, structural refinement through OB-set, identity-conditioned finalization at IdOB, truth-relation shading at TR, and semantic integration at SSR. No additional fields are required; any further surface would duplicate information already captured or would introduce authority outside the defined primitive ownership model, violating the scaffold’s explicit-dependency and minimalism requirements.

**Identity-Conditioned Meaning Refinement**

IdOB uses the four fields by consuming the stabilized token_surface, token_base, and token_expression after RB routing has selected the identity basin. It applies basin-local rules that constrain pragmatic valence according to the identity pathway, ensuring that token_intent is not a generic inference but a contextually bounded assignment. IdOB is the first primitive allowed to finalize token_intent because only identity-basin authority possesses the rules and scope to assign communicative force without violating A/B separation or introducing nondeterministic interpretation. Earlier stages may generate low-confidence precursors but lack the identity context required for authoritative assignment. Identity basins constrain intent by supplying the specific valence rules and identity signatures that IdOB must respect, so that the finalized token_intent remains coherent with the routed identity lane while preserving overall pipeline invariants.

**Truth-Relation Shading and Semantic Structure Construction**

TR uses token_intent to shade truth relations by incorporating the finalized pragmatic valence into routing metadata, directional-change evaluation, and ΔH% attribution. This shading produces deterministic adjustments to truth-relation fields that influence subsequent routing decisions and stability markers without altering the underlying meaning surfaces. SSR integrates all four fields by projecting and composing token_surface geometry, token_base roles, token_expression cues, and token_intent valence into a single deterministic semantic structure. The integration occurs after all prior refinement and shading steps, ensuring that the resulting SSR(t) snapshot is replay-stable, identity-consistent, and ready for Path B grounding and realization. Meaning therefore emerges as the invariant-preserving composition of the four fields rather than as an undifferentiated inference.
