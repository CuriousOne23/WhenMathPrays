# **20.30.005 — RTU‑prm (Routing TP Update Primitive)**

## **1. Scope**
This section defines **RTU‑prm**, the routing‑layer primitive responsible for producing **routing_update**, the structured routing metadata consumed by TR‑prm and the scheduler.  
RTU‑prm operates **only** on routing‑layer signals and performs **no semantic, truth, safety, merge, or commit‑layer computation**.

RTU‑prm **does not write to the TP**.  
It produces a routing_update object that is later consumed by TR‑prm.

---

## **2. Informative Overview**
RTU‑prm is the **first routing‑layer primitive** in Pipeline A.  
It determines:

- lane_activation  
- lane_suppression  
- routing_reason codes  
- routing_confidence  
- routing_error (fallback)

RTU‑prm:

- does **not** modify TP or MTP  
- does **not** write routing lineage (TR‑prm handles lineage)  
- does **not** perform arbitration  
- does **not** perform commit (RBU‑prm handles commit)  
- does **not** interpret meaning  

RTU‑prm is a **pure routing‑signal constructor**.

---

## **3. Normative Requirements**

### **3.1 Routing Metadata Construction**
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑001:** RTU‑prm SHALL construct a routing_update object containing only routing‑layer fields required by TR‑prm and the scheduler.  
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑002:** routing_update SHALL contain no semantic, truth, safety, merge, or commit‑layer fields.  
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑003:** routing_update SHALL include lane_activation signals indicating which interpretive lanes are eligible for execution.  
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑004:** routing_update SHALL include lane_suppression signals indicating which lanes must not be executed in the current cycle.  
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑005:** routing_update SHALL include routing_reason codes describing why each lane was activated or suppressed.

### **3.2 Determinism and Replay Safety**
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑006:** RTU‑prm SHALL produce routing_update deterministically from the user request and prior TP routing metadata.  
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑007:** RTU‑prm SHALL NOT depend on global state, stochastic processes, or latent‑space sampling.  
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑008:** Identical inputs SHALL produce identical routing_update outputs.

### **3.3 Structural Constraints**
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑009:** RTU‑prm SHALL NOT modify semantic_core.  
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑010:** RTU‑prm SHALL NOT modify truth_hypotheses.  
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑011:** RTU‑prm SHALL NOT modify TPTB or TPSF.  
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑012:** RTU‑prm SHALL NOT modify ΔH% or H%.  
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑013:** RTU‑prm SHALL NOT modify merge_result or update_structure.  
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑014:** RTU‑prm SHALL NOT modify any MTP‑level field.

### **3.4 Routing Signal Semantics**
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑015:** lane_activation SHALL be computed using only routing‑relevant features of the user request and prior TP routing metadata.  
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑016:** lane_suppression SHALL be computed when a lane is structurally incompatible with the request or violates routing constraints.  
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑017:** If lane_activation and lane_suppression conflict, suppression SHALL take precedence.  
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑018:** routing_update SHALL include a routing_confidence scalar in \([0,1]\) indicating routing stability (informative to TR‑prm, not a probability).

### **3.5 Interaction with TR‑prm and Scheduler**
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑019:** RTU‑prm SHALL expose routing_update to TR‑prm without requiring TR‑prm to recompute routing signals.  
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑020:** routing_update SHALL be complete and self‑contained for scheduling decisions.  
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑021:** RTU‑prm SHALL NOT invoke TR‑prm, OB‑prm, TB‑prm, RBU‑prm, or any interpretive or commit primitive.

### **3.6 Error and Constraint Handling**
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑022:** RTU‑prm SHALL detect structurally invalid routing metadata and replace it with a deterministic fallback routing_update.  
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑023:** RTU‑prm SHALL log routing_error codes when fallback routing is used.  
- **HLR‑20.30.005‑024:** Fallback routing SHALL preserve safety by suppressing unsafe or undefined lanes.

---

## 4. Mathematical Notes (Informative)

Routing confidence is a structural stability indicator, not a probability.

A typical form is:

$$
routing\_{confidence} = 1 - \frac{\text{num\\_suppressed\\_lanes}}{\text{total\\_lanes}}
$$

This value is used only as a routing heuristic and has no semantic or truth‑evaluation meaning.

---

## **5. Outputs**

RTU‑prm produces a **routing_update** object containing:

- lane_activation  
- lane_suppression  
- routing_reason codes  
- routing_confidence  
- routing_error (optional)

No other fields are permitted.
