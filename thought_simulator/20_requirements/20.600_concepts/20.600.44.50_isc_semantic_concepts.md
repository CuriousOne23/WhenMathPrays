# **20.600.44.50 — ISc‑Semantic (Conceptual Overview)**  
**Subsystem:** TS  
**Primitive:** ISc‑Semantic  
**Layer:** Conceptual (20.600 series)

---

# **1. What ISc‑Semantic *is***  
ISc‑Semantic is the **semantic projection engine** inside Pipeline A.  
Its job is to take a **detected semantic issue** and generate a **finite, deterministic set of semantic repair candidates**.

It does not:

- decide meaning  
- mutate TP  
- mutate semantic_core  
- score candidates  
- expand the meaning space  

It **proposes** meaning repairs.  
IB decides.

ISc‑Semantic is the *semantic counterpart* to ISc‑Scoring (20.44).  
Where ISc‑Scoring evaluates candidates, ISc‑Semantic **creates** them.

---

# **2. Why TS needs ISc‑Semantic**  
TS cannot rely on:

- IIInB (too early, pre‑semantic)  
- Detection (detects issues but cannot fix them)  
- Envelope (constrains but does not propose)  
- IB (decides but does not generate options)  

So TS needs a primitive that:

- understands semantic structure  
- understands envelope constraints  
- can project meaning forward  
- can propose valid repairs  
- can do so deterministically  

This is ISc‑Semantic.

It is the **semantic engine** that keeps TS from drifting, hallucinating, or collapsing meaning under ambiguity.

---

# **3. The core loop: M₀ → M_c → M′**  
ISc‑Semantic operates through a **three‑stage projection loop**:

### **1. M₀ — Current semantic state**  
This is the semantic_core as it exists at the moment of detection.

### **2. M_c — Candidate projection**  
ISc‑Semantic generates a **possible repair**:

- a clarified referent  
- a resolved contradiction  
- a completed incomplete structure  
- a disambiguated meaning  
- a repaired drift  

This is a *proposal*, not a commitment.

### **3. M′ — Envelope‑validated candidate**  
The candidate is then:

- checked against envelope constraints  
- normalized  
- validated  
- made reversible  
- made deterministic  

Only envelope‑valid candidates survive.

This loop is the **semantic heart** of TS.

---

# **4. What ISc‑Semantic actually *does***  
ISc‑Semantic performs four conceptual actions:

### **A. Interpret the detection report**  
It reads:

- the issue type  
- the location in TP  
- the semantic_core region involved  
- the referents or structures implicated  

### **B. Generate bounded repair candidates**  
For each issue, it produces a **finite** set of possible repairs.

Examples:

- For MI_REF_UNRES → propose referent candidates  
- For MI_INCOMP → propose completions  
- For MI_VAGUE → propose clarifications  
- For MI_CONTRA → propose resolutions  
- For MI_DRIFT → propose realignments  

### **C. Project each candidate into semantic space**  
Each candidate is projected into a **possible semantic state** (M_c).

### **D. Validate each candidate against the envelope**  
Invalid candidates are discarded.

The survivors become the **candidate set** returned to IB.

---

# **5. What ISc‑Semantic *never* does**  
ISc‑Semantic is intentionally limited.

It does **not**:

- decide meaning  
- mutate semantic_core  
- mutate TP  
- score candidates  
- call Pipeline B  
- access external knowledge  
- generate new concepts  
- expand the meaning manifold  
- perform nondeterministic search  

These boundaries keep TS stable, deterministic, and replay‑safe.

---

# **6. Relationship to other primitives**

### **IIInB → ISc‑Semantic**  
IIInB fixes *text*.  
ISc‑Semantic fixes *meaning*.

They never overlap.

### **Detection → ISc‑Semantic**  
Detection identifies the issue.  
ISc‑Semantic proposes repairs.

### **ISc‑Semantic → ISc‑Scoring**  
ISc‑Semantic generates candidates.  
ISc‑Scoring evaluates them.

### **ISc‑Semantic → IB**  
IB makes the final decision.

### **Envelope → ISc‑Semantic**  
The envelope constrains the candidate space.  
ISc‑Semantic must obey it.

---

# **7. Why ISc‑Semantic is not generative**  
ISc‑Semantic is **not** a generative model.  
It does not invent meaning.

Instead, it:

- works within the existing semantic manifold  
- respects envelope boundaries  
- preserves referential stability  
- maintains long‑duration coherence  

It is a **semantic repair engine**, not a semantic creator.

---

# **8. Examples (conceptual, not normative)**

### **Example 1 — Referent resolution**  
Input: “Put it on the table.”  
Detection: MI_REF_UNRES (“it”)  
ISc‑Semantic proposes:

- “it = the book”  
- “it = the cup”  
- “it = the box”  

Envelope filters out impossible referents.

### **Example 2 — Contradiction repair**  
Input: “The door is open. The door is closed.”  
Detection: MI_CONTRA  
ISc‑Semantic proposes:

- “two different doors”  
- “one is metaphorical”  
- “one is temporal (open earlier, closed now)”  

Envelope filters based on context.

### **Example 3 — Vagueness repair**  
Input: “He went over there.”  
Detection: MI_VAGUE  
ISc‑Semantic proposes:

- “there = the kitchen”  
- “there = the garage”  
- “there = the backyard”  

Envelope filters based on prior referents.

---

# **9. Why ISc‑Semantic is essential for TS inference**  
Without ISc‑Semantic:

- ambiguity would accumulate  
- contradictions would persist  
- referents would drift  
- incomplete structures would remain incomplete  
- IB would have no options to choose from  
- TS would collapse under semantic uncertainty  

ISc‑Semantic is the **semantic stabilizer** of the entire architecture.

---

# **10. Summary**  
ISc‑Semantic is:

- deterministic  
- bounded  
- envelope‑constrained  
- replay‑safe  
- non‑generative  
- non‑decisive  
- non‑mutating  
- proposal‑only  

It is the **semantic projection engine** that keeps TS coherent under uncertainty.

It is the conceptual counterpart to:

- **20.44 ISc‑Scoring** (evaluation)  
- **20.44.50 ISc‑Semantic** (projection)  
- **20.90 IB** (decision)  

Together, they form the **semantic repair triad** of TS.
