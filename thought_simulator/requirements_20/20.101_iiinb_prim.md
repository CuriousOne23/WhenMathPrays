# **1. Purpose** *(Informative)*

The Input Inference/Repair Basin (IIInB) is the **second deterministic boundary** of Path‑A.  
Its role is to analyze the canonicalized surface form produced by InB and identify **pre‑semantic defects, deterministic shorthand forms, and surface irregularities** that can be repaired **without interpreting meaning**.

IIInB does **not** apply repairs.  
Instead, it produces a **bounded, deterministic, rule‑based set of proposed repair operations** that IE applies in the next stage.

IIInB ensures that:

- downstream modules receive **clean, normalized, pre‑semantic surface form**,  
- all shorthand expansions and surface corrections are **deterministic**,  
- no semantic inference or intent interpretation occurs before IE,  
- ambiguous or context‑dependent expansions are never performed,  
- replay and audit can reconstruct every proposed repair operation,  
- IE and CEx begin from a **stable, deterministic, pre‑semantic foundation**.

IIInB is a **repair‑proposal primitive**, not a semantic module.  
It identifies only corrections that can be made **without meaning**, **without context**, and **without TP.semantic or TP.process history**, ensuring Path‑A remains deterministic and pre‑semantic until IE applies the approved repairs.

IIInB protects the pipeline by preventing:

- nondeterministic shorthand expansion,  
- semantic repair before IE,  
- context‑dependent rewriting,  
- meaning mutation caused by noisy surface form,  
- ambiguity propagation into CEx and CE.

---

# **2. Scope** *(Informative)*

IIInB operates only on the canonicalized surface form produced by InB in:

- **TP.intake.surface**  
- **TP.intake.tokens**

It identifies deterministic shorthand expansions and surface irregularities and produces a coherent set of proposed repairs.  
It does **not** apply repairs, interpret meaning, resolve ambiguity, or access global state.

---

# **3. Interfaces** *(Informative)*

### **3.1 Inputs**
- **InB_Output → TP.intake**  
  Canonicalized surface tokens, spans, punctuation, and intake metadata.

### **3.2 Outputs**
- **IIInB_Output → IE.repair_proposals**  
  Proposed repair operations and suggested normalized surface form.  
  Repairs are not applied until IE.

### 3.3 TP Fields Used and Produced by IIInB (Normative)

#### **HLR‑20.101‑034 — TP Fields Read by IIInB**
IIInB SHALL read only the following TP fields produced by InB:
- TP.intake.surface
- TP.intake.tokens

#### **HLR‑20.101‑035 — TP Fields Written by IIInB**
IIInB SHALL write only the following TP fields:
- TP.repair_proposals
- TP.anomaly_flags
- TP.intake.metadata.iiinb

#### **HLR‑20.101‑036 — Prohibition on Writing Committed Fields**
IIInB SHALL NOT write, modify, or mutate the following TP fields:
- TP.normalized
- TP.tokens
- TP.structure
- TP.semantic
- TP.process

#### **HLR‑20.101‑037 — Required Structure of TP.repair_proposals**
IIInB SHALL express each proposed repair operation in TP.repair_proposals using deterministic token‑span indexing derived solely from TP.intake.tokens.  
Each repair operation SHALL include:
- the deterministic rule identifier,
- the token span it applies to,
- the proposed replacement or normalization parameters.

#### **HLR‑20.101‑038 — Required Structure of TP.anomaly_flags**
IIInB SHALL express each anomaly indicator in TP.anomaly_flags using deterministic token‑span indexing and SHALL NOT include semantic interpretation or inferred meaning.

#### **HLR‑20.101‑039 — IE Intake Contract**
IIInB output SHALL provide sufficient information for IE to construct IE.intake, including:
- TP.intake.surface
- TP.intake.tokens
- TP.repair_proposals
- TP.anomaly_flags
- TP.intake.metadata.iiinb

#### **HLR‑20.101‑040 — Repair Non‑Commitment**
IIInB SHALL NOT apply any repair operation.  
All repairs SHALL remain proposals until IE applies them deterministically.

#### **HLR‑20.101‑041 — Token and Surface Non‑Mutation**
IIInB SHALL NOT mutate TP.intake.surface or TP.intake.tokens.  
All surface and token mutations SHALL be performed exclusively by IE.

# **4. Normative Requirements (HLRs)**  
*(Reorganized, merged/split for clarity; all original information preserved)*

## **4.1 Deterministic Operation Requirements**

### **HLR‑20.101‑001**  
IIInB SHALL operate only on **TP.intake** produced by InB.

### **HLR‑20.101‑011**  
IIInB output SHALL be fully deterministic and replayable for identical **TP.intake** inputs.

### **HLR‑20.101‑013**  
IIInB SHALL NOT depend on nondeterministic sources such as randomness, user‑profile heuristics, or external services.

---

## **4.2 Semantic Isolation Requirements**

### **HLR‑20.101‑002**  
IIInB SHALL produce only deterministic, rule‑based repair proposals that preserve meaning and do not modify **TP.semantic**.

### **HLR‑20.101‑003**  
IIInB SHALL NOT introduce new semantic content, referents, or constraints into **TP.semantic**.

### **HLR‑20.101‑004**  
IIInB SHALL NOT perform semantic inference, ambiguity resolution, or intent interpretation; **TP.semantic** SHALL remain empty until downstream stages.

### **HLR‑20.101‑007**  
IIInB SHALL NOT expand shorthand forms whose interpretation depends on context, history, or semantics.

### **HLR‑20.101‑026 (New)**
IIInB SHALL apply deterministic repair‑proposal rules in a stable, predefined order that does not depend on runtime conditions, semantic context, or external state. The ordering of rule application SHALL be fixed for a given ruleset and SHALL NOT vary across executions. The specific ordering mechanism, rule grouping, or sequencing strategy is implementation‑defined, provided it is deterministic and derived solely from the active ruleset.

### **HLR‑20.101‑027 (New)**
IIInB SHALL interpret the intake token sequence deterministically and without reference to semantic context, historical state, or surface‑string heuristics. All repair‑proposal logic SHALL derive solely from the canonical token sequence produced by InB, including token boundaries, token ordering, and token classification. The specific token structure, token‑classification scheme, and surface‑string representation used by InB are implementation‑defined, provided they are deterministic and stable across executions.

### **HLR‑20.101‑028 (New)**
IIInB SHALL propose surface‑normalization operations only when such normalization is deterministic, pre‑semantic, and does not require contextual interpretation. Normalization SHALL operate solely on the canonical surface form and token sequence produced by InB, and SHALL NOT introduce semantic changes, reinterpret meaning, or alter token boundaries. The specific normalization strategies, categories, and implementation details are implementation‑defined, provided they remain deterministic and stable across executions.

### **HLR‑20.101‑029 (New)**
IIInB SHALL detect and deterministically represent invalid or un-decodable Unicode input produced by InB. Such defects SHALL be treated as pre‑semantic anomalies and SHALL NOT be interpreted, reconstructed, or semantically inferred. IIInB SHALL propose deterministic repair or normalization operations when possible, and SHALL annotate unrepairable invalid‑Unicode defects for downstream handling. The specific representation of invalid Unicode tokens, their classification, and any normalization strategy are implementation‑defined, provided they remain deterministic and stable across executions.

### **HLR‑20.101‑030 (New)**
IIInB SHALL detect and deterministically represent any unrecognized, malformed, or broken tokens produced by InB, including but not limited to partial tokens, decoding failures, tokenizer anomalies, or structurally invalid token objects. Such defects SHALL be treated as pre‑semantic anomalies and SHALL NOT be semantically interpreted, reconstructed, or inferred. IIInB SHALL propose deterministic repair or normalization operations when possible, and SHALL annotate unrepairable broken‑token defects for downstream handling. The specific classification of broken tokens, their representation, and any associated normalization strategy are implementation‑defined, provided they remain deterministic and stable across executions.

### **HLR‑20.101‑031 (New)**
IIInB SHALL operate deterministically when interpreting mixed‑granularity intake consisting of both a canonical surface string and a canonical token sequence. Repair‑proposal logic SHALL derive solely from the token sequence, except where deterministic surface‑level normalization is explicitly permitted by the active ruleset. IIInB SHALL NOT rely on raw surface‑string heuristics, whitespace patterns, or character‑offset analysis that could diverge from the token sequence. The relationship between surface form and token sequence is implementation‑defined, provided it is deterministic and stable across executions.

### **HLR‑20.101‑032 (New)**
IIInB SHALL represent multi‑span repair proposals deterministically and without reliance on semantic interpretation or surface‑string heuristics. Multi‑span repairs SHALL be derived solely from the canonical token sequence and SHALL preserve token order, token boundaries, and pre‑semantic constraints.
The specific representation of multi‑span repairs (including span grouping, ordering, or structural encoding) is implementation‑defined, provided it remains deterministic, stable across executions, and sufficient for IE and CEx to replay and apply the repair proposals.

### **HLR‑20.101‑033 (New)**
IIInB SHALL handle interactions among repair‑proposal rules deterministically, including cases where multiple rules apply to overlapping or adjacent token spans. When multiple rules match the same region of the token sequence, IIInB SHALL apply the deterministic rule‑ordering defined by the active ruleset to produce a stable and replayable set of repair proposals. IIInB SHALL NOT merge, reinterpret, or semantically reconcile overlapping repairs; instead, it SHALL emit all applicable repair proposals in deterministic order for downstream resolution by IE. The specific mechanisms for detecting, ordering, or representing rule interactions are implementation‑defined, provided they remain deterministic and stable across executions.

---

## **4.3 Isolation From Global State**

### **HLR‑20.101‑005**  
IIInB SHALL NOT access **TP.process**, **TP.metadata**, CE, CIL, semantic_core, or any global state.

### **HLR‑20.101‑012**  
IIInB SHALL NOT invoke any OB layer (SOB, SROB, CnOB, SmOB), SSG, TR, RB, or IdOB.

---

## **4.4 Shorthand and Normalization Requirements**

### **HLR‑20.101‑006**  
IIInB SHALL propose expansions only for deterministic shorthand forms explicitly approved for expansion.

### **HLR‑20.101‑009**  
IIInB SHALL propose normalization operations only when such normalization is deterministic.

### **HLR‑20.101‑010**  
IIInB SHALL NOT modify or reinterpret punctuation in ways that alter meaning.

---

## **4.5 Token Preservation Requirements**

### **HLR‑20.101‑008**  
IIInB SHALL preserve token order in **TP.intake.tokens** and SHALL NOT drop, reorder, or duplicate tokens.

### **HLR‑20.101‑020 (New)**  
IIInB SHALL express all proposed repair operations in terms of deterministic token spans derived from **TP.intake.tokens**. Repair operations SHALL NOT depend on raw character offsets, whitespace counts, or surface‑string indexing. The specific indexing scheme (including inclusivity, exclusivity, or zero‑based conventions) is implementation‑defined, provided it is deterministic and derived solely from the token sequence.

### **HLR‑20.101‑021 (New)**
IIInB SHALL propose Unicode normalization operations only when such normalization is deterministic and does not require semantic interpretation. Normalization SHALL operate solely on the token sequence derived from TP.intake.tokens and SHALL NOT rely on raw character offsets or contextual meaning. The specific normalization form (e.g., NFC, NFD, NFKC) is implementation‑defined, provided it is deterministic and stable across executions.

### **HLR‑20.101‑022 (New)**
IIInB SHALL identify and annotate any intake defects that cannot be deterministically repaired, so that downstream modules (including IE and CEx) can propagate or handle these anomalies deterministically. The specific anomaly categories, field names, and metadata structure are implementation‑defined, provided that the anomalies are detectable, replayable, and stable across executions.

### **HLR‑20.101‑023 (New)**
IIInB SHALL express each proposed repair operation in a deterministic, structured form sufficient for IE and CEx to reconstruct and apply the repair.
Each repair operation SHALL identify:
- the deterministic rule that generated it,
- the token span or intake region it applies to, and
- the parameters required for deterministic replay.

The exact field names, object structure, and serialization format of repair operations are implementation‑defined, provided they remain deterministic and stable across executions.

---

## **4.6 Metadata Requirements**

### **HLR‑20.101‑014 (Revised)**  
IIInB SHALL emit metadata sufficient for deterministic replay, audit, and downstream consumption by IE and CEx. This metadata SHALL include a deterministic description of all proposed repair operations and any anomaly indicators required by the active ruleset. The exact field names and structure of this metadata are implementation‑defined.

### **HLR‑20.101‑019**
IIInB SHALL annotate all unrepairable defects in IIInB_Output.metadata.anomaly_flags so that IE can deterministically detect and propagate anomalies.

### **HLR‑20.101‑015 (Revised)**  
IIInB SHALL attach a ruleset identifier that uniquely identifies the active deterministic ruleset used to generate repair proposals. The format and structure of this identifier are implementation‑defined.

### **HLR‑20.101‑025 (New)**
IIInB ruleset evolution SHALL be deterministic and version‑controlled. When the semantics of any deterministic rule change, the ruleset identifier SHALL change accordingly so that replay and audit can reconstruct the exact behavior used at the time of execution. Adding new deterministic rules or extending existing rules without altering their semantics SHALL NOT require a ruleset identifier change. The specific versioning scheme and identifier format are implementation‑defined.

### **HLR‑20.101‑017 (Revised)**  
IIInB output SHALL be serialization‑ready for downstream modules, but the serialization format, field layout, and encoding conventions are implementation‑defined. The output MUST contain sufficient metadata for IE and CEx to deterministically reconstruct and apply all proposed repairs.

---

## **4.7 Error Handling Requirements**

### **HLR‑20.101‑016**  
IIInB SHALL fail fast and report an error if **TP.intake** is structurally invalid or cannot be deterministically repaired.

### **HLR‑20.101‑024 (New)**
IIInB failure behavior SHALL be deterministic and replayable. When IIInB cannot proceed due to structurally invalid intake or violations of deterministic‑operation constraints, it SHALL emit a failure state that is stable across executions and does not depend on runtime environment, external services, or semantic context. The specific mechanism used to signal failure (error object, exception, or structured failure output) is implementation‑defined, provided it is deterministic and pre‑semantic.

# 5. Allowed Transformations (Informative)

The following transformations may be identified and proposed by IIInB when they satisfy all normative requirements and operate only on TP.intake.surface and TP.intake.tokens:

- deterministic shorthand expansion proposals (e.g., “btw” → “by the way”),
- deterministic contraction expansion proposals,
- deterministic punctuation normalization proposals,
- deterministic casing normalization proposals,
- deterministic Unicode normalization proposals,
- deterministic repeated‑character correction proposals.

These transformations are expressed only as repair proposals.  
IIInB does not apply any transformation; IE determines whether to apply proposed repairs.

---

# **6. Forbidden Transformations** *(Informative)*

Forbidden operations include any that require **TP.semantic**, **TP.process**, CE, or context:

- semantic inference  
- expansion requiring TP history  
- ellipsis resolution  
- pronoun resolution  
- intent inference  
- command inference  
- creative rewriting  
- nondeterministic heuristics  

Examples:

- “again” → “repeat the last action”  
- “same as before” → “repeat previous command”  
- “lights kitchen” → “lights in the kitchen”  

These violate semantic‑isolation HLRs.

---

# **7. Determinism Requirements** *(Informative)*

IIInB transformations are fully deterministic:

- same **TP.intake** → same **IIInB_Output**  
- no stochastic branching  
- no user‑dependent behavior  
- no environment‑dependent behavior  

Replay determinism is required for audit and governance.

---

# **8. Metadata Structure** *(Informative)*

IIInB output includes:

```json
metadata: {
  ruleset_id: RulesetID,
  timestamp: Timestamp,
  input_hash: Hash,
  repair_operations: [RepairOp]
}
```

This metadata supports replay, debugging, and provenance tracking.

# 9. Summary (Informative)

IIInB is a deterministic, pre‑semantic repair‑proposal primitive.  
It identifies surface‑level defects, shorthand forms, and normalization opportunities that can be proposed without interpreting meaning.  
IIInB produces repair proposals and anomaly indicators only; it does not apply repairs or modify committed TP fields.  
IE applies approved repairs and produces the committed normalized surface, token sequence, and structural interpretation for downstream primitives.
