# 20.18 Failure Modes and Success Criteria

**Document ID:** 20.18  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 2026-06-03  
**Status:** Draft - For CP Review  
**Source:** 20.10 Architectural Principles (Section 1.14) + Grok-CP Dialogue

## Scope
This document defines explicit success criteria and failure modes for major TS mechanisms. It operationalizes the "Success Criteria and Failure Modes" refinement identified in 20.10.

## Non-Goals
- This document does not define implementation details or algorithms.
- It does not specify test cases (belongs in 30-series verification).
- It does not define recovery procedures (belongs in 30/40-series).

## 1. Purpose
To ensure every critical architectural mechanism has clear, measurable success criteria and well-defined failure modes, enabling early detection of architectural drift and supporting rigorous validation.

## 2. Core Mechanisms – Success Criteria & Failure Modes

### 2.1 GB Arbitration & Supervisory Control

**HLR-20.018-001:** GB Arbitration & Supervisory Control Success Criteria  
- GB decisions are deterministic, auditable, and bounded in time/complexity.  
- All GB actions include explicit reason codes and evidence.  
- GB intervention rate stays within defined safe thresholds.

**HLR-20.018-002:** GB Arbitration & Supervisory Control Failure Modes  
- GB becomes a performance bottleneck or single point of failure.  
- GB makes un-auditable or non-deterministic decisions.  
- Excessive GB intervention (thrashing) or insufficient intervention (drift).

### 2.2 ΔH% Regulation

**HLR-20.018-003:** ΔH% Regulation Success Criteria  
- ΔH% trajectories remain stable and bounded within all basins.  
- ΔH% effectively prevents runaway trace growth and oscillation.  
- Energy accounting is fully deterministic and reproducible.

**HLR-20.018-004:** ΔH% Regulation Failure Modes  
- ΔH% becomes too permissive (allowing instability).  
- ΔH% becomes too restrictive (stifling legitimate reasoning).  
- ΔH% calculations drift or become non-deterministic.

### 2.3 Commitments

**HLR-20.018-005:** Commitments Success Criteria  
- Commitments are created only on stable, high-confidence traces.  
- Commitment decay works correctly over time.  
- Contradictory commitments are detected and surfaced.

**HLR-20.018-006:** Commitments Failure Modes  
- Premature or overly strong commitments on weak evidence.  
- Failure to decay outdated commitments.  
- Unresolved commitment conflicts leading to incoherent state.
