1. **Structure** — the meaning‑blind geometry of the utterance  
2. **Meaning** — the multi‑object semantic content  
3. **Identity** — the stance, pressure, and importance applied to meaning

IdOB is the packet format that encodes these three layers.  
Path A is the deterministic pipeline that reconstructs the mental object:

```
Structure → Meaning → Identity → Freeze
```

This separation of layers allows TS Path A to:

- extract meaning deterministically  
- avoid hallucination and over‑interpretation  
- understand multiple semantic objects simultaneously  
- apply identity safely without corrupting structure  
- produce reproducible, testable meaning vectors  

In short, TS Path A is the operationalization of a communication theory grounded in observed human behavior. It provides the minimal, sufficient architecture for turning human utterances into structured semantic understanding.

## **2. The TS Theory of Communication**

### **2.1 Communication as Transmission of Structured Mental Objects**

TS assumes that communication is not merely the exchange of words.  
It is the **transfer of a structured mental object** containing:

- what is being talked about  
- who is involved  
- what actions or states occur  
- where events take place  
- why the utterance is being made  
- how important each aspect is  
- what stance the speaker is applying  

This mental object must be reconstructed by the listener.

TS Path A provides the machinery for that reconstruction.

---

### **2.2 The Three Layers of the Mental Object**

TS asserts that every utterance contains three separable layers:

---

### **A. Structure — Meaning-Blind Geometry**

Structure is the **shape** of the utterance.  
It contains:

- semantic field  
- roles (agent, patient, location, etc.)  
- object slots  
- gradient class  
- universe of discourse  
- structural fingerprints  

Structure is **meaning-blind**.  
It does not contain names, intentions, or interpretations.

It is the **floor plan** of the mental object.

---

### **B. Meaning — Multi-Row Semantic Content**

Meaning is the **content** of the mental object.  
It contains:

- meaning groups (PERSON, OBJECT, LOCATION, etc.)  
- group categories and primitives  
- prototype meaning vectors  
- six meaning fields:  
  - physicality  
  - sociality  
  - temporality  
  - intentionality  
  - materiality  
  - spatiality  
- resolved semantic content (`meaning_semantics`)

Meaning is **multi-row** because utterances often reference multiple objects.

It is the **furniture** inside the floor plan.

---

### **C. Identity — Stance, Pressure, and Freeze**

Identity is the **stance** applied to the mental object.  
It contains:

- identity envelope  
- importance  
- stance tags  
- identity vector  
- identity delta  
- meaning delta  
- freeze conditions  

Identity modulates meaning without altering structure.

It is the **lighting and mood** applied to the room.

---

### **2.3 Why These Layers Are Necessary**

Human communication is ambiguous, layered, and context-dependent.  
If you collapse structure, meaning, and identity into one blob, you get:

- hallucination  
- meaning leakage  
- stance confusion  
- unstable interpretation  

TS avoids this by **separating** the layers and making each one explicit.
