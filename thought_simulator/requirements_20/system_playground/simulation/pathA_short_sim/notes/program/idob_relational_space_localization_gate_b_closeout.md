## 3) Bounded interpretation of Gate B

Gate B tested one joint realization:

`information -> representation -> selection -> activation -> measured leverage`

Its NEGATIVE result does not isolate whether the binding limitation was:

- available information;
- runtime representation;
- selection mechanism;
- organizational granularity;
- evaluation criterion;
- interaction among these.

No claim is made that a satisfactory non-oracle realization necessarily exists, and no claim is made that it necessarily does not exist.

## 4) Gate A + Gate B interpretation (preserved)

### Gate A (preserved separately)

Gate A provides bounded, fixture-relative evidence that the frozen organizational layer can provide measurable leverage when sufficiently informed oracle localization is supplied.

### Gate B (preserved separately)

Gate B provides bounded evidence that the one frozen provisional non-oracle information/representation/selection realization tested did not recover that leverage under the frozen `q0` dominance criterion.

### Combined bounded conclusion

Defining a localization signal is not sufficient by itself. A successful realization would also require permitted non-oracle information to preserve distinctions usable at runtime and a mechanism capable of converting those distinctions into useful relational-space activation. Gate B neither identifies those distinctions nor establishes that a satisfactory realization exists. It establishes only that the provisional joint realization tested in Gate B did not satisfy the frozen criterion.

Additionally preserved:

“Find a better mapping from the existing `X_t`” is not yet justified as the next research direction because Gate B did not establish that the existing `X_t` contains or preserves the information required for successful localization.
