# **ts_candidate_cognitive_pressures.md**  
**Status:** Draft v1  
**Purpose:** Present the TS invariants as *candidate cognitive pressures* observed across independently accepted cognitive systems, without claiming universality, without definitional circularity, and without relaxing operational standards.

---

## **1. Purpose and Posture**

TS proposes five invariants:

1. **Structural adjacency**  
2. **Constraint geometry**  
3. **Cue stability**  
4. **Routing specialization**  
5. **Identity basins**

This document does **not** claim these invariants are universal or necessary for all cognition.

Instead, it adopts the calibrated stance:

> **These invariants are candidate cognitive pressures that appear repeatedly across systems already accepted as cognitive by independent scientific consensus.  
Whether they are strictly necessary remains open to evidence.**

This avoids circularity and overreach.

---

## **2. What counts as “independently accepted cognitive systems”**

We restrict attention to systems that are widely regarded as cognitive based on:

- behavioral evidence  
- functional evidence  
- neuroscientific evidence  
- engineering consensus  
- cognitive-science consensus  

Examples include:

- humans  
- primates  
- mammals  
- insects with navigational and learning capacities  
- classical cognitive architectures (ACT-R, SOAR)  
- modern neural models (transformers, RNNs)  
- engineered adaptive agents (autonomous robots with planning or learning)

This avoids defining cognition by the invariants themselves.

---

## **3. What we actually observe (modest, non-overextended)**

Across these systems, we repeatedly see **functional analogues** of the TS pressures:

### **3.1 Structural adjacency**  
Systems track continuity, segmentation, and boundaries.  
Not necessarily TS-style recoverable adjacency, but **functional equivalents**.

### **3.2 Constraint geometry**  
Systems detect violations, conflicts, or prediction errors.  
Not necessarily TS-style conflict signatures, but **functional equivalents**.

### **3.3 Cue stability**  
Systems compress and weight relevant signals.  
Not necessarily TS-style cue manifolds, but **functional equivalents**.

### **3.4 Routing specialization**  
Systems route information through specialized pathways.  
Not necessarily TS-style routing heads, but **functional equivalents**.

### **3.5 Identity basins**  
Systems maintain persistent state or continuity.  
Not necessarily TS-style identity attractors, but **functional equivalents**.

This avoids over-extension of TS terms.

---

## **4. What we do *not* claim**

We explicitly do **not** claim:

- that insects have TS-style identity basins  
- that ACT-R has TS-style constraint geometry  
- that robots have TS-style routing specialization  
- that biological systems exhibit TS-style recoverable invariants  
- that TS invariants define cognition  
- that TS invariants are universal  
- that TS invariants are sufficient for cognition  
- that TS invariants appear in the same form across systems  
- that TS invariants are embedded in transformers  

This prevents the earlier conceptual drift.

---

## **5. Open empirical questions (kept prominent)**

The following remain open:

- Are some invariants necessary while others are merely common?  
- Are identity basins required only for self-referential or long-horizon cognition?  
- Are constraint invariants required for planning but not for reactive behavior?  
- Are routing invariants required for complex agents but not simple ones?  
- Are cue invariants required for abstraction but not for reflexive control?  
- Are adjacency invariants required for perception but not for symbolic reasoning?  

These questions keep the stance evidence-seeking.

---

## **6. Why this calibrated claim is scientifically stable**

This framing:

- avoids circularity  
- avoids over-extension  
- avoids universality claims  
- preserves operational definitions  
- aligns with the LLM probe standards  
- invites counterexamples  
- invites empirical testing  
- remains non-hierarchical  
- remains falsifiable  
- remains modest  
- remains defensible  

It gives confidence without overclaiming.

It is the correct conceptual companion to the experimental protocols.

---

## **7. Next step (optional)**

If time allows later, the next scientific move is:

> **Search for cognitive systems that appear to lack one or more invariants under the operational definitions.**

If none are found → invariants gain strength.  
If some are found → invariants can be ranked or refined.

This is the long-term path to necessity claims.
