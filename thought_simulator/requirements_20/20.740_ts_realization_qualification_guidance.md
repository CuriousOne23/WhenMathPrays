# 20.740 TS Realization Qualification Guidance

**Document ID:** 20.740  
**Date:** 2026-11-25  
**Status:** Reference guidance (non-normative).  
**Authority:** Engineering/explanatory guidance only. This document is not an HLR source, not primitive behavior authority, and does not replace or modify normative 20-series requirements.

## 1. Purpose and authority

This document provides reusable TS engineering guidance for qualifying whether and how a concept should be realized, characterized, bounded, deferred, or reopened.

It does not define runtime semantics, required algorithms, or acceptance of any specific mechanism. Normative behavior authority remains in the applicable requirement files.

## 2. Concept-to-engineering progression

General development progression:

`concept -> terminology -> proposed role -> represented structure -> structural assessment -> bounded realization -> characterization -> utility assessment -> validity-boundary discovery -> continue/extend/defer/repair/reject`

This progression is development guidance, not a mandatory runtime sequence.

## 3. Realization qualification dimensions

Qualification may consider, as applicable:

- realization;
- utility;
- efficiency;
- scalability;
- supportability;
- extensibility;
- capability;
- observability;
- testability;
- structural/programming understanding.

These are engineering dimensions, not automatically numerical scores. A mechanism need not maximize every dimension to be useful.

## 4. Local utility principle

Local utility is sufficient to justify bounded realization and characterization.

Local success does not, by itself, establish:

- global validity;
- universal optimality;
- absolute truth;
- unlimited scalability.

Prefer realizations that allow validity domains to be discovered and extended through bounded use.

## 5. Validity-boundary development

Useful engineering pattern:

`smallest useful bounded model -> characterize useful domain -> approach/discover boundary through use -> quantify degradation/failure where meaningful -> extend when justified -> re-characterize`

Reaching a validity boundary is an engineering observation, not automatically a failure. A boundary need not always be numerical.

## 6. Structural health test

Evaluate questions such as:

- Is required information being destroyed?
- Can missing representation/capability be added locally later?
- Can downstream contracts remain stable?
- Would postponement force an approximation/surrogate to become architecture?

Guidance outcomes:

- **SAFE_TO_DEFER**: clean extension/substitution path exists and postponement does not corrupt downstream architecture.
- **STOP_AND_REPAIR**: later realization would require foundation dismantling, lost-information reconstruction, contract replacement, or surrogate crystallization.

These are engineering guidance labels, not runtime enums.

## 7. Surrogate discipline

A surrogate may support bounded realization and characterization if it remains explicitly bounded.

A surrogate should:

- remain explicitly labeled surrogate;
- retain stated scope;
- not silently gain architectural authority;
- not be treated as proof of the future/full mechanism.

Future work should be able to replace or extend the surrogate without invalidating bounded observations unnecessarily.

## 8. Claim/status discipline

Distinguish and communicate status explicitly, as useful:

- proposed;
- represented;
- realized;
- observed;
- characterized;
- locally supported;
- unavailable;
- unresolved;
- unknown;
- not established.

These labels support honest engineering communication and should not be over-formalized into mandatory TS enums by default.

## 9. Decision outcomes

Engineering development outcomes include:

- CONTINUE;
- EXTEND;
- SAFE_TO_DEFER;
- STOP_AND_REPAIR;
- REJECT;
- INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE.

Selection among outcomes should be evidence-backed and scope-bounded.

## 10. Future pickup / deferred work record

Deferred work records should preserve enough context to recover:

- why the work exists;
- what was attempted;
- what is established;
- what is not established;
- known structural/extension seam;
- surrogate boundary;
- risks;
- authoritative dependencies;
- reopen trigger;
- relevant observations/artifacts.

## 11. Testability and engineering evidence

Prefer observable, mechanical evidence. Not every useful criterion needs a scalar.

Legitimate evidence classes may include:

- deterministic/replay behavior;
- interface stability;
- counts where materially relevant;
- candidate-space reduction;
- memory/latency where relevant;
- provenance;
- structural invariants;
- bounded failure states;
- explicit Unknown/Unavailable results.

This guidance does not define TS-wide mandatory metric sets.

## 12. Scope limit

This document does not itself validate any specific TS mechanism. Individual mechanisms still require mechanism-specific requirements, bounded experiments, and evidence records.
