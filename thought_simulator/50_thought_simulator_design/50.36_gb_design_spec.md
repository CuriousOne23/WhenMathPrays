## Role in Development Flow
This document records key design decisions for the Governing Basin (GB) based on 20-series guidance, 40.36 exploratory evidence, and 30.36 verification results.

## 1. Design Decision: Adopt Minimal 3-Tier Hierarchy for Governing Basin

**Decision:**  
The Governing Basin SHALL be implemented as a **minimal 3-tier hierarchy**.

**Rationale:**
- A flat GB risks overload (HLR-20.010-099) and mixed-timescale confusion.
- The 40.36 prototype demonstrated the need for better separation of concerns.
- A minimal hierarchy preserves the core invariants of 20.10 and 20.16 (supervisory-only, non-mutating, deterministic).

**Chosen Structure:**

**Tier 1 — Signal Monitors** (stateless, fast, parallelizable)  
- DriftMonitor  
- OscillationMonitor  
- ContradictionMonitor  
- PopulationMonitor  

**Tier 2 — Supervisory Integrator** (stateful core of the Governing Basin)  
- Collects and correlates signals  
- Applies supervisory rules and priorities  
- Makes final action decisions  
- Tracks intervention frequency and history  

**Tier 3 — Boundary Enforcer** (ultra-thin adapter)  
- Applies approved supervisory actions through safe-boundary APIs only  
- Contains **zero** semantic logic  

## 2. Non-Goals (Design Boundaries)

The Governing Basin hierarchy **SHALL NOT**:

- Contain learned components or neural modules
- Perform semantic reasoning or meaning construction
- Modify TP/MTP semantic state
- Execute multi-step planning or recursive logic
- Maintain deep internal state beyond intervention history and frequency tracking
- Implement recursive supervisory loops
- Expand beyond the 3-tier structure without explicit approval in the 10-series

## 3. Impact on System Requirements (Positive & Negative)

**Positive Impacts:**
- Reduces risk of GB overload (directly addresses 20.10 risk)
- Improves verifiability (tiers can be tested independently)
- Better handles mixed timescales (fast vs slow signals)
- Enhances maintainability and future extensibility

**Potential Negative Impacts / Trade-offs:**
- Slight increase in internal complexity (must be carefully bounded)
- Minor added latency (negligible if implemented efficiently)
- Requires clear safe-boundary contracts (will be defined in 10-series)

**Mitigation:**  
Keep Tier 3 extremely thin and enforce strict contracts.

## 4. Why This Hierarchy Was Created
The flat prototype in 40.36 proved basic supervision works but also surfaced scalability and clarity issues. The hierarchy was introduced in the 50-series (design layer) to address these issues while staying faithful to the 20-series intent.
