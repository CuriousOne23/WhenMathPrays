## 1. Design Decision: Adopt Minimal 3-Tier Hierarchy for Governing Basin

**Decision:**  
The Governing Basin SHALL be implemented as a **minimal 3-tier hierarchy**.

**Rationale:**
- A flat GB risks overload (HLR-20.010-099) and mixed-timescale confusion.
- The 40.36 prototype demonstrated the need for better separation of concerns.
- A minimal hierarchy preserves the core invariants of 20.10 and 20.16 (supervisory-only, non-mutating, deterministic).

**Chosen Structure:**

**Tier 1 — Signal Monitors** (stateless, fast, parallelizable)  
- DriftMonitor  
- OscillationMonitor  
- ContradictionMonitor  
- PopulationMonitor  

**Tier 2 — Supervisory Integrator** (stateful core of the Governing Basin)  
- Collects and correlates signals  
- Applies supervisory rules and priorities  
- Makes final action decisions  
- Tracks intervention frequency and history  

**Tier 3 — Boundary Enforcer** (ultra-thin adapter)  
- Applies approved supervisory actions through safe-boundary APIs only  
- Contains **zero** semantic logic  

## 2. Non-Goals (Design Boundaries)

The Governing Basin hierarchy **SHALL NOT**:

- Contain learned components or neural modules
- Perform semantic reasoning or meaning construction
- Modify TP/MTP semantic state
- Execute multi-step planning or recursive logic
- Maintain deep internal state beyond intervention history and frequency tracking
- Implement recursive supervisory loops
- Expand beyond the 3-tier structure without explicit approval in the 10-series

## 3. Impact on System Requirements (Positive & Negative)

**Positive Impacts:**
- Reduces risk of GB overload (directly addresses 20.10 risk)

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document defines the **software specification** for the Governing Basin (GB) subsystem.

The Governing Basin is a dedicated **non-mutating supervisory subsystem** responsible for monitoring system stability, detecting anomalies, and applying safe supervisory actions.

**Scope:**  
Governing Basin (GB) 3-tier architecture (Signal Monitors → Supervisory Integrator → Boundary Enforcer).

**Out of Scope:**  
- Semantic meaning construction  
- Direct mutation of TP/MTP state  
- Production performance optimizations  
- Learned component oversight

## 3. Contracts and Invariants

**GB-001 (Invariant):** The Governing Basin SHALL NEVER mutate TP, MTP, or any semantic state directly.

**GB-002 (Invariant):** All GB decisions SHALL be deterministic given the same input snapshot and seed.

**GB-003 (Invariant):** The GB SHALL operate only through formally defined safe-boundary APIs (Tier 3).

**GB-004:** The GB SHALL be implemented as a minimal 3-tier hierarchy as defined in 50.36_gb_design_decisions.md.

---

## 4. Operation Flow and State Transitions

1. Tier 1 (Signal Monitors) → Produce SignalPackets from read-only snapshot.
2. Tier 2 (Supervisory Integrator) → Collect signals, resolve conflicts, decide action.
3. Tier 3 (Boundary Enforcer) → Apply chosen supervisory action via safe boundaries only.

---

## 5. Data and IO Contract

**Input:** Read-only global state snapshot.  
**Output:** SupervisoryDecision.  
**Internal Contract:** SignalPacket (defined in 50.36.10_gb_signal_packet_format.md).

---

## 6. Observability and Logging

- All SupervisoryDecisions SHALL be logged with full context.
- Intervention frequency SHALL be tracked and exposed.
- All actions SHALL include reason_code for auditability.

## 7. Verification Obligations

- Must demonstrate non-mutation of semantic state.
- Must maintain determinism and replayability.
